The Council Hall smelled of old parchment and the mineral scent of the Reach's stone ribs. Kael Ashworth sat in the auditor's chair, his father's signet ring cold against the knuckle of his right hand, and watched Lord Malrik Thorne ascend the central dais. Thorne moved with the deliberate grace of a man who had spent decades navigating the vertical politics of Thornmere. His house colors, a deep bruised violet and burnished copper, caught the light from the high apertures where sun-glass panels filtered the midday glare.

Behind him, the tiered seating curved upward in a horseshoe. Representatives from every lineage house occupied their traditional levels. House Vane sat three tiers below Ashworth, their section thinner than it had been a generation ago. Kael found Sol in the second row of the Vane delegation. She wore a practical coat of undyed wool, her hair pulled back in a severe knot, a notebook open on her knee. She was not watching Thorne. She was watching the other auditors, the senior ward-keepers, the Accord scribes. Cataloguing reactions. She looked up once, caught his eye, and gave him the smallest nod. *I see you. I am here.*

Thorne raised a hand. The murmur in the hall died.

"Honored council, sitting auditor, ward-contracted families of the Reach." Thorne's voice carried without amplification, pitched to the acoustics of the chamber. "Three generations ago, my grandfather stood in this hall and swore the Ward Oath. He bound his house to the protection of the lower wards, the ones that bleed heat into the Deep Vault's foundations. My father stood here and renewed that oath. I stood here and renewed it again. In return, the Accord promised that the cost of maintenance would be shared across all houses. That promise was broken."

A ripple moved through the tiers. Kael felt the shift in pressure behind his ears, the subtle hum of active wards responding to heightened emotion.

"The old ward trade failed," Thorne continued. "It failed the houses that paid in blood and coin. It failed the families who lost mothers and fathers to ward-sickness while the Accord looked away. It failed my house specifically. We have carried the heaviest contracts for sixty years. We have watched the ledgers go unbalanced. We have watched the Vault's infrastructure degrade while the Accord debated reform in committee after committee."

He let the silence stretch.

"A house that has already paid the cost has earned the right to control who pays it next."

The words landed like a dropped keystone. Kael saw the senior ward-keepers exchange glances. He saw the Accord scribes bend over their tablets, styli scratching. He saw Sol's pen stop moving.

"That is the principle behind the Reform Proposal of House Thorne, formally submitted this morning." Thorne unrolled a scroll, the wax seal catching the light. Violet and copper. A thorned rose wrapped around a key. "The proposal requests that the Accord transfer administrative authority over all exposed Vault-linked ward contracts to the house that assumes the reform mandate. The house that absorbs the failing contracts. The house that bears the risk of restructuring them. In practical terms, the house administering reform would hold ultimate authority over absorbed ward contracts. Not the Accord. Not a committee. The house that steps forward."

He held the scroll up. "I am that house. House Thorne steps forward. We request the sitting auditor's ruling on the legality of this transfer under Accord law. We request it today."

Thorne's gaze found Kael across the chamber. Direct. Unblinking. The challenge was formal, public, and impossible to ignore.

"The auditor will rule," Kael said. His voice carried. He stood, the chair scraping stone. "The proposal will be examined. The clause granting ultimate authority to the reform-administering house will be cross-referenced against the Accord's founding articles. The ward contracts in question will be audited in full. No decision will be made until the dossier is complete."

"Of course." Thorne inclined his head. "We would expect nothing less from Auditor Ashworth. Your father ruled on the original ward-trade settlements. The symmetry is... fitting."

The hall erupted. Kael raised a hand for order, but the damage was done. The proposal was public. The clause was recorded. Thorne had named him, and the symmetry Thorne invoked was a blade wrapped in silk.

***

Sol found him in the auditor's anteroom two hours later. The adjournment had been called to allow the senior ward-keepers time to review the submitted text. Kael stood at the long table, the Thorne scroll unrolled before him, a magnifier in his hand. He did not look up when the door clicked shut.

"You read the clause," he said.

"I read it." Sol approached the table. She set her notebook down beside the scroll. "Ultimate authority over absorbed ward contracts. Not oversight. Not consultation. Authority. That word has a specific meaning in Accord law. It means the power to modify, terminate, or transfer those contracts without further ratification."

"It does."

"Thorne knows that."

"He does."

"And you're going to rule on it."

"I am."

Sol's fingers brushed the edge of the scroll. The wax seal was intact. Violet and copper. A thorned rose wrapped around a key. She had seen that seal before. Three years ago, in the ward-archives beneath House Ashworth, when Kael had shown her the historical contracts Thorne had held. He had spread them on this same table. *This is what we're up against,* he had said. *Three generations of ward-trade debt. They've paid more than any house. They have the records to prove it.* She had traced the seal with her fingertip then, memorizing it. A habit. A precaution.

"The dossier," she said. "How long?"

"Four weeks at minimum. The contracts span sixty years. Cross-referencing alone will take—"

"I'll start on the cross-referencing tomorrow."

Kael lowered the magnifier. "Sol."

"I'm a ward-keeper. I have clearance. I know the archive system better than the junior scribes." She met his eyes. "You need someone who can read the old hand. The Thorne contracts from the first generation are coded. You know that."

"I know."

"Then we agree."

Kael was quiet for a long moment. The anteroom's single sun-glass panel threw a shaft of light across the table, illuminating dust motes dancing over the Thorne scroll. "I was going to tell you I'd handle the Thorne dossier alone," he said. "To keep you out of the line of fire. Thorne named me. He didn't name you. If I take point, the target stays on my back."

Sol's expression did not change. "And if you take point alone, you make the decisions alone. You decide what I need to know and when. You decide which risks I'm allowed to see. That's not protection, Kael. That's the same pattern with a different justification."

The air between them thickened. Kael set the magnifier down. His jaw tightened.

"I tell you after," he said. "Every time. I don't lie. I don't conceal. I act, then I tell you. That's not the same."

"It's adjacent." Sol's voice was level. "The effect is the same. I find out after the decision is made. My input doesn't exist because the decision already happened. You're not asking me to trust your judgment. You're asking me to accept that my judgment doesn't factor in."

"That's not what I'm—"

"Then what is it?" She stepped closer. The notebook between them was a barrier she had not meant to create. "Because right now, today, you were going to tell me you'd handle Thorne alone. You were going to frame it as safety. And I would have let you, because I know you mean it. But meaning it doesn't make it right."

Kael exhaled. The sound was rough. "I don't want you hurt. The ward contracts Thorne holds... the cost-transfer mechanic is still active. If something triggers during the audit, if a ward契约 flares—"

"It flares for you too. We know how it works. We've lived it." Her hand settled on the table, palm up. An offering. A challenge. "I'm not asking you to tell me everything. I'm asking you to stop deciding what I don't need to know."

The words hung in the sunlit dust.

Kael looked at her hand. At the calluses from climbing rope and ward-tools. At the faint scar on her wrist from the first time she'd taken a ward-hit for him, back when they were rivals and the mechanic had nearly killed them both. He looked at her face, the set of her jaw, the fierce clarity in eyes the color of storm-water.

"All right," he said. "Not alone. Together. The dossier, the cross-referencing, the ruling. Together."

Sol's fingers closed on empty air, then withdrew. "Good. Then we start tomorrow. The archive opens at sixth bell."

"Sixth bell."

She gathered her notebook. Paused at the door. "One more thing. The Thorne seal. On the proposal. It's the same seal on the first-generation contracts you showed me in the archive. The thorned rose and key. That's their house seal for ward-trade documents specifically. Different from their general correspondence seal."

Kael frowned. "You recognized it."

"I memorized it. When you showed me the contracts. I thought... if Thorne ever moved on the Vault, their seal would be on the paperwork. I wanted to know it on sight."

He studied her. The anteroom felt smaller. The light had shifted, the sun-glass panel throwing a longer shadow across the floor.

"You've been waiting for this," he said. Not a question.

"I've been preparing for it. There's a difference."

The door clicked shut behind her.

Kael stood alone in the anteroom, the Thorne scroll spread before him, the violet-copper seal catching the last of the afternoon light. He traced the thorned rose with his fingertip. Three generations of debt. Three generations of pain. Thorne had paid the cost. Thorne believed that payment earned them control.

He picked up his stylus. Opened a fresh ledger page. Wrote the date: 13 Sunspire, Year 3.

The first entry in the Thorne dossier.

He had told Sol they would do this together. He meant it. But as the stylus moved across the page, recording the clause, the seal, the challenge, a quiet voice in the back of his mind whispered that he had already decided the first audit would be solo. A site visit to the old Thorne ward-anchor in the Lower Reach. A preliminary assessment. Just to make sure it was safe before he brought her.

He wrote the note in the margin: *Site visit scheduled. 15 Sunspire. Solo.*

The stylus paused.

He did not cross it out.
