The Council Hall smelled of old parchment and the mineral scent of the Reach's stone ribs. Kael Ashworth sat in the auditor's chair, his father's signet ring cold against the knuckle of his right hand, and watched Lord Malrik Thorne ascend the central dais. Thorne moved with the deliberate grace of a man who had spent decades navigating the vertical politics of Thornmere. His house colors, a deep bruised violet and burnished copper, caught the light from the high apertures where sun-glass panels filtered the midday glare.

Behind him, the tiered seating curved upward in a horseshoe. Representatives from every lineage house occupied their traditional levels. House Vane sat three tiers below Ashworth, their section thinner than it had been a generation ago. Kael found Sol in the second row of the Vane delegation, a practical coat of undyed wool, her hair pulled back, a notebook open on her knee. She was not watching Thorne. She was watching the senior ward-keepers and the Accord scribes, cataloguing reactions. She looked up once, caught his eye, and gave him the smallest nod.

Thorne raised a hand. The murmur in the hall died.

"Honored council, sitting auditor, ward-contracted families of the Reach." Thorne's voice carried without amplification, pitched to the acoustics of the chamber. "Three generations ago, my grandfather stood in this hall and swore the Ward Oath. My father renewed it. I renewed it again. In return, the Accord promised the cost of maintenance would be shared across all houses. That promise was broken. My house has carried the heaviest contracts for sixty years. We have watched the Vault's infrastructure degrade while the Accord debates reform in committee after committee."

He let the silence stretch.

"A house that has already paid the cost has earned the right to control who pays it next."

The words landed like a dropped keystone. Kael saw the senior ward-keepers exchange glances. He saw the Accord scribes bend over their tablets, styli scratching. He saw Sol's pen go still.

"That is the principle behind the Reform Proposal of House Thorne, submitted this morning." Thorne unrolled a scroll, the wax seal catching the light: violet and copper, a thorned rose wrapped around a key. "The proposal requests that the Accord transfer administrative authority over exposed Vault-linked ward contracts to the house that assumes the reform mandate. In practical terms, the house administering reform would hold ultimate authority over the absorbed contracts. Not the Accord. Not a committee. The house that steps forward." He held the scroll up. "House Thorne steps forward. We request the sitting auditor's ruling on the legality of this transfer under Accord law."

Thorne's gaze found Kael across the chamber. Direct, unblinking. The challenge was formal, public, and impossible to ignore.

"The auditor will rule," Kael said, standing, the chair scraping stone. "The proposal will be examined. The clause granting ultimate authority to the reform-administering house will be cross-referenced against the Accord's founding articles. The contracts in question will be audited in full. No decision until the dossier is complete."

"Of course." Thorne inclined his head. "We would expect nothing less from Auditor Ashworth. Your father ruled on the original ward-trade settlements. The symmetry is fitting."

The hall erupted into murmur. Kael raised a hand for order, but the proposal was already public, the clause already recorded, and Thorne had named him in front of every lineage house in the Reach.

***

Sol found him in the auditor's anteroom two hours later, during the adjournment called for the senior ward-keepers to review the submitted text. Kael stood at the long table, the Thorne scroll unrolled, a magnifier in hand.

"Ultimate authority," she said, setting her notebook beside the scroll. "Not oversight. Not consultation. That word has a specific meaning in Accord law. It means the power to modify, terminate, or transfer contracts without further ratification."

"It does."

"Thorne knows that."

"He does."

Sol's fingers brushed the seal, violet and copper, the thorned rose wrapped around a key. "I've seen this before. Three years ago, in the ward-archives, when you showed me the historical Thorne contracts. You spread them on this table. I traced the seal then. I wanted to know it on sight, if Thorne ever moved on the Vault."

"You remembered it exactly."

"I memorize things I think I'll need again." She looked up. "The dossier. How long?"

"Four weeks, at minimum. Sixty years of contracts to cross-reference, and the first-generation Thorne documents are coded. I'll need someone who reads the old hand."

"I read the old hand." It was not a request.

"I know." Kael set the magnifier down. "I want you on it. I'll bring you the archive assignments tomorrow."

"Tomorrow. Sixth bell." Sol gathered her notebook, then paused at the door. "Kael. Thorne named you specifically today, in front of the whole council. That's a target on your back that wasn't there yesterday. Whatever the audit turns into, I want to know the shape of it before it's decided, not after."

"You will." He meant it, and something in his voice must have carried the meaning, because she only nodded once and left.

Kael stood alone with the scroll a moment longer, then returned to his desk and the audit schedule already spread across it, eleven Thorne-linked sites now folded into the dossier alongside the eleven he had inherited from the Lower Spine survey. He worked through the afternoon, assigning archive research to Sol's name in the research column without asking her first, because she had already told him where she wanted to be, and because writing her name down felt, for once, like keeping a promise rather than making a decision for her.

Near dusk, reviewing the site list a final time, he found the old Thorne ward-anchor at Kestrel's Hollow, unvisited by any auditor in eleven years, flagged only as *stable, low priority* in a survey he did not entirely trust. It sat two hours' climb from the Spire, isolated, its access route unmapped since the last generation of Thorne wardens had let the trail overgrow.

He thought of sending word to Sol before he went. He thought of it the way a man thinks of a door he has already decided not to open, the thought arriving and passing without quite becoming an action. The Hollow was low priority. A single visit, a structural check, nothing that required two people or advance notice. He would tell her after, the way he always told her after, and the telling would be true, and the audit would move forward, and nothing about the arrangement would look, from the outside, like anything other than an auditor doing his job efficiently.

He wrote the note in his own schedule, not in the shared dossier: *Kestrel's Hollow. 15 Sunspire. Solo, low priority, structural only.*

He did not show her the note. It did not occur to him, writing it, that there was anything to show.
