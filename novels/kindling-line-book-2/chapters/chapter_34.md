Kael did not go up after her.

He told himself, standing at the mouth of the new stair with the water climbing past his boots, that giving her the distance she'd asked for was itself a kind of listening, and he almost believed it. Then he heard how that sounded, the same reasoning dressed in new clothes, and he made himself stop pretending the two things were different.

He climbed anyway, an hour later, once the water had crept high enough that staying became its own kind of stubbornness. Corren was gone from the top step. Joren stood there instead, arms crossed, saying nothing, which was its own report.

"She went toward the infirmary," Joren said finally, when Kael's silence had stretched too long to be comfortable for either of them. "I didn't follow. Didn't think you'd want me to say I had."

"You did right."

"I didn't do anything. That's the part I'm not sure was right."

Kael left him with the stair and the rising water and walked the long way to the infirmary, not because it was shorter, but because he needed the length of it to decide what he meant to say when he arrived, and did not manage to decide anything at all.

Mara had not changed. The healer said it without being asked, the way she said it every day now, a kindness disguised as routine. Kael sat in the chair beside the bed anyway and took the cold hand from the blanket and held it, because Sol had taught him that much without ever once telling him to do it.

"I did the thing again," he said. "The one I promised I'd stopped doing. Not the same shape as before. I didn't hide anything. I told Joren where I was going before I went. I just didn't ask her first, because some old, ugly part of me still believes that if I get to the danger before she does, I've spent something I'm allowed to spend and she hasn't lost anything I'm not allowed to lose."

Mara's face did not change. It never did.

"She named it," he said. "Not just my part of it. Hers too. Said she'd wanted to hold Tam back the same way, silently, and hadn't, and that not acting on it didn't mean the wanting wasn't real. I don't know what to do with that. I built the whole shape of my apology around being the only one who does this, and she just took that away from me, and I don't know if I'm supposed to feel less alone in it or more afraid, because if it isn't only mine to fix, I don't know whose it is."

He sat with that a while. The healer moved paper somewhere behind him. The window's light shifted, thin and coppery, the particular color Cinderveil's haze always put on a room at this hour.

"I told her I loved her once," he said, quieter. "Weeks ago now. On Tar Lane, at the forge, with Renn's chalk still on the wall behind us. I've never said it since, not because it isn't true every day, but because I'm afraid of what it costs when I only reach for it in the moments I've done something I can't otherwise defend. I don't want it to be an apology. I want it to just be true, sitting there, not doing any work at all."

Mara's hand stayed still and cold in his. He set it back on the blanket, gently, and left before the healer could ask him anything he didn't have an answer for.

***

He found Sol two hours later, not at the house, not at the awning, but on the narrow overlook above Tar Lane where the forge's heat rose warm even through the stone, the same overlook where he had said the words he'd just confessed to Mara he was afraid to repeat.

She did not turn when he arrived. She had heard his stick on the gravel long before he reached her.

"I went to Mara," he said. "I told her what I did. I don't know why it's easier to say it to someone who can't answer."

"Maybe because she can't tell you it's not enough."

"Maybe." He came to stand beside her, not touching, keeping the distance she had asked for without her having to ask for it twice. "I'm not going to tell you I've solved it. I haven't. I climbed down that stair alone this morning for the same reason I've climbed down a dozen stairs alone before you ever asked me to stop, and I don't have a clean account of why today was different from yesterday, when yesterday I asked first and today I didn't."

"I don't need a clean account. I need to know you're not going to hand me one and call it finished."

"I'm not handing you anything. I'm telling you I don't know if I can promise the shape of me is done shifting back into that habit when I'm frightened enough. I want to promise it. Wanting isn't the same as being able to."

Sol was quiet a long moment, her eyes on the forge smoke rising below them, thin and grey against the copper haze.

"I don't have a clean account either," she said finally. "I know what I wanted to do with Tam, and I know I didn't do it, and I don't know if that was strength or only luck, because the chance to do it never quite closed all the way and I never had to find out what I'd choose if it had."

"Then we're both standing on the same unfinished ground."

"That's not comfort, Kael."

"I'm not offering comfort. I don't have any to give that would be honest." He looked at her, finally, and did not reach for her hand, though the wanting to was plain enough on his face that she could have read it without him saying it aloud. "I only have this. I heard you at the stair. I've been sitting with it since, and I don't have a better version of myself to offer you yet. Only the truth that I heard it, and it hasn't left me since."

The forge below them rang once, iron against iron, and neither of them spoke for a while after, the silence holding something that was not yet repair and was not yet its opposite either, only the long, uncomfortable middle of a thing neither of them had finished saying.

"Renn's commission sits in two days," Sol said eventually, because the silence had gone on long enough that naming something else felt like its own kind of mercy. "I still have his second peg to bring, and yours."

"I'll have mine ready." Kael's voice had gone careful again, grateful for the smaller, solvable thing. "The stair under the boiling house is still rising. Joren's posted it closed until the water settles. Whatever's past it will still be past it in two days."

"Or it won't be, and we'll have lost whatever it was."

"Then we'll have lost it honestly, together, deciding to wait rather than one of us deciding alone to go and look."

Sol almost smiled at that, the ghost of one, gone before it fully arrived. "That's the first thing you've said all day that sounded like you actually believed it."

"I'm trying to believe more of them." Kael looked back down at the forge smoke, the copper light, the Reach's long fall beneath them. "I don't know yet if trying is the same as changing. I only know that stopping trying isn't a thing I'm willing to do, whatever it costs to keep failing at it in front of you instead of in private, where it would be easier and worth less."

Sol did not answer that directly. She stood beside him a while longer, the distance between them neither closed nor widened, and when she finally turned to go, she did not tell him whether the ground between them had shifted or only held its place, and he did not ask her to say more than she had.
