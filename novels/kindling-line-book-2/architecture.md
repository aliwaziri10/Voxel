## Direct Continuity From Book 1's Ending
Book 1 ends with Sol and Kael discovering a "Contract of Perpetual
Surety" naming a "Deep Vault" and a "Keeper of the Burning Ledger" above
House Corrin — Corrin was a broker, not the true power. The Keeper is
established as a POSITION, not a named person. That framing is LOCKED and
must not be contradicted anywhere in this book, including in the final
line (see Ending section below).

## Antagonist: House Thorne
Thorne's founding bloodline served as wards for three generations before
Thorne rose to house status — meaning Thorne personally absorbed
cost-transfer years under the old, unreformed ward trade, the same system
that failed Sol's mother. Thorne's public reform platform is not cynical
on its face; the house has a genuine, personal claim to demanding change.
The twist: Thorne wants to replace the old trade not with reform but with
itself as the new Vault-adjacent power, on the internal logic that "a
house that has already paid the cost has earned the right to control who
pays it next." This line is strong enough to be a stated or closely
paraphrased line of Thorne's own dialogue somewhere in the book, not just
architecture notes — write it as a spoken moment.

Thorne's plot must hook BOTH leads independently:
- Hooks Sol: a ward-descended house reacting to the exact system that
  broke her family.
- Hooks Kael: Thorne petitions HIM specifically, as sitting auditor, to
  legitimize its claim — mirroring his father Valerius's role in Book 1
  without repeating the same mechanic (Kael is asked to rule on a house's
  fate again, but the choice and its cost are new).

## Kael's Failure Mode: Overcorrection, Not Concealment
Newly assigned lead auditor for the ward-trade dismantlement, Kael starts
making unilateral protective calls — solo audits, keeping Sol out of
danger — without lying to her. He tells her what he did, after the fact.
The rupture reads as "you stopped treating me as your equal," never "you
lied to me again." IMPORTANT (structural requirement, not a style note):
Sol must show visible awareness or pushback on this pattern well before
it is formally confronted at the rupture — see the ch.16-18 beat below.
Without it, twelve silent chapters of Kael acting alone will read as
concealment to the reader regardless of his stated intent, collapsing the
distinction this book depends on.

## Sol's Arc
Political standing after the Reckoning. Central tension: whether to
become the first voluntary "reformed" ward-taker under a proposed new
contract standard, using the very mechanism that destroyed her mother, to
prove it survivable. This is explicitly HER choice and HER risk — not
decided for her by Kael, the deliberate contrast to his arc. State
explicitly on the page that this voluntary ward-taking does NOT alter the
Sol/Kael involuntary cost-transfer mechanic from Book 1 — that mechanic
stays uncontrollable and non-redirectable between them, unchanged. Sol
choosing to become a sanctioned ward for OTHERS is a different system
(the wards, per Book 1's own distinction) and must not blur into the
Sol/Kael mechanic.

## Set Pieces (eight total)

1. Ch.~5-8 — Thorne's public reform proposal. Kael is named lead auditor
   and begins acting unilaterally — a first small instance, not yet named
   as a problem.

2. Ch.~13-15 — Infiltrating a disused Vault-linked ward site. Real
   physical danger, not a conversation scene. The Ward Deed of Succession
   (see ch.20 below) is DISCOVERED here, though not yet understood — this
   set piece now has direct payoff at ch.20, strengthening it.

3. Ch.~16-18 — SOL-AWARENESS BEAT (new, required). Kael tells Sol he
   spent the day on Accord paperwork. She finds a Thorne-sealed entry
   pass in his coat, dated the same day, to a site he never mentioned.
   NOTE: establish earlier in the book (ch.5-8 or ch.13-15) that Sol has
   already seen or been shown what a Thorne seal looks like, so this
   recognition reads as earned, not convenient. She confronts him
   directly: "I'm not asking you to tell me everything. I'm asking you to
   stop deciding what I don't need to know." He admits it, uncomfortably
   — no denial, no explaining away. The pattern is named eight chapters
   before it's forced into the open publicly at ch.20.

4. Ch.~20 — The Ward Deed of Succession, found at ch.13-15, is fully
   understood: a physical ledger page, sealed in Thorne's own house wax,
   listing every ward contract Thorne proposes to absorb under its reform
   plan, with a clause transferring ultimate authority over those
   contracts to "the house administering reform" rather than the Accord
   itself. Kael cross-references it against Accord law and realizes the
   clause is legal seizure dressed as reform. He tells Sol immediately
   after — no concealment, but this confirms the pattern already named at
   ch.16-18.

5. Ch.~26-28 — Kael's solo handling backfires: alone without backup at a
   Thorne site, he is injured, or a ward-contracted ally is hurt as a
   direct result. Cost lands on the page. Sol and Kael, already an
   established couple since Book 1, share their first "I love you" here,
   or it is interrupted by the fallout — NOT a first kiss (continuity
   fix: they are already together going into this book).

6. Ch.~33-35 — RUPTURE. Sol confronts Kael that deciding for her, even
   honestly and without lying, is still shutting her out as an equal.
   Real, unresolved cost. NOT resolved by ch.35.

7. Ch.~36-39 — REPAIR. Kael publicly and formally cedes unilateral
   authority, making Sol co-equal lead on the audit, at real cost to his
   standing with House Ashworth. This book's FIRST on-page intimate scene
   comes after this repair (scene count starts fresh for Book 2, not
   continued from Book 1's tally).

8. Ch.~40-43 — Climax: public Accord ceremony. Thorne's takeover bid,
   built on the Ward Deed of Succession, is exposed and defeated. Sol
   voluntarily undergoes the first "reformed" ward-taker trial — real
   risk, since the involuntary mechanic still applies in full elsewhere;
   her earned control is incomplete, same logic as Book 1's climax, not
   suddenly solved.

9. Ch.~44-45 — Short aftermath. Sol and Kael together. Final line
   reveals the Keeper's seat sits inside the Reach's own founding
   government — write this so it reads as an institutional, structural
   fact (a SEAT, an OFFICE within the government), not as the unmasking
   of a specific person. Do not name or characterize an individual here;
   the Keeper remains a position, consistent with Book 1's locked
   framing, preserved for Book 3.

## Ending Shape
Resolve Thorne fully within this book. Only the Keeper's institutional
existence stays open for Book 3. This repeats Book 1's "villain defeated
+ Happy For Now" shape a second time — accepted deliberately, since it's
only the second occurrence. Book 3 must NOT repeat this shape a third
time (locked direction, see below).

## Book 3 Direction (locked now so Book 2's final line can be written
knowing what it sets up)
Book 3 ends with (a) a permanent HEA for Sol and Kael, not another HFN,
and (b) the Keeper thread resolved through negotiation and exposure —
forced into public accountability under the Accord — rather than
defeated or destroyed, consistent with the Keeper being institutional,
not personal. This keeps the trilogy's three endings structurally
distinct rather than repeating the same shape three times.
