# DIGEST - Kindling Line Book 2, ch.20-24

Fact lookup written 2026-10-03 from the live chapter files (main). Not a summary and not new canon. If this file and a chapter disagree, the chapter wins; fix this file.

## KEY CANON FOUND IN THIS BATCH (use these)
- THE DEED IS OPEN AND ITS CLOCK IS RUNNING. "Ward Deed of Succession": vellum wrapper tied with cord of violet and copper, eleven leaves of the instrument, six leaves of a Schedule (eleven houses, by line). Opened by the Council clerks before the full Council at ninth bell on 28 Sunspire (ch.20). Clause 7 moves all named contracts, and all that follow them, into the custody of the house administering reform. Clause 12 makes that house's reading final, no body in review. Clause 19 sets the clock: thirty days from the opening, set aside only by nine of the twelve houses, otherwise ratified. The clock ends 28 Cinderveil. The Council vote is at the public sitting in the last week of Cinderveil. These dates imply Sunspire has 30 days.
- COUNCIL ARITHMETIC: twelve houses. Eight signed the petition; four did not (Vane and Ashworth are two; the other two are not named). Setting aside needs nine, so five signing houses must turn. By 29 Sunspire two signing houses had asked the clerks for their own line of the Schedule, not the clauses (ch.21).
- THE REFORM IS THE SEIZURE (ch.21). The last line of the Schedule reaches every contract not yet written. Thorne withdrew the petition's second head (the replacement contract standard) at noon on 28 Sunspire, so nobody can hold it up beside that line.
- THE CHANCELLOR is a woman: "Her voice was thin and carried everywhere" (ch.20).
- THE AWNING (ch.22 on): violet awning at the foot of the Lower Spine stair; two clerks, a brazier (a second brazier by 2 Cinderveil), a rack of tin cups, tea. "Provisional engagement" for ward-takers with no line since the Accord sealed; wage from 1 Cinderveil paid by the house; lamps kept. Ansa's figure: about four hundred such ward-takers. Signature counts (Ansa's slate, counted from her window at lamp-lighting): 31 on 29 Sunspire, 69 "in two days" (by the evening of the 30th), 143 by the evening of 1 Cinderveil.
- THE FALSE SENTENCE: clerks tell each signer "The Auditor has read the standard and raised nothing." Kael never said it and has not been shown the standard. After he spoke at the table on 30 Sunspire the clerk's line became "the Auditor has been asked".
- KAEL'S WRITTEN STATEMENT (ch.23, third copy): "I, Kael Ashworth, Auditor of the Accord, state that I have been shown the form of provisional engagement and have not been shown the standard it refers to. I have not read it. I have raised nothing because I was given nothing to raise. I certify no engagement under the Accord's article on transfer. Let no signer take my silence for a reading." Added last line: "This statement is made against no one who has signed." Witnessed by Sol, signed "Isolde Vane". Plan: Ansa reads it at the splicers' hall at the noon break, Mara reads it on the ropewalk floor, copy to the Chancellor's clerk under Kael's wax.
- THE WITHDRAWAL FORM exists (ch.24) and traps the signer: see ch.24.
- SOL'S HANDS, day by day: 28th leaf-turning takes two tries; 29th fingers bend to the second knuckle, she turns leaves one at a time; 30th she closes both fists in three breaths (a day early) and writes with the pen in her whole fist; 1 Cinderveil healer takes the linen off, fingers open to a count of five and no wider, can close on a pen but not a rope, "nothing heavier than a cup for four days"; 2 Cinderveil grey wool gloves lined with the healer's linen, fingers go in to the second joint, coat buttons take the better part of a minute.
- BELL NOTE: morning bells run at least to ninth (ch.20), "fourth bell of the morning" (ch.23), "second bell of the afternoon" (ch.22). Do not state a bell as a clock hour in Book 3 without checking.

## CH.20 | 28 Sunspire | POV Kael
- WHO: Renn (hand flat on a brass case no longer than a forearm); the Chancellor; senior clerk (bald, grey, ink to the wrist); junior clerk (cuts the wax; named Bram in ch.21); Malrik Thorne (deep grey, plain silver clasp) on Aldous's arm; Aldous (pale); the works steward (copper clasp, takes her place behind the Thorne bench); Sol (hands wrapped); Kael. Eight signing houses on the tiers and the four who did not sign.
- WHAT: Before the box is opened Renn tells Kael: at eighth bell Malrik brought "it" (the brass case, Thorne wax closing both ends, Renn's commission strip across the seam) up the Reach on foot; Renn signed for it before the Chancellor's clerk, has not opened it, and "may not read it until the Council says". The copper link is still on the plate. The note on the link has been with the Chancellor's clerk since noon on the 27th under Renn's seal. Renn and Joren walked the sublevel twice more: no door, no drain, nothing off the survey; "So the survey is wrong." The Chancellor first has House Thorne's filing of the evening of the 27th read: Thorne received the auditor's reply, does not withdraw the offer of the shutting order, and the order in the master smith's hand was lodged that morning with the Council surveyor under seal "to be read by him alone until the Council decided otherwise". Sol: "Why would he give up the page?" Kael: "Because he has found a better thing to hold." The box comes in on two clerks' arms from the strongroom: lead-lined oak, bound in iron, one band sprung. Seals checked by the senior clerk: the Council surveyor's strip (cut words "Twenty-three Sunspire. First bell. Unbroken.") whole; the Thorne wax (three strands in a knot, a drop, a feather and a flame at each terminus, near-black, violet and copper) whole, with two later layers laid over it and not through it. Junior clerk cuts Renn's strip with a bone knife, then the Thorne wax. Kael and Sol read the clauses; Kael says it is a seizure. Clause 19 depends on "its own seal being whole", which is the safeguard in the last article of the Accord's dissolution schedule for old succession instruments. Sol: "Four houses on this Schedule did not sign the petition. Ours is one. Yours is another. Nobody asked either of us." Kael declines to certify, asks leave to read every leaf with Isolde Vane under a clerk's eye and give a written finding at the public sitting, and asks the record to show the Schedule observation was hers. Leave granted. Aldous looks at the Lower Spine leaf, goes white, and begins "I did not."; the steward's fingers settle on his sleeve and the sentence ends.
- OBJECTS AND COUNTS: Deed: 11 leaves of the instrument + 6 of Schedule; wrapper lettered Ward Deed of Succession. Schedule: eleven houses by line; under the Lower Spine "Thorne and Vane" side by side, ruled off and added into one sum. Kael sets Renn's chain sums (nine by mid Cinderveil, eight by the end) beside the clause-19 clock: "Both ran out in the same week."
- LINES AND BEATS: Malrik: "The house is content to be read, Chancellor." / "The box could hardly have been in better hands." Kael: "It has been in the Council's hands, my lord." Malrik: "Yes. That was the hope." Kael to Sol: "It's a seizure. Seven takes the contracts, twelve takes the last word, and nineteen makes them live." Sol: "It's built to be easier to keep than to stop." Sol turns a leaf with thumb and forefinger, two tries.
- CONTINUITY SNAG (soft): Renn says he "may not read" the brass case until the Council says; the filing says the order is "to be read by him alone". The text never names the brass case as the shutting order; it is inferred from the seals and the day. Schedule lists eleven houses, yet four non-signers are said to be on it and there are twelve houses and eight signers.
- OPEN: Who drew the survey wrong; what Aldous did not do (the text implies his name is on the Lower Spine leaf but not in what role); contents of the brass case.

## CH.21 | 29 Sunspire | POV Sol
- WHO: Sol; Kael; Bram (junior clerk, told to watch, stands against the wall, hands folded); a Council porter (tray: heel of bread, jug of cold tea); Renn (mud to the knee, the tally board).
- WHAT: Reading room behind the Chancellor's dais: one window, one table, one clerk. Eleven leaves under clear glass with brass rims, beside Renn's fair copy stamped by the clerks with the hour and date. Kael has headed a sheet "Finding of the Auditor on the Instrument Opened 28 Sunspire" and left it empty; he moves the pen to the far side of the table. The recital lists thirty-three dead of the trade in a column by line and year; at the twenty-first is a girl of fifteen whose name Sol read in a Thorne ledger in red-brown ink four days ago. No Vane is in the column, but the Vane block sits in the Schedule added into Thorne's sum. Sol: "Their dead pay for it. Ours are in the total." Kael: "That goes in the finding." On the last leaf of the Schedule, under the totals, the final line: "And all that shall follow them upon these lines, in whatever form the house administering reform shall set." Kael first calls it the common close; Sol works through it: it takes contracts nobody has written yet, so every future contract on the Reach is Thorne's before the ink is dry. Kael: "It's the reform itself that's the seizure." Sol: "He asked to be read. I took it for a plea." Kael: "It was an instruction." Bram reports two signing houses sent yesterday afternoon for copies of the Schedule, each asking for its own line only; and that the petition's second head was withdrawn yesterday at noon by a Thorne runner under the house's seal, within the petitioner's right. Kael: "Three hours after the box was opened." Sol: "After that they only needed the lines." Sol and Kael agree to ask at the Thorne house together before noon. Sol's unspoken thought: a person would have to be the first to sign a new standard and stand in front of the Reach to prove it could be survived; the word she does not say to him is "first".
- OBJECTS AND COUNTS: 33 names in the recital. Renn: cycle "Ten", steady on the landing at midnight and at dawn; chain down an ounce and a half in six days; copper link on the plate beside it, counted apart; "My scale is good to a quarter."
- LINES AND BEATS: Sol: "It's true. That's what I can't forgive." (Kael: "It's true. All of it." Sol: "I know it is. That's what I can't forgive.") Kael: "I read that leaf yesterday in front of the whole Council and took it for a formula." (ch.20 shows him reading only the Lower Spine heads aloud; the last line is not shown being read.) Kael and Sol stand ready to go together; Kael leaves the empty sheet untouched.
- OPEN: Where the withdrawn second head (the replacement standard) is kept; Sol's word "first".

## CH.22 | 30 Sunspire | POV Kael
- WHO: Ansa (climbs the spire stair before first bell, forty years at rope, tar-shop smell in her shawl); Sol; Kael. In Kael's recounting of noon on the 29th: two retainers (bowed to Sol, not to him), the steward (came down four steps, took the joint note unread, copper clasp bright), Aldous (hands hanging, watched the note go into the steward's sleeve). Tam (Ansa's sister's boy; signed at the second bell of the afternoon on the 29th).
- WHAT: The joint note (six plain lines) asked where the second head's standard could be read; the steward answered that it was withdrawn under the Council's own rules and the house had nothing further to file. Sol notes Aldous watched the paper, not them, and Kael had left that out. Ansa brings a printed engagement form from the new awning, which went up while they stood at the Thorne door. The form: "Provisional Engagement of a Ward-taker under the Standard of the House Administering Reform"; wage and lamps; term wording identical to Sol's mother's contract ("Until dissolution of the trade" ... "or death of the bearer"); a line "Dependents, name and age" (Tam wrote MOTHER. SISTER, 14.); the house knot in near-black wax, no larger than a coin; a witness place; and a last line "Consent of the holder, given freely, upon the standard as set." Kael sees the method: every signature lawful, the Deed only has to be ratified over the top. Kael sums it: at even half Tam's day-one pace the house holds the Reach's trade before the Council sits. Sol closes both hands to fists (three breaths) and writes with the pen in her whole fist on the back of Ansa's paper: "Received of Ansa, one form. 30 Sunspire, first bell." and "I.V."; Kael signs beneath. Kael confesses he thought of going to the awning alone in a plain coat to read the standard and sign nothing, and says he will not. Sol: "Thank you for the notice." Plan: fourth bell, together, Kael in his coat, asks aloud for the standard in full. Kael's request (Sol had him cut "respectfully"): "The Auditor of the Accord requires a copy of the standard in full, under the Accord's article on transfer, and will read it at the table." Renn's sealed note came back from the Chancellor's clerk at midnight, wax whole, one line under it: received, and the survey books of the Reach are being fetched. Ansa's last news: the clerk says to every signer "The Auditor has read the standard and raised nothing." Kael: "I have never seen it. Before this morning I did not know a copy had left the house." Tam says the thirty-one heard it and it is why the first ones signed.
- OBJECTS AND COUNTS: 31 names before lamp-lighting on the 29th; about 400 ward-takers with no line; 28 days from this morning to 28 Cinderveil.
- LINES AND BEATS: Ansa: "They gave you nothing because they'd already given it to us." Kael on the form: "No one will ever be able to say the house forced a single one."
- CONTINUITY NOTE: "a copy" in Kael's line is unclear (the form, or the standard, which is not yet set). Ansa's window overlooks the awning (see Book 2 ch.12 for her room over the tar-shop).
- OPEN: How the clerks came to use the Auditor's name; whether Kael can take back what he never said.

## CH.23 | 1 Cinderveil | POV Sol
- WHO: Sol; Kael; the healer (takes the linen off at first bell); Ansa's runner boy (slate at dawn); Tam (arrives at the end: under twenty, splicer's apron, tar to the wrists, cap in one fist, the folded leaf sealed in whole near-black wax; the curve of Ansa's jaw).
- WHAT: Healer lifts the last linen; hands pink and shiny, welts flat and white heel to fingertip. Kael has written three copies of a statement; Sol makes him read the third (text in KEY CANON). Sol witnesses, signs "Isolde Vane" in letters the size a child makes; Kael waits for her nod and sands her name himself. Flashback to the 30th at the fourth bell: the line forty long at the foot of the Lower Spine stair parted for them. The younger clerk (ink on his cuff, pleasant voice) bowed to Sol first, said the standard is set upon ratification and cannot be laid before the Auditor, entered the request in the table's book with the hour, and poured Kael a cup of tea. When a broad woman with a boy of six took the pen and the clerk began "The Auditor has read the", Kael said: "I have been shown the form. I have not been shown the standard. I certify nothing, and my silence is not a reading." The clerk: "The house has never said he certified. The clerk will say instead that the Auditor has been asked." Sol: the house "lost a sentence and bought a better one for the same price". Nine people left the line; the rest stayed because the wage begins 1 Cinderveil and the rents fall due the same morning. Sol: "I would have signed." Kael: "I know. I'm not writing this against them." Ansa's slate: sixty-nine names in two days, nine of yesterday's queue not among them; Sol: "Sixty-nine. Of four hundred." Renn sent word at dawn: three of the nine survey books of the Reach have been brought up; he will tell them nothing until he has read all three. Tam: fourth to sign, signed because the clerk said Kael had read it; first wage paid at the awning at dawn; "I want to know if a man can take his name back." Kael realises the article on transfer says how a contract changes hands and nothing about handing one back.
- OBJECTS AND COUNTS: Line forty long at the fourth bell on the 30th; 9 left the line; 69 names by the evening of the 30th; Tam is fourth signer. Survey books: three of nine brought.
- LINES AND BEATS: Healer: "Grease at night, gloves in the wet, nothing heavier than a cup for four days." Healer: "If I hear it was a knife, I'll know." Sol: the paper "has to be true, and it has to be first." Sol still holds the word she has not said aloud.
- OPEN: Whether Mara's reading at the ropewalk lands (not shown); Tam's question.

## CH.24 | 2 Cinderveil | POV Kael
- WHO: Kael; Sol; Tam (flashback to the landing on the 1st); the younger clerk at the awning (ink on his cuff).
- WHAT: On the landing Kael answered Tam "I do not know." and stopped himself saying "Come down with me now", seeing it would decide for a man already decided for by paper. Tam: his rent is paid at the door; he asked at dawn when the standard would be put out and the clerk said upon ratification and wrote him in the book; two had asked before him and were written in too ("Everyone was very kind."). Kael promises to read the article, form and dissolution schedule again and send word by Ansa; if no, he says no; Tam keeps the wage whatever he chooses. Kael reads the article at lamp-lighting and at midnight: it says nothing about getting out. At first bell on the 2nd he decides: the clerk's sentence of the 29th ("read the standard and raised nothing") was false when said and is why the fourth man signed. Those who signed before the fourth bell on the 30th signed on a false sentence; those after on a true one heard wrong. Any door is for the first group, "a door for a court", possibly not even that. Sol: "That is the first plan you have brought me with the hole already found." Kael wants to ask the clerk aloud at the awning what he told the fourth signer, with Sol beside him. Sol puts on grey wool gloves. Ansa read the statement at the splicers' hall on the noon of the 1st to forty splicers; a woman asked if it was a warning; Ansa: "It's a receipt." Slate: 143 names by lamp-lighting on the 1st. At the awning the line runs past the end of the street; a second brazier; the younger clerk produces a stand with a placard WITHDRAWAL and a stack of leaves under a brass weight, saying "A question was put yesterday morning on your own stair" and "The house is glad to have had an answer ready." Kael reads the form: "Withdrawal of Provisional Engagement. I, the holder, having consented freely upon the standard as set, ask leave to withdraw my name. I make this request of my own will, advised by no officer of the Accord. Wages paid to the day of withdrawal are kept by the holder. No further engagement under the standard will be offered to the holder, or to any dependent named on the leaf." Kael sees it: a man who asked an officer of the Accord must either leave that off the paper or sign a lie beside it. Sol: Tam's sister (fourteen) is the dependent who would be shut out. The clerk: the house does not want to be "a stair that any man can go up and down at his leisure". Kael: "Both are true." Sol lifts the stack corner and counts; her glove comes back clean: the ink long dry, corners soft, the stack shaped by the days it has waited under the weight. Sol: "Two hundred. For a table with a hundred and forty-three names."
- OBJECTS AND COUNTS: 143 names by lamp-lighting on the 1st; withdrawal stack counted by Sol at about two hundred (the chapter does not say it is leaves; that is the reading); forty splicers heard Ansa read the statement.
- LINES AND BEATS: Kael to Tam: "I would be guessing at your rent." Tam: "I can read. My aunt saw to that." Three people stood in the spire room when Tam asked about taking back his name (Kael, Sol, Tam); the clerk knew the question anyway.
- OPEN: How the clerk learned what was asked on the spire stair; why the withdrawal stack was printed long before Tam asked; Tam's decision (not shown); Mara's ropewalk reading (not shown).
