Romantasy, Thornmere Reach, sequel to "What the Gift Demands." Isolde "Sol" Vane and Kael Ashworth, already together since Book 1, dual POV. Opens 9 Sunspire, Year 3 of the Reckoning Accord, two days after Book 1's close, as the Contract of Perpetual Surety naming the hidden "Deep Vault" and unnamed "Keeper of the Burning Ledger" becomes public record.

LOCKED RULES (the beat map must obey all of these):
1. Antagonist is House Thorne, NOT the Vault or Keeper directly. Thorne's founding bloodline served as wards for three generations under the old ward trade - the same system that failed Sol's mother - giving Thorne a genuine personal grievance, not manufactured cynicism. Thorne's real goal is to seize the Vault's exposed infrastructure for itself under cover of reform, on the internal logic "a house that has already paid the cost has earned the right to control who pays it next" - render this as an actual line of Thorne's dialogue somewhere in the book. Thorne petitions Kael specifically, as sitting auditor, to legitimize its claim, mirroring his father Valerius's role in Book 1 without repeating the mechanic. The Keeper stays unnamed and institutional throughout - never characterize it as a specific person.
2. Kael's failure mode is overcorrection, not concealment. He is named lead auditor for the ward-trade dismantlement and starts making unilateral protective decisions (solo audits, keeping Sol out of danger) without ever lying to her - he tells her after the fact every time. Do not write any scene where Kael conceals or lies about a fact - that mechanic belongs to Book 1 and must not repeat. Sol must show visible awareness or pushback on this pattern well before ch.20 (see rule 4) - do not let her remain passively unaware for the first third of the book.
3. Ch.5-8: Thorne's public reform proposal. Kael named lead auditor, begins acting unilaterally - a first small instance, not yet named as a problem. Establish here or in ch.13-15 that Sol has seen or been shown what a Thorne house seal looks like, so she can recognize one later without it feeling convenient.
4. Ch.13-15: infiltration set piece at a disused Vault-linked ward site. Real physical danger. A sealed document, the "Ward Deed of Succession," is discovered here but not yet understood.
5. Ch.16-18: Sol finds a Thorne-sealed entry pass in Kael's coat, dated a day he told her he spent on paperwork, to a site he never mentioned. She confronts him directly: "I'm not asking you to tell me everything. I'm asking you to stop deciding what I don't need to know." He admits it without denial or excuse. This names the pattern between them before ch.20 forces it into the open publicly.
6. Ch.20: the Ward Deed of Succession is fully understood - a ledger page sealed in Thorne's own house wax, listing ward contracts Thorne proposes to absorb, with a clause transferring ultimate authority over them to "the house administering reform" instead of the Accord. Kael cross-references it against Accord law and realizes it is legal seizure dressed as reform. He tells Sol immediately - no concealment, only confirmation of the already-named pattern.
7. Ch.26-28: Kael's solo handling backfires - alone without backup at a Thorne site, he is injured, or a ward-contracted ally is hurt as a direct result. Cost lands on the page. Sol and Kael, already a couple, share their first "I love you" here or it is interrupted by the fallout. Do NOT write a first kiss - they are already together entering this book.
8. Ch.33-35 is the RUPTURE: Sol confronts Kael that deciding for her, even honestly, still shuts her out as an equal. Real, unresolved cost. NOT resolved by ch.35.
9. Ch.36-39 is REPAIR: Kael publicly and formally cedes unilateral authority, making Sol co-equal lead on the audit, at real cost to his standing with House Ashworth. This book's first on-page intimate scene comes after this repair - Book 2 has its own scene count, not a continuation of Book 1's.
10. Ch.40-43 is the climax: a public Accord ceremony where Thorne's takeover bid, built on the Ward Deed of Succession, is exposed and defeated. Sol voluntarily undergoes the first "reformed" ward-taker trial as her own choice and risk. State explicitly that this does not alter the Sol/Kael involuntary cost-transfer mechanic from Book 1, which remains uncontrollable and non-redirectable between them.
11. Ch.44-45: short aftermath, Sol and Kael together. The final line reveals the Keeper's seat sits inside the Reach's own founding government - write this as an institutional, structural fact (a seat, an office), never as unmasking a specific named person.
12. Reveals stay staggered across at least two of the set pieces above, never dumped in one chapter window.
13. Spread the story over a realistic multi-week span with internally consistent in-world dates per chapter, starting 9 Sunspire, Year 3, two days after Book 1's close.
14. Every chapter ends on an open question, hook type varied, never the same type twice in a row.
15. No ward-binding, no chosen buffer role between Sol and Kael - the involuntary cost-transfer mechanic from Book 1 is never overwritten or made voluntary in this book.
