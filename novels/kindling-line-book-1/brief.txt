Romantasy, Thornmere Reach (vertical cliffside lineage-houses). Isolde "Sol" Vane (fallen house, secretly training) and Kael Ashworth (rival house, neutral Reckoning auditor with legal power over House Vane's claim), enemies-to-lovers, dual POV. Central mechanic: the Kindling (inherited flight-gift) burns years off the wielder; when forced to use it near someone, part of the cost transfers involuntarily to whoever is nearest, uncontrollable, never redirectable by choice. Sol can reduce HOW MUCH burns off with training; she never controls WHO absorbs it. Wards (contracted, consenting cost-absorbers, paid, controlled) are the system's sanctioned version. House Vane couldn't afford one, the real reason Sol's mother failed her Reckoning. House Corrin (antagonist, financial motive) controls the ward trade. Book 1 resolves its own conflict (the Reckoning, Corrin's scheme) and ends with Sol and Kael together (Happy For Now); a larger world-level threat stays open for Book 2.

LOCKED RULES (the beat map must obey all of these):
1. The cost transfer is always involuntary. Kael never volunteers as a ward or buffer for Sol, there is no ward-binding ceremony between them, and no technique lets Sol choose who pays. Wards belong only to the Corrin-controlled trade, where absorption is chosen and contracted. What happens to Kael is none of those things, which is why it is illegal and why the marks on his body are evidence he must report as auditor.
2. There is exactly ONE Reckoning, the public climax in ch.40-43. Earlier public events (ch.20, ch.26-28) are not Reckonings. No trial or verdict on House Vane's claim before ch.40. The Reckoning date is fixed in ch.1 and every countdown after it is consistent with it.
3. Kael's arc: ch.10 he learns the mechanic is worse than House Vane disclosed and does not report it (a deliberate first compromise). Ch.20 is the FIRST time anyone finds proof that House Corrin engineered Sol's mother's failure and that Kael's father Valerius was the ruling auditor at the time; before ch.20 show only Corrin pressure, never Valerius's involvement. In the same ch.20 window House Ashworth makes a concrete, dated offer for Kael to rule against Vane. Kael HIDES the proof from Sol from ch.20 until the rupture; he never sends it to her, and Sol does not learn it before ch.33.
4. Ch.26-28: a public event goes wrong and a ward is hurt or dies as a direct consequence of Kael's delay in surfacing the proof. The cost lands on the page. The first kiss is here or is interrupted by this fallout.
5. Ch.33-35 is the RUPTURE: Sol learns what Kael knew and when. It plays as "you knew and chose not to tell me," with real cost, not a misunderstanding. It is NOT resolved by ch.35. Repair happens ch.36-39 through a real, costly act by Kael (surfacing the proof publicly at cost to his house and position). The second on-page intimate scene comes after the repair.
6. Ch.40-43 Reckoning climax: Kael is standing close because his auditor role requires his presence, not because Sol chooses. The involuntary transfer hits him. Sol shows earned but incomplete control over HOW MUCH burns. Corrin's scheme is exposed and resolved. Ch.44-45 are short aftermath, Sol and Kael together, and the final line recontextualizes who really controls the ward contracts (the Book 2 threat).
7. Reveals are staggered across at least two set pieces: Kael's private cost-count, the engineered-failure proof, and the ward system's true horror never surface in the same chapter window.
8. Other set pieces: ch.5-8 first forced-proximity Kindling use, cost lands on Kael for the first time. Ch.13-15 night chase across the lineage-house ledges, shared wounds under real physical danger.
9. Spread the story over about ten weeks with realistic gaps between chapter dates, not one day per chapter. Every chapter gets a concrete, internally consistent in-world date.
10. Every chapter ends on an open question, varying the hook type, never the same type twice in a row.
