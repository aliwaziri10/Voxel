The cold bit deeper down here. Not the clean bite of wind on the spire's face but something older, damp and patient, seeping from stone that had not seen sunlight in generations. Sol held the lantern higher, its light trembling across walls scored with the same claw-marks she had found in the training shaft above. The Kindling did not burn in her today. She had not flown in three days, not since the ledger fragment in Kael's satchel had sent her searching through her mother's papers with a sickness growing in her chest.

The concealed door had not been on any Vane blueprint. She had found it by accident, pressing her palm against a section of wall that sounded hollow beneath the tap of her knife hilt. The catch released with a sound like a held breath finally exhaled. Behind it yawned a stairwell descending into dark.

She had expected a storage crypt. Perhaps her mother's private journals. Instead she found a room the size of the council chamber above, its floor flagged with slate worn smooth by decades of boots. Shelves lined every wall, crammed with bound volumes in Corrin livery. Red leather. Gold tooling. The sigil of the ward trade stamped on every spine.

Her fingers shook as she pulled the first volume free. *Ward Contracts: Northern Reach, Year 1-5 of the Accord.* The pages inside were dense with names, dates, terms of absorption. Contracted cost-absorbers. Consenting. Paid. Controlled. The sanctioned system. She turned to the index, heart hammering, and found her mother's name on page forty-seven.

*Elara Vane. Reckoning Year 1. Ward: Marian Corrin. Contracted absorption: 12 years. Actual absorption: 23 years. Cause of death: Kindling exhaustion.*

The ink blurred. Sol blinked, forced her eyes to focus. Marian Corrin. Not a random ward drawn from the pool. A Corrin. The family that controlled the trade had placed one of their own as the absorber for the most critical flight in a generation. And the contract had been exceeded by eleven years. Eleven years burned from a woman who had not agreed to them. Eleven years stolen.

She flipped forward. *Kira Vane. Training accident. Ward: Torin Corrin. Contracted: 3 years. Actual: 8 years.* Her sister. The accident that had left Kira grounded at seventeen, her wings clipped before she had ever truly flown. Sol had been told the ward had absorbed more than expected. A tragedy. An anomaly. Not a pattern.

Her hands moved faster now, pulling volume after volume. *House Dresen. House Merrow. House Falke.* Every major lineage in the Reach. Every Reckoning. Every training incident. Every emergency Kindling use. All of them contracted to Corrin wards. All of them exceeding their terms. The margins grew thicker with notation as the years progressed. *Adjusted per Auditor Valerius Ashworth ruling. Adjusted per Auditor Valerius Ashworth ruling. Adjusted per Auditor Valerius Ashworth ruling.*

Kael's father. The ruling auditor at the time of her mother's death. The name appeared dozens of times, each notation a rubber stamp on theft.

Sol's breath came shallow. She had known Corrin was powerful. She had known the ward trade was rigged. She had not known it was *this* complete. Every single contract. Every single year. The Corrins did not just control the market. They *were* the market. Every ward in the Reach bore their sigil or answered to their agents. There was no alternative. There had never been an alternative.

House Vane had not failed to afford a ward. House Vane had been *denied* any ward but a Corrin one. And the Corrin ward had taken what was not contracted.

She found the latest volume. *Ward Contracts: Northern Reach, Year 3 of the Accord. Current.* The pages were already filled through the first month. She turned to the Vane entry, expecting blank space. Her house had no ward. Her house had no contract.

*House Vane. Contracted Ward: None. Status: Default to Corrin Pool. Designated Absorber: Corrin Selection Pending.*

The words sat there, innocent as a death sentence. When Sol's Reckoning came, when she flew the trial that would decide her house's survival, the Corrin pool would provide the absorber. A Corrin would choose. A Corrin would absorb. And the contract would say whatever the Corrins needed it to say.

She slammed the volume shut. The sound cracked through the chamber like a gunshot.

Her pack sat at her feet. Inside, the copying kit she had lifted from the scribe's table above. Vellum sheets. Ink. A steady hand. She had perhaps two hours before the evening meal bell summoned her to the great hall, before her absence would be noticed, before someone might think to check the concealed stairwell.

She worked fast. The critical pages. Her mother's contract. Kira's. The index pages showing the Corrin monopoly. The Auditor Valerius notations. The current volume's Vane entry. Each sheet took three minutes. Her hands knew the work. She had copied enough forbidden texts in the training shaft to have developed a rhythm. Breath. Copy. Turn. Breath. Copy. Turn.

The lantern guttered. She fed it a sliver of pitch from her pocket.

Halfway through the current volume's index, she paused. A name caught her eye in the marginalia of a Dresen contract. *Kael Ashworth. Auditor observation. Cost transfer anomaly noted. No ward contracted. Recommendation: monitor.*

Her pen stopped. The ink pooled on the vellum.

Kael. In the ledger. Noted by a Corrin scribe. An anomaly. A cost transfer with no ward contracted. That could only mean one thing. The night in the training shaft. The first time she had burned near him. The years that had vanished from his face in the lantern light afterward.

She had not known. She had *felt* it, the wrongness of it, the years peeling away from someone who had not signed a contract, who had not consented. But she had not known it was recorded. That Corrin knew. That Corrin was *watching*.

The recommendation chilled her. *Monitor.* Not report. Not investigate. Monitor. As if Kael were a specimen in a jar.

She copied that page too. Her hand trembled badly now, the vellum rippling under her fingers.

When the last sheet was dry, she bound the copies with a leather thong and slipped them into the inner pocket of her coat, against her skin. The original volumes went back exactly as she had found them. Red leather. Gold tooling. Perfect order. No dust disturbed on the shelves except where her own fingers had brushed.

She climbed the stairs on shaking legs. The concealed door clicked shut behind her, sealing the chamber once more. The training shaft above was colder now, the wind howling through the cracks in the spire's face. She moved through it like a ghost, unseen, unheard.

The great hall smelled of roasted root vegetables and woodsmoke. Her father sat at the high table with the steward and the senior wing-captain, his face drawn in the way it always was these days. Kira picked at her food beside him, her left sleeve pinned empty at the shoulder. Sol took her place without speaking, her appetite gone.

Kael was not there. He had not been at the midday meal either. His auditor's badge gave him leave to move through the Reach on Corrin business, or so the story went. Sol knew better now. He was monitoring. Being monitored. Caught in the same web.

She waited until the meal ended, until her father retired to his study with the steward, until Kira was helped to her rooms by the wing-captain's daughter. Then she slipped to the solar where the outgoing post was kept, the leather satchel hanging by the door for the dawn courier.

The note was small. A scrap of vellum torn from her copying sheets. *Found the ledger. Corrin controls all contracts. Mother's ward was Marian Corrin. Kael's father signed the adjustments. Meet me at the east buttress tonight. Third bell. S.*

She tucked it into the margin of the top volume in the satchel, the Corrin ledger that traveled between spires. The courier would carry it to Ashworth Spire by morning. Kael would find it when he checked the post. He would come.

She returned to her room and waited.

The third bell rang from the central spire. Its tone carried across the Reach, deep and sonorous, vibrating in the stone bones of every lineage house. Sol wrapped her wings tight, pulled her cloak close, and climbed to the east buttress.

The wind there was savage. It tore at her cloak, whipped her hair across her face, tried to pry her fingers from the handholds carved into the stone. She did not move. She watched the sky path between Vane and Ashworth, the narrow ledge route that auditors used, that couriers used, that no one else dared.

The courier did not come.

The fourth bell rang. Then the fifth. The sky path remained empty. No lantern light bobbing in the dark. No silhouette against the stars.

Something had happened. The courier had been turned back. The note had been found. The ledger had been intercepted.

Or worse. The courier had never left Vane Spire at all.

Sol's fingers went numb on the stone. She had left the copied pages in her room, hidden beneath a loose floorboard. If the note was found, they would search her room. They would find the copies. They would know she knew.

She should fly back. She should retrieve the pages and run. The Kindling would take her far, fast, before anyone could stop her. But the cost would land on someone. Kira. Her father. A random ward in the Corrin pool. The transfer was involuntary. She could not choose who paid.

She could reduce how much burned. That was her training. That was her secret. But she could not control the *who*.

The sixth bell rang. The night was fully fallen now, the Reach a jagged silhouette against stars. Somewhere in the dark, Kael was waiting for a note that would never reach him. Somewhere in the dark, the Corrins were reading her words and smiling.

She pressed her forehead to the cold stone. The wind keened through the spire's bones.

*What do I do now?*
