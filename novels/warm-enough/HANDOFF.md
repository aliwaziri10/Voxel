## Decision (made by Claude for Zia on 2026-10-22, Zia may overrule)
Book 2 is a winter book: a grumpy, guarded knitter and a sunny newcomer schoolmaster who cannot get warm. Different from Book 1 in season, craft, who is the newcomer, and wound (giving versus receiving, not leaving versus staying). Market tags it serves: cozy fantasy, small town, slow burn, forced proximity (snowed in), grumpy/sunshine, found family, second-chance-ex-returns tension, winter/seasonal, HEA, closed door.
Working title: Warm Enough. Alternates: The Open Door; A Winter's Worth of Warm. Pen name: Elif Kessler. Series line: Larkmere, Book Two. Check Amazon title and series-name search before locking metadata.
Logline: In Larkmere every newcomer is knit a winter sweater, and it only warms if the knitter means it. Hester Tansley would rather freeze than mean it. Ansel Brandt would rather freeze than ask.

## Why 40 chapters (revision 2, 2026-10-22, after Zia asked why not 50 or 60)
Version 1 had 32 chapters only because Book 1 had 32. That was inherited, not a market decision. Market check: romance sits around 70,000 to 100,000 words; fantasy 80,000 to 120,000 with most around 100,000 to 115,000. A cozy fantasy romance belongs at the top of the romance range and the bottom of the fantasy range: about 100,000 words. 32 chapters was about 80,000. 40 chapters at 2,300 to 2,700 words is about 92,000 to 108,000 words, squarely right. 50 to 60 chapters (125,000 to 160,000 words) overshoots what romance readers expect, makes the print book thicker and dearer, raises the sag risk in a book whose first kiss is already late, and makes Zia's hand-written, hand-proofread process much slower. The real gain is not length but more turns: version 2 adds a returning rival, two twists, a community set piece and a hook rule. Kindle Unlimited pays by pages read, so a longer book earns more per finished read, but only if readers finish it.
Changes from version 1: (1) 32 to 40 chapters, first kiss at chapter 33 (82.5 percent), release in chapter 40. (2) Added Piers Ellory, the cartographer who left nine winters ago, who returns on the winter post: a kind, charming, wrongly-timed rival, never a villain. (3) Twist one: Ansel's tin holds a scrap of grey wool that twelve-year-old Hester gave him on the quay the one winter he lived in Larkmere as a boy. It is the only thing he has never given away. (4) Twist two: the first Winter Knitter's page in Gran's pattern book says the toll is paid back only when the knitter is knit for. Nobody has ever knit for Hester. Ansel knits her the scarf. (5) Community set piece: the Great Knit-In at the Kettle Room during the cold snap (found family). (6) Hook rule: every chapter ends on a turn, a question or a held image, never a summary. (7) The old Longnight, hut and Breaking set pieces stay.

## Canon lock
CAST (all new, audited against every other Voxel book on 2026-10-22; Pell, Bram, Hale, Oakes were rejected as already used; Piers, Ellory, Ostler checked clear):
- Hester Tansley: heroine, Winter Knitter of Larkmere, late thirties. POV in EVEN chapters. Dry, exact, knitting metaphors only. Tag: grey wool scarf she never takes off, cold fingers.
- Ansel Brandt: hero, new schoolmaster, late thirties. POV in ODD chapters. Sunny, funny, gives everything away, cannot receive, says "I'm fine". Comparisons from the schoolroom, rivers, chalk, children. No wool comparisons in his chapters. Tag: wears every coat he owns, children call him the Onion. Keeps a flat tin.
- Winifred "Gran" Tansley: Hester's grandmother, past eighty, retired Winter Knitter, bent hands, meddler. Tag: copper kettle, cane.
- Tilda Brook: eleven, Hester's unwanted apprentice, bold, knits badly with total heart. Tag: lumpy blue scarf. Never in an intimate beat.
- Mina Cray: Hester's friend, runs the Kettle Room tea shop. Blunt. Tag: flour-white apron, chipped blue teapot.
- Dunstan Rudd: hill farmer at High Fold, sixties, few words.
- Piers Ellory: cartographer, left Larkmere at the thaw nine winters ago wearing the one warm sweater Hester ever made; returns on the winter post. Kind, easy, rueful. Tag: ink on his fingers, the old jumper still faintly warm. Not a villain. He left because he could not bear to be that warm and owe nothing.
- Mr. Ostler: old neighbor of the school, chilled easily, borrows the Green in chapter 29.
- Never name or involve any Book 1 character. Shared world only (see series bible).
PLACES: Larkmere, Larkmere Water (frozen from early winter), Spindle Lane, the Spindle House (shop, workroom with drying racks, loft), the Kettle Room, the lake school and the master's stone cottage with one stove, the Ice Road (lantern poles across the ice), the Net Sheds (the ferry Heron is laid up there until the thaw), High Fold (hill pasture) and its shepherd's hut.
MAGIC (state once, never vary):
1. Wool holds the honest feeling of the hands that knit it, row by row. Care knit in stays as warmth. Polite knitting holds only tidiness and is cold.
2. A knit warms its wearer only if the wearer lets it in. Someone who refuses care wears it like armor.
3. The warmth is set when knit and does not change after. A garment is warm where it was knit warm and cold where it was knit politely (so he can feel where she began to feel). Piers's old jumper is still warm; warm knits last.
4. A knit with no flaw sits like a shell. Gran's saying: "Leave one door open." One deliberate dropped stitch lets warmth in.
5. Newcomer's toll: anyone arriving in the last of autumn who has not been truly knit for feels the cold of the whole lake in the bones until thaw, indoors and out. Not illness, never danger, no villain, no doctor can help. Fire and coats do not fix it. It lifts at the Breaking regardless; a true knit worn and let in lifts it sooner.
6. Anyone's hands can knit warm, not only a Tansley's.
7. The first Winter Knitter's page (revealed chapter 32): the toll is paid back only when the knitter is knit for. Hester has been cold for nine winters because no one has ever knit for her.
8. Harbor lanterns are lit at dusk (shared custom). The ferry Heron runs again after the Breaking.
NOT: not a curse placed by a person, not a spell on the schoolmaster alone, not a countdown with numbers.
OBJECTS (describe exactly this way every time): the Grey (flawless undyed grey jumper, cold as slate); the Blue (cabled, half-finished, never completed); the Green (moss green, seed stitch; warm from the shoulders to the waist and at the left cuff, cool at the hem because the hem was knit first, politely); Tilda's lumpy blue scarf (warm at once); the tin (flat, dented, holds one scrap of undyed grey wool with a twisted first row); the Polite Knit (flawless charcoal jumper, cold, given in chapter 36); the Open Door (cream jumper with one deliberate dropped stitch at the left side seam); Ansel's terrible scarf (lopsided, rust red, knitted in one night with Gran's help, warm).
LOCKED LINES (once only): "Leave one door open." Gran, chapter 6; echoed once by Hester, chapter 32. "Stop being fine at me." Hester, chapter 10 only. "I'm cold. I've been cold since the ferry and I didn't want to be any trouble. Will you stay here a minute?" Ansel, chapter 33 only, the first time he admits it. "Everything I make warm, I lose." Hester, chapter 35 only. "I came for the winter I was warm." Ansel, chapter 31 only. "Stay." Hester asks, plainly, in chapter 40.
TIME: no dates, weekdays, years, digits for ages. Season, light, weather and wool tell time. Rows and stitches are texture only, never a count that matters.
STYLE: all rules in HUMAN_WRITING.md and the charter. American spelling except "grey" is used throughout this book. No em dashes.
HIDDEN FACTS: Ansel mends things in town at night (seed 3, reveal 10). Tilda's scarf (seed 5, reveal 13). Ansel is the eldest of a river-town family and sends his wages home (seed 1, reveal 21). The tin (seed 9, reveal 30). Piers and the warm jumper (seed 2, confirmed 17 to 18, his reason revealed 23). Gran dropped a stitch on purpose in her husband's sweater and put Hester in a cold room with Ansel on purpose (seed 6, reveal 36). The first Winter Knitter's page (seed 22, reveal 32). Nobody has ever knit for Hester (reveal 32 to 37).

## Chapter plan (A = Ansel, H = Hester; touch level in brackets)
ACT ONE, COLD
1 A, last of autumn [0]: Last ferry before the freeze. Ansel steps off shivering, gives away every blanket and pie the quay presses on him. Across the harbor a woman with a closed face is knitting; her yarn tail slides to his boot and is faintly warm. Gran announces the Winter Knitter will see to him. Hester's mouth thins.
2 H, last of autumn [0]: Spindle House at night. Gran: "He's yours." Hester explains to herself why she makes correct wool, not warm wool. Decides to knit the best sweater Larkmere has seen by skill alone. The warm yarn tail is still in her pocket and it infuriates her.
3 A, last of autumn [1]: First day at the school, the stove, the Onion nickname. Hester arrives with her tape to measure him on a stool in front of the class. She never touches skin. A look held one second too long. The mittens on the school hooks, her old work, feel like a rumor of mittens.
4 H, first frost [2]: Wrist reading in the Spindle House with Gran in the corner. His wrist is a river stone. First rows of the Grey lie flat as a held breath. He tries to carry Gran's coal.
5 A, first frost [0]: The Grey left on his doorstep at dawn. Perfect fit, cold as slate. He wears it over three layers and tells everyone it is wonderful. Alone with the stove, writes home that all is well. Tilda asks his favorite color. He puts a flat tin under his pillow.
6 H, first frost [1]: Mina teases at the Kettle Room. Gran: "Leave one door open." Hester starts the Blue, a cabled masterpiece. One row comes out warm; she rips it out in horror.
7 A, early winter [1]: The lake freezes. School skating day, he falls again and again; Hester at the shore to fetch Tilda does not laugh and does not help. He cannot stop thinking about the not-laughing.
8 H, early winter [3]: First try-on of the half-knit Blue in the workroom. She fixes his collar. For one breath the wool is warm as a stone with a morning of sun in it. She sends him out rudely.
ACT TWO, THE THREAD
9 A, early winter [3]: Skein winding. His arms out, the wool looped on his wrists, her facing him, the thread between them for an hour while Gran sleeps. The wool is warm where it crosses his wrists. Fingers brush at the hand-off. Hook: Gran is awake in the dark and smiling.
10 H, early winter [2]: Before dawn she finds him on her roof with a lantern clearing ice, shaking, grinning, fine. He has been mending the street since the first frost. "Stop being fine at me." He laughs it off and goes.
11 A, early winter [2]: Market on the shore; he carries every basket in town. Hester takes him by the sleeve into the Kettle Room, sits him down and makes him eat soup in front of everyone. The first thing he has accepted since the ferry.
12 H, early winter [4]: Ice-slick lane in a snow squall. She grabs him, he catches her at the waist, a drift pushes them against a wall. Neither lets go first.
13 A, Longnight [3]: The whole town walks out on the ice with lanterns and rings the longest night in their Winter Knits. He is blue in the Grey. Tilda wraps her lumpy scarf round his neck in front of everyone. Steam rises off him. The ring gasps. Hester's face.
14 H, Longnight [4]: Dawn on the ice. She sits apart, ashamed that a child's bad knitting beat her best. Ansel brings two cups and sits, shoulder to shoulder because the sleeping ring closes up round them. He says he is sorry the scarf was not hers. The sun comes up.
15 A, midwinter [1]: He is warm at the neck only. Tilda explains the rules in kid language. He stands outside the Spindle House at midnight and watches Hester knit with her eyes closed and a soft face. He cannot make himself knock. Reveal: eldest of a river family, sends his wages home, never asks.
16 H, midwinter [2]: Midpoint. She starts the Green and lets herself mean one row. It stays warm. She does not rip it out. Gran gives her the old pattern book. First fear: warm things go. Hook: a sledge bell on the Ice Road, the winter post.
ACT THREE, THE RETURN
17 A, midwinter [2]: The winter post crosses the ice with a letter from his sister and a stranger in a worn grey-blue jumper: Piers Ellory. Hester goes white on the quay. Ansel sees it and says nothing.
18 H, midwinter [2]: The Kettle Room. Piers is easy and rueful: he is back to finish the map of the lake, and to see her. His old jumper is still warm at the cuff after nine winters. Warm knits last. She remembers the thaw he left.
19 A, midwinter [4]: The Green's fitting before the shop window. Her thumb runs down his forearm to the wrist for the sleeve length. The left cuff is warm. He gasps. She drops the pins. Piers watches from the Kettle Room. Ansel offers Piers his spare cot, and means it, and hates himself.
20 H, deep winter [3]: Piers asks her to walk the Ice Road at dusk and asks her to come away with him at the thaw. She says no. For one second she could have said yes. Across the lake Ansel sees them from the school window.
21 A, deep winter [4]: Cold snap; his stove fails. At night Hester comes with a pot and blankets and sits on the floor with her back to the door, knitting till dawn, until he takes the blankets. They talk in the dark: his family, her work, nothing about Piers. At dawn she covers his hands with hers. She came to him.
22 H, deep winter [4]: The cold snap bites the whole town: polite winter knits are not enough. The Great Knit-In at the Kettle Room, everyone knitting through the night. Ansel winds skeins for all and refuses to try a needle ("I am better at holding things"). On a crowded bench her hands steady his over a skein. Gran's pattern book falls open to a pressed page; Gran snaps it shut.
23 A, deep winter [3]: Piers leaves on the winter post. Ansel carries his bag, because he cannot not. Piers tells him why he left: "I could not stand to be that warm and owe nothing. Don't be me. Let her be owed." Ansel does not understand yet.
24 H, deep winter [3]: Climb to High Fold for fleece, roped together in the snow. Dunstan's hearth, the sheep, raw lanolin on his fingers: "warm." They start down as the sky closes.
25 A, deep winter [5]: Blizzard. The shepherd's hut, one stove, one blanket. She sits as far off as she can. The stove runs low. "Come here." Shoulder to shoulder in the blanket. Neither sleeps. He almost asks.
26 H, deep winter [6]: He sleeps for the first time all winter, against her shoulder. She knits the Green's last sleeve by firelight and admits she has meant every row since the roof. Their hands joined under the blanket.
27 A, deep winter [2]: The search party with lanterns at dawn finds them wrapped in one blanket. Gran and Tilda stand at the bottom of the hill. They step apart in front of the whole town. He tries to refuse breakfast. "Eat."
28 H, late winter [5]: She finishes the Green. Mina: "You have been polite for nine winters." At dusk she gives it to him. It warms from shoulders to waist. He takes her hand in both of his to thank her too long. Cool at the hem.
29 A, late winter [4]: He lends the Green to Mr. Ostler, who was chilled. Hester is not angry. She is frightened. After hours in the schoolroom she stands too close and tells him to keep one thing. He says there is nothing he can keep. Tilda, sent for blankets, is in his cottage and finds the tin.
30 H, late winter [3]: Tilda brings the tin to the Spindle House. A scrap of undyed grey, one twisted first row: Hester's own mistake from the winter she was twelve. She walks to the school and asks him. Twist one: he was the cold boy on the quay for one winter, and a girl gave him a scrap. He has never given it away.
31 A, late winter [7]: Night on the ice. "I came for the winter I was warm." "I was twelve." Foreheads together, breath shared, no kiss. He still cannot ask. She cannot either.
32 H, late winter [8]: The workroom, the cream wool, she shows him the dropped stitch. "Leave one door open." Faces an inch apart. Gran's kettle screams overhead and Gran calls down. Gran then shows her the pressed page: the toll is paid back when the knitter is knit for. Nobody has ever knit for a Tansley. Hester has been cold for nine winters.
ACT FOUR, THE BREAKING
33 A, late winter [9]: Dusk on the Ice Road between the lantern poles, the ice booming. "I'm cold. I've been cold since the ferry and I didn't want to be any trouble. Will you stay here a minute?" "I am staying." The first kiss. His hands are warm without any wool. A worse hunger by morning.
34 H, late winter [10]: He walks down Spindle Lane without a single coat and the Kettle Room goes silent. Tilda wins a bet. They are never alone: every doorway has a neighbor. One kiss stolen among the drying wool is cut short.
35 A, late winter [10]: The second measuring in his cottage by the stove, the tape across his chest and waist, kissing between the measurements. She stops. "Everything I make warm, I lose." Piers. "I'm not a map." "You haven't been asked to stay." Neither can ask.
36 H, thaw [10]: The Heron is tested in the Net Sheds. Panic. She hands him the Polite Knit, flawless, cold, as a goodbye gift. He says it is perfect and thanks her. Both are back in their old selves. Gran at night: she dropped a stitch on purpose for her own husband, a newcomer too; she put Hester in a cold room on purpose; warm things do not leave, people do. Hester starts the Open Door.
37 A, thaw [10]: He wears the cold Polite Knit and is blue again. Tilda tells him nobody has ever knit for Hester. For the first time he asks someone for help: Gran teaches him to knit all night. A terrible rust red scarf, warm.
38 H, thaw [10]: The last night of ice. She finishes the Open Door. Neither can sleep. They meet on the shore in the dark, each holding a parcel behind their back, and neither hands it over. The ice groans.
39 A, thaw [10]: The Breaking. The whole town on the shore. He gives her the scarf in front of everyone, the first thing he has ever given to be worn by someone who gave nothing first. She puts it on and gasps. The ice goes out with a roar.
40 H, thaw [10]: She gives him the Open Door and says, "Stay." He puts it on and it is warm from collar to hem, including the open door. The lake opens, the Heron whistles. End on the last real beat, not a summary.
