# Voice Guide: Warm Enough

Read `novels/EDITORIAL_CHARTER.md` and `novels/HUMAN_WRITING.md` first. Canon and plan: `novels/warm-enough/HANDOFF.md`.

## Point of view
Close third person, past tense. Odd chapters are Ansel Brandt. Even chapters are Hester Tansley.

## Ansel (odd chapters)
Warm, quick, funny, generous. Jokes first, feeling second, and the feeling leaks out in what he does with his hands. Notices children, faces, who is cold, who is carrying too much. Never says what he needs. Says "I'm fine" and the narration lets the reader see he is not. Comparisons from the schoolroom, chalk, slates, rivers, ferries, children. NO wool or knitting comparisons in his chapters. Sentences run looser and longer when he is happy and shorten when he is cold.

## Hester (even chapters)
Dry, exact, guarded, funny in a flat way. Notices hands, tension, gauge, how a thing is made and where it will fail. Comparisons from knitting and wool: tension, stitch, ply, selvedge, cast on. NO schoolroom or river comparisons in her chapters. Short, clipped sentences when she is controlling herself; a long unspooling sentence when control slips.

## The magic on the page
Show the wool in every chapter, small and specific: where a garment is warm and where it is cold, what a hem or a cuff does. Warmth is felt in the body, never explained as a rule in dialogue except by Gran or Tilda in their own words. Rows and stitches are texture, not counts.

## Romance rules (same as the series)
- Desire lives in small physical detail: a thumb on a wrist, a thread pulled tight between two people, breath on a collar, the effort of staying still.
- Pressed closeness is built from restraint: a locked grip, a held breath, eyes on a point, a word not said. Sensual, never explicit.
- Every chapter raises the ache one notch. After the kiss in chapter 33 the wanting only grows. Release only in chapter 40.
- Tilda and Gran never appear in an intimate beat. They interrupt, tease and meddle at the edges.
- Interruptions come from feeling, weather, magic or someone who loves them. Never a procedure.
- Every chapter ends on a turn, a question or a held image, never a summary.

## Locked fixed text
See HANDOFF.md: names, places, objects, locked lines, hidden-fact reveals.

## Banned
Everything in HUMAN_WRITING.md. Plus: no digits for years, ages, dates; no weekday or month names; "days later"; planning words in prose (chapter, beat, protagonist, sub-beat, touch level, canon); em dashes; the words some, something, someone, somewhere, somehow, particular, the specific, the kind of, kind of. Use "grey" throughout, American spelling otherwise. Names to keep out: Wren, Pell, Bram, Hale, Oakes.
