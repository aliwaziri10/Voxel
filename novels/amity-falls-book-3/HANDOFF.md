# WHAT THE BLOOD REMEMBERS — HANDOFF NOTES

**Series:** The Amity Falls Series, Book 3
**Working title:** "What the Blood Remembers" — TENTATIVE, not yet confirmed by Zia.
**Target length:** 100,000+ words, ~42-45 chapters, same structure as Books 1 and 2.
**Status:** CONFIRMED by Zia 2026-09-14 (said "start"). Drafting underway, currently one chapter at a time per Zia's request (session resource conservation), no longer multiple chapters per turn.

## What this project is

Direct sequel to "What the Valley Still Owes" (Book 2, `novels/amity-falls-book-2/`, complete at 41 chapters). Picks up from Book 2's final-page hook: Adelaide's letter named Ambrose Whitlock as the one who taught her the bargain, not its origin, meaning Denise's fifty-year cycle was only one branch of something older.

**Book 3's engine, CONFIRMED:** the bargain is hereditary as well as taught, and skips generations. Dev, Priya's brother, is discovered to descend from the Whitlock line through his and Priya's late mother's side, a fact none of them knew. The hunger is waking in him, untrained and unwanted. Unlike Denise's decades of controlled, surgical harvesting, an unwilling, untrained carrier is unstable: left alone, it will not take one memory every several years, it will take a great deal from a large part of the valley at once, in a single uncontrolled event, and it is actively endangering Dev's physical health as it grows, not just his memory. A hidden faction, referred to in the beat map as "the quiet family," who have tracked Whitlock bloodlines across generations, resurface, wanting the awakening to complete rather than be stopped, believing total completion would end the practice everywhere. The valley has six weeks, until the night of the autumn eclipse, to find a way to save Dev without repeating Denise's near-catastrophe or letting the quiet family get what they want. A council-adjacent mole feeding the quiet family information is also confirmed for Act Two (see beat_map.md ch.22-23).

## Why this direction over the alternatives considered

On 2026-09-14, Zia asked for a critical review of the initial pitch (a brand-new stranger character discovering a Whitlock inheritance) before committing. Six options were weighed:
1. Brand-new stranger as carrier (lowest risk, weakest emotional stakes).
2. Wren as carrier (highest emotional stakes, but risk of contradicting her stated parentage in Book 1/2 canon if it exists — NOT verified, so NOT used).
3. Dev as carrier (strong emotional stakes, low retcon risk since his lineage was never pinned down in Books 1-2, and direct payoff of the trust-repair arc built with Priya across Book 2 ch.8-9).
4. A hard countdown clock (the eclipse) instead of Book 2's slower, months-long pace.
5. A hidden antagonist faction (the quiet family) to actively oppose, not just a mystery to research.
6. Real physical danger to the carrier, not just memory loss, raising stakes past Book 2's version.

Chosen combination: 3 + 4 + 5 + 6, confirmed and now in active drafting.

## Established canon this book must not contradict

- Everything in Book 2's HANDOFF.md "Established canon (final, for reference)" section carries forward unchanged: Denise alive, aged naturally, living alone with valley check-ins; Caleb's memory fully restored; Yusuf as permanent council chair who learned from his earlier hesitation; Wren a Finder apprentice who has learned real caution about full reads since her ch.12/ch.26/ch.36 costs in Book 2.
- Dev's arc across Book 2 (ch.8-9 introduction and trust-repair with Priya, ch.24 onward joining the investigation fully, ch.36-38 alongside Priya in the climax, a hint of something more between Dev and Priya left open but undeveloped) is the direct foundation for his role here.
- Priya's professional caution and instincts, established Book 1 and sharpened through Book 2's incident-log discipline, carries forward in how she investigates.
- Ambrose Whitlock remains largely unseen and unexplained by design, deepened but not fully resolved here, per the planned ch.45 hook into a possible Book 4.
- Ch.1-2 canon: Dev experiences three unwitnessed blackouts (4 minutes, 20 minutes, several hours), each one worse than the last; the third ends with him at the old stone wall along the Voss property holding a smooth river stone he has no memory of picking up. A fourth blackout happens directly in front of Priya on her porch step; she reacts with fear, not suspicion, and takes him straight to Mara and Caleb that same night.
- Ch.3 canon: Wren performs a light, careful read on the stone only (not on Dev directly), consistent with the caution she learned in Book 2. She surfaces a single word: Whitlock. The group connects it immediately to Adelaide's letter.
- Ch.4 canon: Re-reading Adelaide's letter, the group notes she describes the bargain as "taught," not inherited, but concludes that doesn't rule out a hereditary channel Adelaide simply never knew about. Odette, consulted, recalls an old family saying of her own grandmother's: "some debts choose their own collectors," which the group takes as folk-memory confirmation that the bargain can move by more than deliberate teaching.
- Ch.5 canon: Priya and Dev search county marriage and death records and confirm their grandmother Eleanor was born Eleanor Whitlock, married Samuel Halloway in 1947 and changed her name, two years after an Ambrose Whitlock (unrecorded cause of death, 1945) died. This Ambrose may be the same man from Adelaide's letter (aged unnaturally, per the established bargain mechanics) or a descendant carrying the name forward. Eleanor deliberately buried the Whitlock name and never spoke of her girlhood again. Dev and Priya conclude their grandmother was trying, and ultimately failing, to outrun what the bargain eventually found its way back to in Dev, two generations later.

## Standing rules for this book (carried from Book 2 unless noted)

- Target 2,000–2,500 words per chapter, 2,100 acceptable floor. Priority is pace and page-turn tension over hitting a word count exactly, per Zia's standing instruction from Book 2.
- **No em dashes anywhere in chapter prose.** Book 2 caught one late lapse in a high-tension action scene (ch.36) — treat action beats as a specific risk area for this, not just a general rule.
- Match established voice exactly: Mara dry/controlled/engineering-metaphor, Caleb steady/plainspoken/dry humor, Wren blunt/raw/fast-talking, Priya steady/procedural, Dev cautious/self-monitoring but growing more assertive across this book specifically as his agency becomes the point of his arc.
- **"Particular" remains the single most recurrent AI-tell word across all three books so far.** Caught and self-corrected in ch.3 (3 instances), ch.5 (1 instance) of this book; ch.1, ch.2, ch.4 were clean on first pass. Mandatory find-and-replace pass on every chapter before pushing regardless of how clean a recent streak has been, since it recurs unpredictably. Same for "the specific ___" and "the kind of ___" constructions.
- No other AI-authorship tells (uniform clause-stacked openings, rule-of-three lists, stock phrases).
- All filenames lowercase (`chapter_NN.md`).
- Zia is a non-technical, voice-dictation user — file paths and full GitHub URLs always given in copy blocks.
- Push each finished chapter directly via the API, verify by reading it back, before moving to the next.
- Repo owner is `aliwaziri10`, not `Wazzaboyzz`.
- Per Zia 2026-09-14 (updated): draft ONE chapter at a time now, self-correct and push it, update this table and beat_map.md, then stop and wait rather than continuing straight into the next chapter, due to session length considerations.
