CHAPTER TWO

Dev never got the chance to rehearse the conversation the way he'd planned, because Thursday came before he was ready for it, and he was still halfway through deciding how to start when the fourth blackout took the decision entirely out of his hands.

He'd made it to Priya's porch steps, the good bottle of wine he'd bought that afternoon still in one hand, when the world simply folded shut around him mid-sentence, taking whatever he'd been about to say and swallowing it whole.

Priya, standing in her open doorway watching him climb the steps, saw the whole thing happen in real time, and it was worse, far worse, than anything Dev himself had experienced in his own three blackouts, because she had no gap in her own memory to soften it. She watched her brother's eyes go suddenly distant mid-word, watched his body sway, watched the bottle of wine slip from fingers that had simply forgotten, for a moment, that they were holding anything at all.

"Dev." She caught the bottle before it shattered on the steps, catching his arm with her other hand a half second later as his knees started to buckle. "Dev, look at me."

He didn't look at her. His eyes stayed open but unfocused, fixed on some middle distance that wasn't the porch, wasn't her face, wasn't anything Priya could see. His lips moved slightly, no sound coming out, and for one terrible, suspended moment Priya thought of every worst possibility at once, seizure, stroke, some catastrophic failure of a body she'd only just finished learning to trust again.

Then, as quickly as it had come, it passed. Dev blinked, staggered slightly, and found himself sitting on Priya's porch steps with no memory of sitting down, his sister's hand gripping his arm hard enough to leave marks, her face white with a fear he'd never once seen her wear, not even during the worst of his own trial.

"Priya." His voice came out rough, disoriented. "What happened. What just happened."

"You tell me." She knelt in front of him, both hands now on his shoulders, steadying him and herself at the same time. "You went somewhere, Dev. Right in front of me. Your eyes were open and you weren't there. How long has this been happening?"

He wanted, badly, to lie again, to reach for the same automatic reassurance he'd been offering everyone for days now. But looking at his sister's face, white and frightened and utterly without suspicion, without even a trace of the old wariness that used to live behind her eyes whenever something about him didn't add up, he found he couldn't manage it.

"Three days," he admitted. "This makes four. The first one, I was working, lost maybe four minutes. Second one, walking home, lost twenty. Third one." He stopped, swallowed hard. "Third one I lost most of an afternoon. Woke up out by the old Voss property wall with a rock in my hand I don't remember picking up."

Priya's grip on his shoulders tightened. "Why didn't you tell me. Any of it. The very first time."

"Because I know how it sounds." The words came out harder than he meant them to, old shame surfacing fast. "Because the one person in this town everyone still half expects to relapse into something is standing here telling you he's been losing hours of his own life and can't explain why, and I didn't want that to be the first thing you thought when you looked at me. Not after everything it took to get you looking at me the way you do now."

"Dev." Priya's voice cracked, something in it that had nothing to do with suspicion and everything to do with pure, uncomplicated fear for him. "I wasn't going to think you'd relapsed. I was going to think you were sick, or hurt, or in danger, and I was going to want to help you. That's it. That's the whole thought. You don't get to make that decision for me anymore, deciding what I'm allowed to worry about on your behalf."

He looked at her, and something in his chest, some old, defensive posture he'd been carrying since long before his sentence, since maybe long before any of this, finally cracked under the weight of how plainly she meant it.

"I'm scared," he said, quiet, the admission costing him more than almost anything he'd said in the past year. "I don't know what's happening to me. It doesn't feel like anything I've heard of. It doesn't feel like a seizure, or a blood sugar crash, or anything a doctor's going to have a clean answer for. It feels like something's using my body while I'm not in it, and I don't know how to stop it, and I don't know who to ask."

Priya was quiet for a long moment, and Dev watched her mind visibly working, the same steady, procedural instinct that had carried her through every hard case since she'd pinned on a badge, now turned fully toward the one problem she couldn't approach with any procedure she'd ever been trained for.

"The Voss well," she said finally. "You said you woke up near there. With a stone in your hand."

"Yeah."

"Dev." Something cold moved across her face. "After everything that happened with Denise. After what we learned that place can do to people. You're telling me your body walked you there on its own, three separate times you can account for and God knows how many you can't, and picked something up."

Dev felt the same cold settle into his own chest, hearing it said out loud by someone else, someone whose instincts he'd learned, slowly and painfully, to trust more than his own. "I didn't think about it that way. I didn't want to think about it that way."

"We need to get you to Mara and Caleb tonight," Priya said, already standing, already pulling him up with her, the deputy and the sister moving as one single, decisive person for the first time since this had started. "And Wren, if she'll do it, though God knows I don't love asking her for another read after what the last one cost her. This isn't something we figure out over dinner and wine, Dev. Something's happening to you that's connected to what almost happened to this whole valley six weeks ago, and we don't have the luxury of waiting to see if it gets better on its own."

"And if it's what I think it might be." Dev's voice had gone very quiet. "If somehow, some way, whatever Denise carried for fifty years has found its way into me."

Priya didn't answer right away, and the silence between them stretched long enough that Dev understood, without her needing to say it, that she was thinking the exact same terrible thought he was.

"Then we deal with it," she said finally, steady despite everything Dev could see it cost her to sound that way. "The same way we dealt with everything else. Together, carefully, and a lot faster than we dealt with Denise, because I have a feeling, looking at you right now, that we don't have fifty years to figure this one out slowly."

She didn't let go of his arm the entire walk to her car, and Dev, shaken and frightened and grateful in equal measure, found himself thinking, even through the fear, that whatever was happening to him, at least it was happening to a version of himself his sister no longer had to decide, every single day, whether to trust.

That decision, it seemed, had already been made, quietly and completely, sometime during the year he hadn't been watching for it.
