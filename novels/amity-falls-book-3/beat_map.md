## The pitch

Adelaide's letter named Ambrose Whitlock as the one who taught her the bargain, not its origin. Book 3's reveal: the practice is also hereditary, passed through blood as well as teaching, and it skips generations unpredictably. Dev, Priya's brother, discovers through his and Priya's late mother's side that he descends from Whitlock. The hunger is waking in him, untrained and unwanted, and unlike Denise's fifty years of controlled, surgical harvesting, an unwilling carrier is unstable. Left alone it won't take a memory every few years, it will take everything from a large part of the valley at once, in one uncontrolled event, and it is killing Dev physically as it grows. A hidden faction who have quietly tracked Whitlock bloodlines for generations resurface, wanting the awakening to complete rather than be stopped. The valley has until the night of the autumn eclipse, six weeks out, to find a way to save Dev without repeating what almost happened to Denise or letting the faction get what they want.

**Confirmed 2026-09-14, "why now" mechanism:** their grandmother knew of the Whitlock connection and deliberately buried the name after a family tragedy, generations back, which is why no one alive remembers it. What actually wakes the dormant blood now, rather than leaving it dormant Dev's whole life the way it stayed dormant for his mother and grandmother, is proximity: Dev stood at the well through the entire redistribution ritual in Book 2's climax (ch.36-38), and that direct exposure to an active release event is what triggered something in him that would otherwise likely never have surfaced at all.

**Confirmed 2026-09-14, added twist:** the quiet family has a source inside the valley's own trusted circle, someone in or adjacent to the council, quietly feeding them information. Revealed partway through Act Two. This was seeded lightly and early so it landed as a real surprise rather than something the reader was obviously meant to guess. Resolved ch.20-24 (Elias Thorne, fear not malice). A SECOND leak is now confirmed live as of ch.26 (the orchard tree-line contact knew Dev's exact four-minute supervised window, information only five named people had) — investigation ongoing, not yet resolved as of ch.28.

**The ring mechanism, established ch.25:** Wren determines Dev's building taking is too large and too fast to run through a single receiver the way Denise's redistribution worked at the well; it would kill whoever stood at the end of it, possibly Dev too. The fix is distribution: nine to twelve volunteers standing in a ring around Dev, each absorbing a small enough piece that no one is broken by it, the same principle as grounding a lightning strike through many rods instead of one. Real, permanent cost confirmed likely for every ring member (a lasting mark, not the memories themselves, which redistribute normally) — Dev insists every candidate be told the full, undiluted truth before being asked, no softer framing. Mara is engineering an abort signal into the design, something Denise's version never had or needed. Odette has already volunteered and become candidate one. Four weeks of runway at ch.25, narrowing.

**Established color from Wren's ch.17 full read:** the quiet family calls itself simply "the family." They believe every past "merciful," scattered release (like Denise's) has only delayed the debt onto some future generation, never truly ended it, and they have approached other carriers across generations with the same offer, mostly refused, occasionally accepted, never yet successfully "closing" the debt everywhere the way their doctrine promises. Wren felt this pattern directly in the read (a woman decades ago who said yes, whose valley's version ended, while the debt simply resurfaced elsewhere later) — useful ammunition for the group's argument against completion in later confrontations (ch.30 especially).

**Tone clarification, confirmed 2026-09-14:** closed-door romance convention unchanged from Books 1-2. "Racy" is being delivered as tempo, dread, chapter-ending hooks, and earned emotional heat between existing characters, not open-door content or a new invented romantic thread.
