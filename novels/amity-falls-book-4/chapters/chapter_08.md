The manuscript sat on the kitchen table of the Reyes farmhouse, its pages loose in a manila folder that had seen three generations of orchard receipts. Morning light cut through the window above the sink, catching dust motes that drifted over Whitlock's cramped handwriting and Adelaide's steadier script.

Wren adjusted her glasses. "We start with the lineages. Six of them. Wardens, Menders, Callers, Weathers, Finders, Wicks. Each has a section in the original terms." She tapped the first page. "The manuscript doesn't list them in that order. Whitlock grouped them by what he called 'debt architecture' — which obligations feed into which."

Theo sat across from her, a legal pad open, a mechanical pencil clicking in his hand. He wore the same cardigan he'd worn yesterday, charcoal with a frayed cuff. "Debt architecture. That's his phrase?"

"Appears twelve times in the margins." Wren turned a page. "He thought of the bargain as a structure. Load-bearing walls. Redundancies. The lineages aren't separate — they're engineered to distribute weight."

Mara leaned against the doorframe, arms crossed. She still carried the engineer's posture, shoulders squared against invisible loads. "Which means if one fails, the others compensate. Until they can't."

"Exactly." Wren's finger traced a line of ink. "The Wardens hold the physical boundary. The Menders absorb sickness and injury. Callers —" she glanced at Priya, who stood by the stove with a mug of tea "— maintain the social contract. Truth-telling. Calm. The Weathers buffer flood and drought. Finders locate what's lost, including the bargain's own memory when it frays. And Wicks..." She paused. "The manuscript says 'carry the first light, that the debt may be seen.' Only two Wicks left in the valley. Odette's cousin and a boy in Millbrook who doesn't know what he is."

Caleb came in from the porch, setting a tray of coffee down beside Theo's pad. "The Wicks nearly died out in the thirties. Flu took three in one winter. The bargain didn't break, but the debt... shifted."

"Shifted how?" Theo didn't look up from his notes.

"Found its way to the Finders." Caleb pulled out a chair, sat heavily. "Dev's grandmother took a toll meant for a Wick. She didn't tell anyone. Not until the ringing in her ears wouldn't stop."

The kitchen went quiet. Dev's name hung in the air like smoke.

Priya set her mug down. "Which brings me to why I'm here." Her voice carried the flat calm of a traffic stop. "I'm not here as Dev's sister. I'm here as a Caller. Deputy. Procedural oversight."

Wren watched her. Priya's posture was deliberate — feet planted, hands visible, the small badge on her belt catching the light. "Oversight of what?"

"Of the sessions." Priya's gaze moved across each face. "We're transcribing a document that compels truth by its nature. Whitlock wrote it to be read. Adelaide wrote hers to be witnessed. If we read aloud, if we discuss — my lineage activates within five feet. I compel honesty. I pay for it after." She exhaled. "I need to be the one who decides when we read, when we pause, when we stop. Not because I want control. Because if I don't manage the radius, someone says something they didn't mean to say, and the cost comes out of my composure. Which means I can't do my job tomorrow. Which means the valley loses a deputy."

Theo's pencil stopped. "You're saying the manuscript itself is a compelled-truth vector."

"I'm saying Whitlock was a Caller. Adelaide was a Caller. They wrote with the expectation of being heard." Priya's jaw tightened. "The original terms were spoken aloud at each renewal. That's in the manuscript. 'The bargain lives in voice, not ink.' So yes. Reading it aloud triggers the lineage. Every time."

Mara pushed off the doorframe. "Then we don't read aloud. We transcribe silently. Annotate silently. We compare notes after."

"And when we need to resolve a discrepancy?" Theo asked. "When Wren reads 'the Finder pays in memory of the search' and I read 'the Finder surrenders the memory of finding' — those aren't the same obligation. We'll have to speak to sort it."

Priya nodded. "Then we speak in sequence. One voice at a time. I monitor. If I feel the pull —" she pressed two fingers to her temple "— I call a break. Five minutes. I recover. We resume. No exceptions."

Wren felt something shift in her chest. The precision of it. The care disguised as procedure. "You'd do that for us? Take the hit so we can work?"

"I'd do it because it's my job." Priya's mouth quirked, barely. "And because Dev would never forgive me if I let his best friend's wife get compelled into admitting she hates his pie crust."

A laugh broke from Caleb. "She does. Uses lard instead of butter. Told me once, swore me to secrecy."

"See?" Priya tilted her head. "Already working."

The transcribing began.

Wren read Whitlock's Warden section first, her eyes moving over lines she'd memorized in the week since the manuscript surfaced. *The Warden holds the boundary against what presses from outside. The cost is paid in the memory of what the boundary excludes — the flood that didn't come, the fire that turned, the sickness that passed the valley by. The Warden forgets the danger so the valley need not remember it.*

She wrote in the margin: *Obligation — boundary maintenance. Cost — memory of averted catastrophe. Lineage trait — shifting (physical form). Note: current Warden unknown. Last known: Ambrose Whitlock, role relinquished 1923.*

Theo's pencil scratched. He'd set up a parallel column: *Statutory language vs. operational reality. Whitlock describes the cost as 'memory of what the boundary excludes.' Adelaide's letter describes it as 'the Warden forgets the near-miss so the town sleeps.' Semantic difference: 'excludes' implies active rejection. 'Near-miss' implies passive survival. Implication for publication — which framing protects the valley?*

Mara read the Mender section, her brow furrowed. *The Mender draws the wound into themselves. The cost is paid in the memory of health — their own, or another's. The Mender forgets what wholeness feels like, that the healed may remember it.*

She wrote: *Cost structure ambiguous. 'Their own, or another's' — suggests toll redirection possible within Mender lineage. No recorded instances. Cross-reference with Dev's case (Finder toll redirection).*

Caleb took the Weather section. *The Weather tastes the storm before it breaks. The cost is paid in the memory of fair days. The Weather forgets sunshine, that the valley may keep it.*

His annotation was a single line: *My grandmother was a Weather. She never spoke of summer after 1962. I thought it was grief. It was the bargain.*

Wren's throat tightened. She looked at him — really looked — and saw the recalculation happening behind his eyes. Forty years of memory reframed in thirty seconds.

The Caller section came next. Priya read it herself, her face unchanging. *The Caller speaks the truth that binds. The cost is paid in the memory of silence. The Caller forgets peace, that the valley may keep its secrets.*

She wrote: *Cost = composure. Confirmed. 'Memory of silence' = operational definition of post-compulsion depletion. Range: five feet. Duration: immediate. Recovery: variable. Note — Caller cannot compel themselves. Requires external witness.*

"Five feet," Theo murmured. "That's specific."

"Measured," Priya said. "Ambrose Whitlock measured it. In 1919. He paced it out in the town hall. Three paces. Wrote it in the margin of his own copy."

Wren turned to the Finder section. Her section. *The Finder locates what is lost. The cost is paid in the memory of the search. The Finder forgets the path, that the found may remember the destination.*

Her hand hovered over the page. The words were familiar — she'd read them a dozen times — but today they sat different. Heavier.

*The Finder forgets the path.*

She wrote: *Obligation — location/recovery. Cost — memory of search process (not the finding, not the result). Critical distinction: the debt takes the journey, not the arrival. Lineage trait — sensing absence. Current Finders: Wren Castellano, Dev Nair, Mara Voss (née Kincaid). Note: toll redirection (paying with another's memory) is the valley's oldest taboo. Dev Nair redirected toll to Caleb Reyes (1998-2000). Consequence: Dev served sentence. Caleb's memory restored 2024 via split-weight ritual.*

Theo's pencil paused beside her annotation. "The journey, not the arrival. That's how you read it?"

"That's what it says." Wren kept her voice level. "'The memory of the search.' Not 'the memory of what was found.' The search is the work. The finding is the gift."

"And if the search takes years?"

"Then the Finder loses years." Simple. True. "That's the bargain."

Theo wrote something in his column. Wren didn't ask what.

The Wick section was last. Only three lines. *The Wick carries the first light. The cost is paid in the memory of darkness. The Wick forgets fear, that the valley may see.*

"Three lines," Mara said. "For a lineage that nearly died out."

"Whitlock wrote a note in the margin," Wren said. "'The Wicks hold the bargain's memory when all other lineages fail. They are the failsafe. We forgot to tell them.'"

The kitchen was silent again. Coffee went cold. The light shifted across the table.

Wren stretched her fingers, stiff from writing. "We have the lineage obligations annotated. Now we need the renewal terms. The schedule. The mechanism. The —"

Her phone buzzed on the table. Screen lit with a text from Yusuf.

*Regional paper just published an excerpt. Millbrook Chronicle. "Secret History of Amity Valley: Memory for Safety." Drake's leak. Two weeks until he drops the rest.*

The phone screen blurred. Wren read it again. Then a third time.

"Two weeks," she said aloud. The words didn't sound like hers. "He gave us a month yesterday."

Theo's phone buzzed. He glanced at it, face unchanging. "Same text. Yusuf sent it to the council list."

"Drake moved the deadline." Mara pushed off the doorframe, moving to the window. "He leaked an excerpt to force our hand."

"Or to prove he has more." Caleb stood, chair scraping. "The excerpt — what does it say?"

Wren pulled up the article on her phone. The mobile site loaded slow. Headline: *SECRET HISTORY OF AMITY VALLEY: MEMORY FOR SAFETY*. Byline: *Corwin Drake, Independent Researcher*.

She read fast, scrolling. *For generations, the residents of Amity Falls have traded memory for protection from flood, fire, and disease. This arrangement, known locally as 'the bargain,' is administered through six hereditary lineages. The cost of this protection is paid in stolen years — memories removed from designated lineage members without consent. Records obtained by this researcher document at least fifty years of covert renewals managed by a single individual...*

Her thumb stopped scrolling. "He names Denise."

"Does he name the split-weight ritual?" Theo asked.

"Not in the excerpt. Just — 'covert renewals managed by a single individual.' Implies Denise acted alone. Doesn't mention the ring. Doesn't mention Dev's heritage. Doesn't mention Whitlock's letter." Wren scrolled further. "He quotes the original terms. The Warden section. Verbatim. Which means he has the manuscript. Or a copy. Or photos."

Priya moved from the stove, close enough to read over Wren's shoulder. "He's framing it as exploitation. 'Stolen years.' 'Without consent.' He's not publishing the whole truth. He's publishing the version that looks worst."

"Because the version that looks worst gives him leverage." Theo's voice was flat. "If he publishes the complete record — the ritual, the ring, the fact that the valley *freed* Denise and *restored* Caleb — his leverage evaporates. The story becomes 'town solves its own problem.' He needs the problem to look unsolved."

"So he leaks the damning piece," Mara said. "Forces us to either give him the complete terms — which he'll bury or twist — or race him to publication with the full truth."

"Which is what we were already doing." Caleb's jaw was tight. "Just on a compressed timeline."

"Two weeks," Wren repeated. "To transcribe, annotate, verify, format, print, and distribute the complete true history. Before Drake drops his partial version."

Theo closed his legal pad. "We need to change how we're working. Parallel tracks. Mara and Caleb take the renewal schedule and mechanism sections. Priya and I take the lineage obligations — we'll cross-reference with Millbrook County records, see what matches. Wren —"

"I take the Whitlock and Adelaide letters." Wren's voice surprised her. Steady. "The narrative sections. The context. The why."

"And the Finder section." Theo looked at her. "You're the only one who can annotate it from inside."

Wren nodded once. "Okay. Parallel tracks. Two weeks."

Priya set a hand on the manuscript. "I'll stay here. Monitor the sessions. If we're all reading different sections simultaneously —"

"The radius," Wren realized. "Five feet. If we're all at this table —"

"Then I'm compromised in fifteen minutes." Priya's tone didn't waver. "I'll rotate. One pair at a time at the table. Others work in the living room, the porch, the orchard office. I'll move with the active pair."

"Your composure," Mara said quietly. "You'll burn through it in two days."

"Then I'll burn through it." Priya met her eyes. "Dev did eighteen months in a cell for redirecting a toll. I can handle two weeks of headaches."

The words landed. Wren felt them in her ribs.

"Priya —" she started.

"No." Priya cut her off, not unkindly. "No martyrdom conversations. We have a deadline. You need procedural oversight. I'm providing it. End of discussion."

Theo stood, gathering his pad. "Living room. Renewal schedule. Mara, Caleb — with me."

Mara moved without hesitation. Caleb followed, pausing to squeeze Wren's shoulder. "We'll have the mechanism mapped by tonight."

The kitchen emptied. Priya remained, leaning against the counter. Wren stayed seated, the manuscript pages spread before her like cards.

"Two weeks," Wren said again. Quieter this time.

"Two weeks," Priya agreed. She pulled out the chair beside Wren, sat. "Read the Finder section to me. Aloud. I'll monitor."

Wren's fingers found the page. *The Finder locates what is lost. The cost is paid in the memory of the search. The Finder forgets the path, that the found may remember the destination.*

She read it aloud. Her voice held steady.

Priya watched her face. Five feet between them. Close enough.

"Anything?" Wren asked when she finished.

Priya shook her head. "No pull. You read it like text. Not like truth."

"Because it *is* text. Whitlock wrote it. He wasn't a Finder."

"Ambrose Whitlock was the Warden of the first debt." Priya's voice softened fractionally. "He held the role. He knew every lineage's cost from inside. He wrote what he knew."

"Then why does it feel like he got the Finder cost wrong?"

Priya was quiet long enough that Wren looked up. "What do you mean?"

"The manuscript says 'the memory of the search.' But Dev —" Wren stopped. Swallowed. "Dev said the search isn't what the toll takes. He said it takes the memory of *who you were while searching*. The person you become on the path. Not the path itself."

Priya didn't flinch. "And you think Whitlock didn't know that?"

"I think Whitlock knew exactly that. And he wrote 'memory of the search' anyway. Because 'memory of who you were' sounds like identity theft. 'Memory of the search' sounds like... overhead. Administrative loss."

"A distinction that matters for publication."

"It matters for *truth*." Wren's hands tightened on the page edges. "If we publish 'memory of the search,' every Finder in the valley — every future Finder — reads that and thinks the cost is just... lost time. Lost footsteps. They don't know it takes the self you built while walking."

Priya studied her. The deputy's face, the Caller's stillness. "Then we annotate it correctly. In the published version. We add the note: *Operational reality: cost includes identity memory formed during search. See Dev Nair testimony, 2024.*"

"Will that be enough?"

"It has to be." Priya stood, rolling her shoulders. "We publish the truth. The whole truth. That's the job. Your job." She moved toward the door, paused. "Wren. The rupture you're worried about — the one where Theo's publishing instincts destroy the valley you love —"

Wren went cold. "I didn't say —"

"You didn't have to. It's in your face every time he talks about 'public interest' and 'complete disclosure.' " Priya's voice dropped, gentle now. "He's not Drake. He's not going to weaponize the truth. He's going to *witness* it. There's a difference."

"How do you know?"

"Because I've watched you two for months. Because I know what it looks like when someone loves a place enough to stay without every reason handed to them." Priya's eyes held hers. "That's your mother's theme. Mara's theme. It's becoming yours. And Theo — he's not trying to expose your home. He's trying to make sure it's *seen*. There's a difference."

The door clicked shut behind her.

Wren sat alone at the kitchen table. The manuscript pages rustled in a draft from the window. *The Finder forgets the path, that the found may remember the destination.*

She picked up her pen. Wrote in the margin: *Cost clarification — the search creates a self. The toll takes that self. The Finder forgets who they became while looking. This is not in the original terms. Whitlock omitted it. Adelaide omitted it. Dev Nair confirmed it. This annotation is the correction.*

Her handwriting was cramped. Pressed hard.

Outside, the orchard stretched toward the ridge line, rows of trees marching up the slope in the particular geometry Caleb had planted. Somewhere in those rows, Dev was walking the substation line, measuring evidence. Somewhere in Millbrook, Drake was checking his watch, counting down fourteen days.

And in the living room, Theo was reading the renewal schedule aloud to Mara and Caleb, his voice steady, Priya five feet away taking the cost so they could work.
