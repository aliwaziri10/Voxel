## 2026-09-21 addendum: countdown clock — RESOLVED, not a contradiction
Traced every deadline phrase ("thirty days," "four days," "two weeks,"
"ten days," "nine days," "six days," "fourteen days") to its exact chapter
via direct grep of all 45 chapter files (not the concatenated manuscript,
which lacks chapter markers). Finding: these are **four separate,
narratively-distinct deadlines**, each internally consistent within its
own thread, not one broken clock:

1. **Drake's opening ultimatum — "thirty days"** (ch4 only). Set once,
   restated 4x verbatim within the same scene/chapter. Never contradicted.
2. **The court's evidence-disclosure deadline — "four days"** (ch5, ch9,
   explicitly "the court gave us four days before the contents enter the
   public record"). Unrelated to Drake's ultimatum — a separate legal
   clock. Ch29 later confirms "we beat it by four days," consistent with
   this thread's endpoint.
3. **The council's accelerated publication schedule — "ten days" → "nine
   days"** (ch18 vote explicitly moves the schedule from "the original
   six-week schedule" to "ten days from today"; ch28/ch31 count down to
   "nine days" the next day, explicitly the same countdown one day later).
   Fully sequential, not contradictory.
4. **The final injunction-response deadline — "ten days" → "six days"**
   (ch31/ch37/ch38, explicitly Ellery's court schedule and the printer's
   final-files deadline, converging on "the Archive's opening was in six
   days" in ch38). Internally consistent.

No chapter text needs to change. Recommend one edit only, for a first-time
reader's clarity, not a contradiction fix: ch18's vote line already
explains the jump from "six-week schedule" to "ten days" — no further
action needed there either. **No fixes applied; audit item closed.**
