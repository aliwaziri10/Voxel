# ROMANCE BEATS - BATCH 7 (CH.31 TO 35) - Kindling Line Book 3

Written 2026-10-04. Replaces the one-line romance gestures for ch.31 to 35 in BEAT_MAP_BATCH7.md. Each romance beat is a FULL SCENE of at least 600 words, placed first, before any plot. The plot beats in BEAT_MAP_BATCH7.md stay as written and follow each scene. No new characters or places. No restaged first kiss, first "I love you" or first night. Counts allowed on the page only as BEAT_MAP_BATCH7.md says (Sol sixteen and a half all batch; Kael about thirty-four before the burn in ch.31 and about thirty-three after; stored seventeen; twelve heartbeats make a year). Do not add Sol's count to the stored sum and do not subtract one count from the other. No em dashes. The collar beat is ch.12 and ch.23 only. The stand-back echo is ch.1, 8, 31 and 43 only; in ch.31 it is Kael's failure and nobody says the words, and nobody says them in ch.32 to 35. In this batch Sol and Kael are apart for ch.32 to 34. In those three chapters the romance scene is the absent one: it is carried by what she left, what he says aloud to an empty room, and the stick and the counts. Ch.31 and ch.35 are the two chapters in the batch where they have a full scene together, and both must be warm, with humour as well as dread.
Format: SCENE | WHAT HAPPENS | WHAT CHANGES | HANDOVER.

## CH.31 | SOL | Day 29, the head of the cellar stair, at the door of the passage, before the descent
SCENE. A lantern on a hook, cold stone, cords coiled on the floor and running down into the dark. Odo and Tessa at the turn of the stair, busy with the cords and out of earshot; Joren and Corren at the passage. Mood: tight, tender, a little funny, because both of them are being very brave in front of an audience of two techs.
WHAT HAPPENS.
- Sol shakes out the rope, ties a bowline in the end and puts the loop in Kael's hand, as she did at the gallery water. Neither of them speaks of it. Odo takes the knot, tests it twice, and says it is good. Tessa says that is the nicest thing he has said in a month. Odo says he takes everything twice.
- Humour: Kael says he wishes to inspect the knot too. Sol says Odo has inspected it and so has the rope. He says he does not trust a knot he did not watch being tied. She says he watched it being tied; he has been watching her hands for a week. He says that is not an inspection, that is something else, and Tessa has to turn away to the cords.
- Friction: Kael asks, quietly, to be allowed to come down to the foot of the stair and not stay at the door. Sol says the plan has him at the door. He says it is a poor door. She says it is the door he promised. She says: "You wrote it." He says he did. She asks him to say what he will do, and he says he will be at the door and he will say it first if he wants to step in. She says that is all she asks, and does not say it twice.
- Touch: she wraps the loose end once more round his wrist, closes his fist on it with both her hands, and holds it there. He turns her fist over and presses his mouth to her knuckles. She breathes out through her nose, a short laugh, and says he is making it hard to be brave.
- Want: they say their counts aloud, as they did the night before. Sixteen and a half. About thirty-four. She says she will count the heartbeats aloud the whole time so that he knows she is there. He says he will count with her from the door. She goes down the stair without looking back and her back is straight, and his hand tightens on the rope.
WHAT CHANGES. The promise on the fifth page becomes spoken, out loud, in front of other people, and he has held it for the whole length of the scene. That is what the chapter then breaks.
HANDOVER. The foot of the stair, the plate, Tessa's count in the trough, the year burned, the shouting at the third heartbeat, Kael drops the rope and runs. Plot as in BEAT_MAP_BATCH7.md. Nobody says "stand back".

## CH.32 | KAEL | Day 29 night to Day 30, the room over the tar-shop, waking
SCENE. A grey square of window, a cold cup of tea on the table with the spoon still in it, the lamp burned out. Mood: quiet, raw and a little absurd, because the only thing in the room that shows she was there is a sock.
WHAT HAPPENS.
- Kael wakes with a wool sock on his numb left foot. He did not put it there. He lies and looks at it and does not move, as if movement would take it back. He reaches out for the other side of the bed; it is cold. There is a dent in the pillow. Her initials, I.V., are in the margin of the gauge-book where she signed it weeks ago.
- Humour: he says to the sock, aloud and in a very dry voice, that it is a competent sock. Joren, on the stool by the door, says he did not put it on. Kael says he assumed not. Joren says he would have used the other foot. Kael says that is an unfortunate thing to say to a man in bed.
- Friction: Kael tries to rise and the leg refuses, folds, and puts him back on the blanket. He swears under his breath, in a quiet furious voice that is mostly at himself. Joren says: "She said you would try to stand." Kael says she did not say that. Joren says she did, in fewer words. Kael says it is intolerable to be known that well by someone who is not in the room.
- Touch: he takes the sock between thumb and finger and holds the wool, the only thing of hers he can reach. He can feel nothing in the foot and he feels all of it in the hand. He says he can still see her hands doing it, and that he would rather have the sleep he lost than the sock he got.
- Want: he asks, very low, how long she has been gone. Joren says since the carrying-up, a night. He asks if she ate. Joren says he does not know and that Ansa will. Kael says to ask Ansa to carry a pot down to the foot of the steps. Joren says Ansa has already left with the pot. Kael closes his eyes, and the want is plain and not eased.
WHAT CHANGES. Kael learns what waiting is when he is the one who has been left, and that she left the sock as a message, not an apology.
HANDOVER. Joren says the message exactly as she asked him to. Yelva comes with the leg and the news about Mara. Kael finds the fifth page, reads the one line and puts it in his coat. He does not follow her and says aloud that he is not going. Plot as in BEAT_MAP_BATCH7.md.

## CH.33 | SOL | Day 30, the 211 steps down to the anchor chamber, then the last step
SCENE. Torch light on wet stone, the slow descent, a stick in her hand that is not hers. Mood: raw, funny in a crooked way, because she is talking to a walking stick, and alone.
WHAT HAPPENS.
- Sol carries Kael's stick down the 211 steps. Halfway she begins counting aloud, as they did the first time they came down. At one hundred and seventeen she says a number that is not a number, in his voice, and then says it is a bad impression and he is a bad liar. The stair gives the echo back and she nearly laughs and then does not.
- Humour: she complains to the stick that it is too tall for her, that it is a very good stick, that it has opinions, and that she holds the wrong end of it. She tries the grip and finds it is worn smooth where his hand sits and her hand does not fit. She says to the stick that she does not recommend being a stick.
- Friction: she argues with the empty chamber, then with herself. She puts the stick against the wall and says his name, then says it again to hear how it sounds with nobody to answer. She says his count, about thirty-three, aloud, flatly, the way he said it once, as a figure. She cannot make the number sound like a figure. She says it again and it is worse.
- Touch: she leans her hand flat on the cold wall by the stick, as she would lean on his arm, and finds she has left a mark in the wet. She does not touch the anchor plate and does not go near it. She sits on the last step facing it, the way she sat as a child in her mother's records room.
- Want: she says to the room that she would give a year to have him here and that is the very thing she must not say, and says it anyway, aloud, to the one person in the world who would not take her at her word.
WHAT CHANGES. The counting, which began as a way to stay near, becomes a way to speak to him when he is not there. It is the first time she has used the habit as a message instead of a comfort.
HANDOVER. The confession aloud on the last step, the order lapsing above her, Ansa's pot at the top of the steps, no one there. Plot as in BEAT_MAP_BATCH7.md. The anchor plate is not touched and not burned.

## CH.34 | KAEL | Day 31, the room over the tar-shop, before light
SCENE. Dark, a lamp not yet lit, the stick leaning on the wall where he can reach it. Mood: stripped and a little comic, because he is a man who documents everything, talking to a room.
WHAT HAPPENS.
- Kael says both counts aloud to the empty room, sixteen and a half and about thirty-three, the way they say them at night, and waits as if she might answer. The lane outside is quiet. He says that thirty-three is a figure and not a complaint, and then that this is exactly what a man says when it is a complaint.
- Humour: he addresses the stick as the one witness. He says the stick has seen him at his worst and will say nothing in court. He notices he has been talking to a stick, then to a sock, then to a window, and says in a flat voice that this is how the Ashworths end. The window does not reply.
- Friction: he moves his hand to take the stick, then puts it down again, then takes it. He tells himself aloud that he is not going to the anchor, that she asked, and that his not going is the only thing he can do for her this morning. He says it twice, and the second time he sounds like a man reading a ruling against himself.
- Touch: he presses his thumb to her initials in the margin of the gauge-book, I.V., and holds it there until the ink warms. He puts his palm flat on her side of the bed. He says her name once, quietly, not as a count.
- Want: he says to the empty room that he does not need her to come back tonight but that he would like it known that the bed is the wrong size. A rooster or a bell is not needed; the lane is silent. Light comes in a long grey bar across the boards.
WHAT CHANGES. The habit of saying it aloud is no longer only a rule he keeps for her; it is the only way he can speak to her while he waits. It makes the empty room bearable and does not make it less empty.
HANDOVER. The yard gate, the pick at the gate, Joren and Corren hold, Kael says "Joren and Corren" aloud. Kael at the door of the passage with his feet on his side of the threshold. Plot as in BEAT_MAP_BATCH7.md. Nobody says "stand back".

## CH.35 | SOL | Day 32, the top of the 211 steps at first light, then the room, then the end of the night
SCENE. Cold air at the mouth of the stair, first light, a stick in her hand, a man standing at the top who has come as far as he said he would and no farther. Mood: shy, wary, aching, with real warmth under the shame.
WHAT HAPPENS.
- Sol comes up out of the dark holding the stick out in front of her like a flag. Kael is at the top of the steps already; he came up from the lane and went no farther, because Joren told him she would be coming and because he had promised to wait where he was asked. He takes the stick. For a long moment neither of them speaks first.
- Humour: he says she carried it very well. She says it is a heavy stick and a good one and she has told it so. He says he has been talking to a window. She says she has been talking to a stick. They look at each other and it is the first almost-laugh in three days.
- Friction: he starts to step toward her and stops himself with his weight half forward. She sees it and says nothing, and he sees her see it. He says he is keeping to his side of the step. She says the step is wide enough for both and she would like him to come to her when she asks, not before. He says he knows and that it is a very small distance to be so hard.
- Touch: she puts her palm flat on his chest and keeps it there; she can feel his heart going too fast. He puts his hand over hers and does not press. She says: "Slower." He breathes. He does not say yes before arguing and she does not ask him anything; he only breathes slower, and she feels it under her hand.
- Want: she says she has a great deal to say and not here. He says the room is the right place. They walk down the lane side by side, a hand apart, not touching, and then her fingers find his sleeve and take hold.
AND AT THE END OF THE CHAPTER (after the plain talk in the room, as in BEAT_MAP_BATCH7.md). They lie down together, the door closed. Humour: she says the sock is a very good sock and he says he has had good reason to be grateful for it. Friction: none; they have said the hard things and are tired. Touch: she lies with her back to his chest and his arm over her, and says his count under her breath, thirty-three, and he says hers, sixteen and a half, and neither adds nor subtracts. Want: she asks if they can have the whole hour tomorrow that she asked for before. He says he has it in his coat. No first night restaged; no love scene; closeness and sleep.
WHAT CHANGES. The shame has been said aloud and not forgiven by anyone; they are close anyway, and the plan is still unfinished.
HANDOVER. Ansa at the top of the steps with an empty pot: "Good." Sol, to Kael, going up the lane: "Renn first." Plot as in BEAT_MAP_BATCH7.md.
