## CH.11 | SOL | Day 9, the passage to the third gallery
SCENE. A narrow dry passage, lantern light, Ulla ahead and Corren behind. Mood: wry, then softer.
WHAT HAPPENS.
- Kael carries Ulla's lantern behind Sol so her hands stay free. Humour: he says he has been promoted to furniture. Ulla says furniture is more useful than most husbands. Kael says he will take the compliment.
- Sol notices he walks where she can hear the stick. She says nothing about it. He says: "I am keeping to the left so you know where I am." She says: "I know exactly where you are." He says: "Good."
- Friction: she wants to lead the way; he wants her to wait for Corren's scouts. They settle it in four words each. Ulla says it is like watching two people tie a knot from opposite ends.
- Touch: his hand at the small of her back at the one narrow turn, guiding without steering. She leans back into it for half a step.
- Want: a short silence. The lantern light moves on the wall. She thinks of the word "later" and does not say it, because Ulla is a pace ahead and listening.
WHAT CHANGES. Sol lets him walk behind her and finds it is not the same as being pushed.
HANDOVER. The sealed third gallery door with two peg-holes, tapping behind it in elevens. Plot as in BEAT_MAP.md.

## CH.12 | KAEL | Day 10, the room over the tar-shop (POLITICAL SCENE 2 OF 3 follows)
SCENE. Morning before Malrik arrives at the door. Ansa's pot on the table, Kael at the window. Mood: tense, affectionate.
WHAT HAPPENS.
- Sol straightens his collar and says she will not come inside. She will wait at the door and he can leave when he wants. He says he would like her inside. She says: "I know. I am not furniture either." He laughs.
- Friction: he says Malrik will use her presence as a weight. She says that is why she will stay outside; if she is in the room Malrik will speak to her and not to him. He sees she is right and says so.
- Touch: she stays holding the collar a moment, thumb at his jaw. He turns his head into it. Neither speaks.
- Humour: Ansa passes the doorway and says something about people who stand in doorways all morning. They both pretend not to hear.
- Want: Sol says: "Later." Kael says: "Hold me to it." It is the third time and neither of them has had it yet.
WHAT CHANGES. She chooses where to stand and tells him why. He agrees without arguing.
HANDOVER. Malrik at the tar-shop door; the concession; the custodian's claim; "My works steward has not been at the house since the third day." Plot as in BEAT_MAP.md (political scene under half the chapter). Ledger addition: Malrik confirms one line that the awning came down and wages were paid to the day (Ansa repeats it in ch.3).

## CH.13 | SOL | Day 11, the walk to Corwin's forge and the forge itself
SCENE. A grey lane of smithies, hot metal smell, noise from every door. Mood: light, the first real lightness of the book.
WHAT HAPPENS.
- Sol laughs for the first time in the book, properly, out loud, at Kael trying to be polite to Corwin, a gruff guild master with burn scars to the elbow, in his own cluttered shop. Kael bows. Corwin stares. Kael says good morning twice.
- Humour: Corwin asks if the Auditor is ill. Sol says he is always like this. Kael says he is not. Sol says: "Say good morning a third time." He does not.
- Friction: Kael is annoyed at being laughed at and says so. Sol says she is not laughing at him; she is laughing because it is the first thing in days that did not hurt. He accepts that.
- Touch: there is soot on his cheek from the doorframe. She wipes it with her thumb and he lets her, in front of Corwin, who pretends to examine a file.
- Want: she thinks of the clasp and the word "copper" and the quiet in her chest comes back; she lets it come back and does not hide it from him. He sees.
WHAT CHANGES. Sol lets herself be glad in the middle of the worst of it and he lets her.
HANDOVER. Corwin's account: the old mold was his master's, the commission was for a woman who paid in copper; the pegs close a line, two hands, one breath; "She said never let anyone stand close when they come out." Plot as in BEAT_MAP.md. Corwin is closed after this chapter (THREAD_LEDGER row 14).

## CH.14 | KAEL | Day 12, the stair to the third gallery
SCENE. The stair down to the third gallery, damp, one lantern. Mood: honest, a little bleak.
WHAT HAPPENS.
- On the stair Kael's leg fails. He lets Sol take his weight for four steps and does not apologise. She says nothing. At the fifth step he says: "I am not going to say sorry." She says: "Good."
- Humour (dark): he says if he must fall down a stair he would like it to be one with a rail. She says that is the first reasonable thing he has said all day.
- Friction: Kael says aloud that he will stand where he cannot pay, out of the room's reach, on purpose, when she opens the plate. She says it back to him to be sure he has not hidden anything in the sentence. He says: "I am not hiding anything." She says: "I am checking."
- Touch: she puts his hand on the rail and her hand on top. The rail is cold. Her hand is warm.
- Want: she says: "Come back to me." It is not romantic. It is an order. He hears the order and the romance inside it and answers both.
WHAT CHANGES. He says out loud, before the burn, exactly where he will stand and why. This is the habit growing.
HANDOVER. Sol burns half a year at the third-gallery plate; Kael stands out of reach; they hear Renn tap eleven and a twelfth; Renn is found with a cracked wrist; "She locked it behind me. She was kind about it." Plot as in BEAT_MAP.md.

## CH.15 | SOL | Day 13, the stair outside Yelva's room
SCENE. The stair outside Renn's recovery room, evening. Ulla inside with Renn. Mood: spent, soft, very little talk.
WHAT HAPPENS.
- Sol and Kael sit on the stair with their shoulders touching. For a long time nobody says anything. Ulla's voice comes through the door scolding Renn for walking on his wrist. Humour: Kael says Ulla could run a Council. Sol says she would be more honest.
- Friction (small): Sol says she wants to be the one to tell Mara that Renn is found. Kael says Mara will want to hear it from Joren. Sol says: "Then I will tell Joren." Kael says: "You are very good at telling people things." She says: "I am learning from someone."
- Touch: Kael takes a heel of bread from his coat and hands it to her without looking. She eats it. He has been carrying bread for her for three days.
- Want: she says the numbers have turned into a person. She means Renn. She also means Kael. He says he knows. They do not say more.
WHAT CHANGES. Sol says out loud that the numbers have become people, and Kael understands she means both of them.
HANDOVER. Renn's account (the chamber, the carried tally, being locked in by a woman who was kind, "the gauge reads seventeen and it was only fourteen when I came down"). Ledger addition: Renn takes the sealed shutting page from inside his coat and gives it to Sol unopened. Plot as in BEAT_MAP.md.
