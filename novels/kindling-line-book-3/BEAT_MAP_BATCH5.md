## CH.21 | SOL | Day 19
SETTING. The stair head of the Lower Spine anchor, then the 211 steps, then the round chamber [V B2 ch.4]. The chamber is "the size of a small church"; a dark stone slab "as long as a barge" lies in a pit with veins of old silver; the plate stands at the north end in a shallow socket: black iron, a hand's breadth thick, wide as a door, scorch fanning out "like a scorched hand" [V B2 ch.4]. The upper stair was shut by Thorne "since the accident"; Malrik's concession in ch.12 opened it [PROPOSED link].
CAST (only these). Sol (Isolde Vane, undyed coat). Kael (square hand, stick, numb left leg). The Thorne house steward at the stair head: narrow man in Thorne grey, violet cuffs, "a face designed for not being remembered" [V B2 ch.4]. He is NOT the works steward. He speaks at most two lines, opens the visitors' book without comment, and stays at the top. No one else.
CARRIED. Sol brings the LEAF and Renn's sheet. The diagram in oilcloth is still behind the facing stone at the base of the east wall [V B2 ch.4].
ROMANCE FIRST. On the stair Kael counts the steps under his breath, as Odo did [V B2 ch.4]. At forty Sol joins the count without being asked. They count the rest together. (Their counting habit is the beat. No touching needed.)
PLOT. At the foot Sol says she wants to go in alone and Kael says he will sit on the last step. This is a plain courtesy, not the stand-back echo: no burn, no cost, different words. It is the first time Sol has stood in this room; in B2 ch.3 and 4 she took the register desk while Kael went down [V]. She walks to the plate and does NOT touch it. She says her count aloud to it, sixteen and a half, and says her mother's carried year is on the leaf. She lays the leaf's row for her mother beside Renn's sheet: the carried difference is one year, in the second, darker hand that leans right [PROPOSED amount; Canon says a year or two]. She takes the diagram from behind the facing stone and sets it beside Renn's sheet. The thin dotted line from the anchor's south edge runs east toward the cliff face and the Lower Reach [V B2 ch.4]. Renn's sheet marks the boiling-house cellar "warm" [V ch.3 of this book]. The dotted line ends where the warm mark sits [PROPOSED]. Sol sees the anchor and the second line are one system.
MECHANICS. The plate is not touched and not explained beyond B2: ward-taker sits at the plate, cost goes down through her hands [V B2 ch.4]. Her mother sat here thirteen years ago, walked out on her own feet and never fully walked back into her body [V B2 ch.4]; she died at forty-two, three winters after her Reckoning [V HANDOFF]. The stored years are NOT traceable to their owners (Canon section 5). Sol only knows from the leaf that a year was carried off her mother's row. She cannot know it is in the seventeen. She decides to count it as hers anyway, and the text says it is a decision, not proof.
LOCKED. No burn. No demonstration of stored years (ch.8 only). Do not name Sol's mother. Do not state the binding year (B2 snag 4). The steward is not named or hinted at.
INTERIOR. Sol wants to be angry with her mother for the contract and cannot find the anger. She finds the count instead.
STAKES. If the anchor and the second line are one system, the stored years run a road from this plate to the boiling house, and whoever closes the east line is closing her mother's plate's far end.
SUB-BEATS. Relationship (the shared count); interior (no anger found); world (the dotted line lands on the warm mark); callback.
CALLBACKS. B2 ch.4 (211 steps, plate, diagram); B1 ch.45 (the leaf); ch.3 and ch.7 of this book (Renn's sheet, the leaf).
DATE. Day 19.
HOOK. Sol, climbing, realizes a share of the seventeen may be her mother's, and says nothing to Kael on the stair until the top. At the top she says it in one sentence.

## CH.22 | KAEL | Day 20
SETTING. The room over the tar-shop, evening [V B2 ch.12, 44]. No other location.
CAST (only these). Kael (square hand, stick). Sol. Ansa may knock once and leave a pot at the door with one line of dialogue (old splicer, forty years). No one else.
ROMANCE FIRST. Sol sits on the floor with her back against Kael's shins while he writes, so her weight rests on the good leg's side. She eats Ansa's soup from the pot and gives him half the spoon-work. Warm, domestic, not erotic. (Fresh beat; ch.17's night was the tender one.)
PLOT. Kael does the arithmetic on the back of a gauge-book page, in his square hand, with her initials still in the margin from B2 ch.2: Sol sixteen and a half; himself about thirty-two; the gap fifteen and a half. He says the gap aloud. He offers to leave the Reach for a winter if the gap is what is hurting her. Sol answers with the rule, plainly: when no one stands near, she pays all of it; when he stands near, he pays for her; his leaving is only her paying. She refuses. She says she wants him where she can count him. He stops writing.
Kael also thinks about the seventeen and refuses to finish the addition in his head. The text must NOT write the sum. He sees how neat it would be and hates that it is neat.
MECHANICS. Cost rule: the nearest living body pays most; a fraction stays with the wielder (B1 ch.3, ch.14). Transfer is always involuntary. No chosen buffer. No ward-binding.
LOCKED. No stored-years demonstration. No political scene. The custodian's pick is named on Day 26; Kael states that date once, from the order [PROPOSED fixed date].
INTERIOR. Kael realizes his offer was an attempt to decide for her what she could bear, which is the old habit. He says so before she can. (Callback to the rule "tell me first, every time", B2 ch.1.)
STAKES. The pick will stand where the custodian says. Whoever stands nearest the line takes the stored years.
SUB-BEATS. Plot (arithmetic); relationship (refusal); interior (the old habit).
CALLBACKS. B1 ch.44 (the count, "grow old together", about thirty to thirty-five years for Kael); B2 ch.1 (tell me first); ch.18 of this book (the quarrel about standing near).
DATE. Day 20.
HOOK. Sol, after a pause: "Then we say it out loud, where they can hear." Kael does not ask who "they" are.

## CH.23 | SOL | Day 21 | MIDPOINT | THE PUBLIC STEP
SETTING. The mouth of the gallery under the burned ropewalk, where Ansa handed out bread after the tap closed [V B2 ch.45]. Dusk. The fourth roof from the corner (Ansa's) is above them [V B2 ch.45].
CAST (only these). Sol, Kael (stick, because of the steps down), Ansa, Tam (Ansa's nephew), Ulla, Renn (cracked wrist, standing by Ulla), Corren and Joren (scar across the back of one hand), Odo Marl, Tessa Rook, and the households of the Lower Reach (unnamed crowd, no speaking parts). Bram stands at the back in his grey ribbon. Yelva stays with Mara and is NOT present. Mara is NOT present. Aldous and Malrik are NOT present. No custodian people.
ROMANCE FIRST. Before they go down to the crowd, Sol fixes Kael's collar and he lets her, then he fixes hers. Neither speaks. (Echo of ch.12 collar beat is deliberate and small; do not extend it.)
PLOT. Sol speaks first, plain, to the Lower Reach: "I am Isolde Vane. I have sixteen and a half years left. I name Kael Ashworth, who has about thirty-two, and who I want near me." Kael: "I am Kael Ashworth. I have about thirty-two. I name Isolde Vane." Sol adds one sentence: this changes nothing about who pays; she wants it said anyway. Silence. Ansa says "Eat." That is the Reach's whole answer. No ritual word, no custom, no handfast (UNSET, must not be invented). Joren and Corren are named by Kael as present, as the rule requires [V]. Odo takes a reading of the east gauge after, out of habit, and it reads seventeen, unchanged. The step moves nothing in the mechanism.
PRIVATE SCENE AFTER. The room over the tar-shop. High heat tempo, written to the door and cut away at the same point ch.17 cut; no explicit content. The register is different from ch.17: loud with relief, shaking, laughing, Sol letting him see she was afraid of being seen. (Different emotional resolution from ch.17: avoid sleep, avoid tender wordlessness.)
MECHANICS. It is NOT a ward-binding and changes nothing about who pays. The text says so once, in Sol's line, and never again.
LOCKED. Public step is ch.23 only. No restaged first kiss or first night. No political scene. The custodian is not on the page.
INTERIOR. Sol notices that saying the count to strangers made it smaller and truer, not larger.
STAKES. Saying it aloud tells the Reach, and anyone who asks the Reach, who the nearest body to Sol would be and what each is worth.
SUB-BEATS. Plot beat (the step); relationship (public naming); interior (smaller and truer); world (exposure).
CALLBACKS. B2 ch.45 (the gallery mouth, Ansa's bread, "Forty households. Fifty."); ch.1 of this book (the count aloud for the first time); ch.22 (their agreement to say it).
DATE. Day 21.
HOOK. Bram, at the back, writes two numbers on a slip and does not hide it. He folds it and leaves toward the Council road.

## CH.24 | KAEL | Day 22
SETTING. The room over the tar-shop, morning; then the east gauge hut at the boiling-house yard [V ch.17 of this book].
CAST (only these). Kael, Sol, Odo Marl (fifty, broad, says little, takes every reading twice), Tessa Rook (twenty-three, quick, chalk-line at her belt), Tam (carries the stick up from the door once). No one else.
ROMANCE FIRST. Sol, over tea: "You hedged in front of the whole Reach." Kael: "I do not promise what I cannot measure. About thirty-two." She laughs. They are easy with each other the morning after; nothing is explained.
PLOT. Kael walks the level lane to the yard without the stick and forgets it; Tam jogs after him with it and says nothing. On level ground the left leg now holds. The numbness is changing to pins. Kael is not triumphant; he is frightened to trust it. At the hut Odo lays out the closing plan: two pegs, one breath, cords tied to the pegs and run out through the door, two pullers at the far end of the passage beyond the room's reach, pulling together on the count in the trough of the cycle. Sol sits at the plate. Where the pegs sit is NOT stated here: it waits for Renn's map in ch.38. Writer must not describe sockets, rods, rig hardware or distances. Odo says only that the pullers are Tessa and himself. Kael says nothing. Tessa shows the gauge still reads seventeen and does not fall at night.
MECHANICS. Closing needs both twin pegs pulled in one breath, by rope rig from outside the room [Canon section 5]. The cycle is eleven with a twelfth added. When the second line closes, the stored sum goes to the nearest living body [Canon]. Nobody says at this point who that would be.
LOCKED. Do not state who pulls other than Odo and Tessa. Do not decide the peg sockets. Kael does not volunteer to pull yet (that is ch.26).
INTERIOR. Kael hears himself think that pulling would be a way to be near the fight without being near her, and does not say it yet.
STAKES. The plan has a person standing at the plate and nobody else near it. That person is Sol.
SUB-BEATS. Relationship (teasing, ease); plot (the plan); interior (the leg, the unsaid wish to pull).
CALLBACKS. ch.21 and ch.22 (the count, the gap); ch.8 and ch.9 (the gauge fixed at seventeen); B2 ch.4 (Odo's two readings).
DATE. Day 22.
HOOK. Tessa, softly: "Whoever stands at the plate is nearest. That is not a choice, that is where the line is."

## CH.25 | SOL | Day 23
SETTING. The Thorne house, up the wide shallow stair with a lamp under violet glass at every fourth step [V B2 ch.18], to the still-room, and the small room beside it that is the works steward's [PROPOSED; B2 ch.15 to 31 never shows her room]. Morning.
CAST (only these). Sol, Aldous Thorne (about twenty-five, his grandfather's height, none of his ease [V B2 ch.18]; his letters lean right [V B2 ch.42]). Malrik Thorne is in the house but does not appear. The Thorne house steward (narrow man, violet cuffs) may open the front door and say nothing. The works steward does NOT appear and is NOT named.
ROMANCE FIRST. At the tar-shop door Kael tells her where he will be (Odo's hut) before she asks, and she tells him where she is going before he asks. It is "tell me now, and tell me first, every time" [V B2 ch.1], inverted into ease. He gives her the lantern; she does not need it by day and takes it anyway.
PLOT. Aldous sent word that the works steward's room, which was bare when he looked on Day 18, is not bare now. In the small room beside the still-room: the bed made, the table cleared, and on the table on a folded cloth a copper clasp, three strands knotted, in order drop, feather, flame. It was not there five days ago, so she was in the house in the night. Under it a note in a right-leaning hand: Sol sees the lean and the matching lean on the leaf, and says nothing aloud. The note reads, in one sentence: she will come to Lady Vane, not to the house and not to the Council. Aldous asks nothing and offers nothing. Sol takes the clasp and the note; Aldous agrees without argument. No search of the house, no questioning of the household, no confrontation, no accusation.
MECHANICS. none new.
LOCKED. The steward as the carrying hand is ch.27 only. This chapter is not an investigation. The inked-out name in the primer is NOT resolved. The clasp is described exactly: copper, three strands knotted, in order drop, feather, flame [Canon section 2].
INTERIOR. Sol discovers she wants the woman allowed to say it, whatever it is, before anyone decides what she is.
STAKES. If the steward comes only to Sol, nobody else controls what is said; if she is taken first, the account is lost.
SUB-BEATS. Relationship (the inverted rule); plot (the clasp and note); interior (wanting her heard).
CALLBACKS. B2 ch.15 (the clasp at her throat); B2 ch.9, 11, 16 (the hooded watcher with the clasp); ch.16 of this book (Mara: "I would know it again"); ch.20 (the primer).
DATE. Day 23.
HOOK. Sol reads the note a second time on the stair going down and understands it is dated: the writer wrote "tonight". She goes home to wait.
