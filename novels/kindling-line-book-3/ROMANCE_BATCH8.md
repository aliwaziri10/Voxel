## CH.36 | KAEL | Day 33, the room over the tar-shop, morning
SCENE. Grey light, a pot of Ansa's tea going cold, a table too small for two. Mood: playful and slightly absurd, then suddenly tender, because they are practising the thing that nearly broke them.
WHAT HAPPENS.
- Sol says that if she is going to ask him aloud and wait, she had better practise on small things. She asks him to pass the cup. He passes it at once. She says no, he has to wait until she has finished asking and then count three before he answers. He says he did wait. She says he handed it to her while she was still on the word "cup".
- Humour: they try it again with the salt, the spoon, a stool, the window. He waits so carefully for the third heartbeat that he stops breathing. She says he is not meant to be turned to stone, only to be a person who answers. He says that is a great deal to ask of a man in a coat. She says to take the coat off, then.
- Friction: she asks, in the same light voice, whether he will let her carry the lamp down the second stair when they go to see it. His body wants to answer before she has finished. He waits for the third heartbeat. Then he says yes and that he would prefer to carry it himself and that he is saying so aloud because she asked him to. She says that is the exact right answer and that it is also a very small lie. He says it is a very small truth with a lie standing next to it.
- Touch: she takes both his hands in hers across the table and says the count with him, one, two, three, as one might teach a child to breathe. He turns her hands over and presses his mouth to the inside of one wrist. She says that is not part of the exercise. He says he is adding a clause.
- Want: she says she would like to be asked a small thing by him, aloud, with a wait after, and that she will not hurry the answer. He asks if she will pass the tea. She says yes after the third heartbeat, and he finds he has been waiting for it with his whole body.
WHAT CHANGES. The rule stops being a thing they argue about and becomes a game they can both win, with the thing at stake sitting quietly in the room.
HANDOVER. Ulla at the door with Renn, Sol asking him aloud and plainly to draw the room, Joren with Malrik's note. Plot as in BEAT_MAP_BATCH8.md.

## CH.37 | SOL | Day 34, the wall of the boiling-house yard, afternoon, and then the room at night
SCENE. A low wall in the late sun, a heel of Ansa's bread, Odo and Tessa busy and loud with the cords across the yard. Mood: easy, sun-warm, a little cheeky, with a thread of the coming days running underneath.
WHAT HAPPENS.
- They sit on the wall with the bread between them. Kael breaks it and gives her the larger half. Sol takes the smaller piece out of his hand and puts the larger one back in it. He says it is the same bread. She says it is not the same half. He says that is not a good argument. She says it is an excellent argument and that he is doing it again.
- Humour: Tessa, across the yard, calls out that if they are going to argue about bread she would like a ruling, since she keeps the chalk-line and can measure it. Odo says in a deep voice that a ruling takes two readings. Sol says to measure each piece twice and report. They do, and the pieces are equal, to the crumb, and Kael looks as if he has been cheated.
- Friction: she says that giving her the bigger half is not a small thing in him; it is the same hand that stepped in. He says it is bread. She says it is not bread, it is the whole of him in the shape of bread, and she would like to eat what she is given and to be asked. He goes quiet and eats his own half slowly, and says that she is right and that it is very good bread.
- Touch: she brushes a crumb from his sleeve and leaves her hand there. He puts his own hand over hers on the stone of the wall and neither of them moves. The sun is warm on her face. She leans, a little, against his shoulder.
- Want: she says she would like very much to be an ordinary woman on a wall in the sun, eating bread, for one afternoon, and not at the plate. He says it is the afternoon and she is on the wall. She says she knows and that it is the best thing she has been given all month.
WHAT CHANGES. She stops watching his habit as a danger and sees it as a love that has not yet learned another shape. She says so, lightly, and he says he is learning, and they leave it there.
HANDOVER. The cords tested again in the yard, Tessa asking that the count be Sol's, and at night in the room the gap said aloud. The private scene after the plain talk is written to the door and cut away; no first night restaged; closeness, then sleep. Plot as in BEAT_MAP_BATCH8.md.

## CH.38 | KAEL | Day 35, the room over the tar-shop, morning, before Renn's map
SCENE. A bare floor in a small room, early light, no table. Mood: tender and funny, the way children play a game that is really about something else.
WHAT HAPPENS.
- Sol says she wants to know what a map will feel like before she sees one. She sits on the floor with her back against the wall and her hands on her knees, in the exact space of one person, and says this is the plate. She tells Kael to sit where the map would put him. He sits against the far wall, too far, and says this is the door. She says that is much too far and that he looks like a man waiting for a ferry.
- Humour: he scoots closer in small, solemn increments, stopping at each one to ask if this is the right distance. She says no, no, no, and then very seriously, no. At the last one she says that is the other kind of far. He asks which kind that is. She says the kind where he is almost near enough to be nearest, and the kind he is not allowed.
- Friction: he says he will stop where the map says and not a step nearer. She says she knows, and that she wants him to say it with his body now, so he can feel where the line is. He stops at the third step of the floor and finds that it is hard in a way he did not expect, like holding a hand over a candle. She watches him do it and does not help him.
- Touch: when he has held it long enough she crawls across the boards to him, puts her head on his knee and says: "Now nobody is nearer." He rests his hand on her hair. They stay that way a long time, in the quiet, in the early light.
- Want: he says he would like to be the person who sits in the right place because he has been told, not because he has been made. She says that is the only reason it counts.
WHAT CHANGES. Standing where a map says becomes something they have done together first, on a floor, in play, before it has to be done in the dark with the line awake.
HANDOVER. Renn's page on the table, the sockets, the nearest-body test, the walk down to the turn of the stair. Plot as in BEAT_MAP_BATCH8.md. The line "Now nobody is nearer" is used once in this scene and Renn's hook says it in another form.

## CH.39 | SOL | Day 36, the wall of the boiling-house yard at dusk, waiting for Mara
SCENE. Long shadows, a smell of tar and cold stone, the Thorne guard standing in line at the far gate. Mood: quiet, warm, a little tired, with the whole of the evening held in it.
WHAT HAPPENS.
- They sit on the yard wall to wait for Mara. Kael leans back and Sol finds tar-dust in his hair, and says so, and he says it lives there now. She sits behind him with her knees either side of him and picks the dust out with her fingers, one strand at a time. He says it is the first time anyone has looked for anything in his hair that was not grey. She says there is plenty of grey, she is sorting it.
- Humour: she says the grey is coming in handsomely on the left. He says it is the left side of his head that has been listening to her. She says that is not how listening works. He says it is how he works.
- Friction: she says she would like him to be very honest with her about one thing: whether he wants to be at the door on the last night, or whether he is only agreeing because it is his place. He is quiet for a moment, and then says he does not want to be at the door, he wants to be at the plate, and that he knows exactly why he cannot, and that the door is the thing he will do. She says that is the true answer and that it is also a terrible one, and she lays her cheek on the back of his neck.
- Touch: her arms round his shoulders, her chin on his head, his hands over her wrists. The yard darkens. Somewhere a bell, or the gate, rings once and does not repeat.
- Want: she says she would like to spend the whole next season combing things out of his hair. He says he would like to spend it letting her. Then there is a footstep on the stones, and neither of them moves for one more breath.
WHAT CHANGES. He says out loud, for the first time, that what he wants is the plate and that he will not take it, and she is the one who has to hold that. It does not make it lighter. It makes it theirs.
HANDOVER. Mara arrives on Joren's and Corren's arms, upright, grey in the face, Yelva following; the day told, the clasp, "You called. I ran." Plot as in BEAT_MAP_BATCH8.md.

## CH.40 | KAEL | Day 37, the room over the tar-shop and the lane, night, before the march
SCENE. A dark room, one shuttered lantern, the company waiting below. Mood: hushed and fond, a little funny, with a heavy night ahead.
WHAT HAPPENS.
- Sol holds out the lantern to him at the door. He says he does not need it, the lane is familiar. She says that is not the reason. He stares at her. It is the speech he made on the morning she went to the Thorne house, turned round. He takes the lantern, and laughs, once, quietly, and says she has been saving that.
- Humour: she kneels to check his boot, ties the lace in a bowline, and says it will not come undone. He says it will not come off either, and that he will have to be buried in it. She says that is a very good reason to keep the boots on.
- Friction: he says he would like to carry the other end of the cord down tomorrow and she would like him not to; neither of them says it. They look at each other across the lantern and both know what the other has not said. Sol says she wants to say a thing aloud, because it is the rule. She says she is afraid. He says he is too. She says that was easy. He says it was the easiest thing he has said all week.
- Touch: in the dark lane she takes his free hand and tucks it under her arm, as on the cliff road, and they walk with the lantern between them and their shadows long. Joren and Corren walk ahead, not looking back. Odo and Tessa behind them with the cords, not talking.
- Want: a pace before the light of the Thorne guard's lamps reaches them she lets go of his hand, without a word, and he lets her. He puts his hand in his coat, where the fifth page is, and keeps it there until the line is in sight.
WHAT CHANGES. The lantern goes from an argument to a joke to a trust, in the space of a lane. They go toward the Thorne line as a pair who have said the fear aloud and are not hiding it.
HANDOVER. The Thorne line, Aldous stepping out of it, the steward, her request, her one line to Kael about his leg, the night in the yard in turns, her words to Sol. Plot as in BEAT_MAP_BATCH8.md.
