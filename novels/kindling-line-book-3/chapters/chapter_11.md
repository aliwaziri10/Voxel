The passage narrowed to a shoulder-width crack where mineral water had eaten the seam between two strata of grey limestone. Lantern light ran ahead of them, carried by Ulla, her steps sure on the uneven floor. Behind Sol, Kael walked with Ulla's second lantern hooked over his wrist, the brass catch clicking against the leather strap each time his stick struck stone.

"I have been promoted to furniture," he said, his voice pitched low for the tunnel. "Lantern furniture. Stationary. Reliable. Does not complain about the damp."

Ulla did not turn. Her braid swung against the wool of her coat, grey shot with white at the temples. "Furniture is more useful than most husbands. It holds the light where you need it. It does not offer opinions on the structural integrity of the ceiling."

"I will take the compliment." Kael's stick tapped, paused, tapped again. The rhythm was deliberate, placed so the sound reached Sol's ears before the echo returned. "Also the ceiling is sound. Renn's chalk marks are still visible on the third rib. He would not have marked a rib that was about to fall."

Sol did not look back. She knew the sound of his stick on limestone. She knew the cadence of his gait, the slight hesitation on the left step, the way he placed his weight before committing. He walked where she could hear him. Always where she could hear him.

She said nothing about it.

"I am keeping to the left so you know where I am," he said, as if reading the thought.

Her boots found a stable purchase on a wet patch. "I know exactly where you are."

"Good."

The word settled between them, warm in the cold air.

Ahead, Ulla paused at a junction where the passage split, one arm dropping toward the boiling house vents, the other climbing toward the third gallery door. Corren materialized from the darker fork, one hand raised in a silent signal. His silhouette blocked the faint glow from the metering chamber vents.

"Scouts report clear," Corren said. "The stranger with the chain has not come back to the east approach."

"Good." Ulla's voice was flat, stripped of the warmth she used with Renn. "We go up."

"I want point," Sol said. Her hand rested on the hilt of the short blade at her belt, the leather grip worn to the shape of her palm.

"Corren's scouts know the stranger's rounds," Kael said from behind her. "They know the timing. You wait for the signal."

Sol's jaw tightened. "I lead."

Kael's stick tapped once, sharp. "You wait. The scouts clear the door."

The silence stretched. Lantern light trembled on the wet walls.

"Done," Sol said.

"Done," Kael answered.

Ulla's shoulders moved, a breath held and released. "It is like watching two people tie a knot from opposite ends. Both pulling. Neither yielding. The knot holds because neither will let go."

She turned and took the climbing fork, her lantern throwing long shadows across the ascending passage.

The grade steepened. The air grew colder, smelling of old iron and the slow breath of the Kindling moving through rock. Sol's breathing deepened. Her calves burned. Kael's stick kept its rhythm behind her, a metronome in the dark.

The passage turned sharp at a shear zone where the rock had fractured and healed crooked. The wall closed in on the left, a protruding fin of quartzite that caught the lantern light and threw it back in fractured angles.

Kael's hand found the small of her back. Not gripping. Not steering. Just there, fingers spread, the heel of his palm warm through the wool of her undyed coat. A guide. A tether.

She leaned back into it for half a step. The contact anchored her. The quartzite fin passed. The passage widened.

His hand fell away.

Ahead, Ulla's lantern illuminated a rectangle of blackness set into the tunnel wall: the third gallery door. No hinges. No handle. Two round holes bored through the iron at waist height, spaced a hand's width apart. Brass rings set into the holes, oxidized to dull green.

Corren moved past them, his boots silent on the stone. He crouched before the door, his fingers tracing the rings. "Renn's tally pegs. The holes match. Same diameter. Same spacing."

Sol stopped beside him. The door was cold. Not warm like the enamelled knot above the boiling house cellar. Not warm like the plate at the foot of the second stair. Dead iron, sealed long ago by the Council, the seams fused, lead poured into the bolt channels.

"The twin pegs," she said. "We have them."

Kael came up on her left. His stick rested against the tunnel wall. From his coat pocket he drew the table peg, cold brass, the edges worn smooth. He held it to the left hole. The fit was exact. The peg seated with a click that echoed in the gallery.

He withdrew it. Tested the right hole. Same click. Same seat.

Kael returned the table peg to his pocket. His palm lingered on the brass before the wool covered it.

"The ledge peg," Sol said. "It is in my pack."

Seventeen years. The gauge had not moved since Odo read it. Seventeen, carried off other people over forty years, held in the second line beneath the boiling house.

Sol's fingers brushed the pack strap at her shoulder. The ledge peg was a hard shape against her ribs through the wool. She had carried it since the ledge below the Tar Lane forge.

Two pegs. One mold. One breath.

"Can we open it?" she asked. "Just to see what is behind it."

Corren shook his head. "The seams are fused. The bolts are leaded. The door is the seal."

Ulla set her lantern on a ledge in the tunnel wall. The flame steadied behind glass. She faced the door, her hands folded at her waist, the wool of her coat straining across her shoulders.

The air in the passage seemed to thicken. The Kindling's pulse thrummed in the rock, a slow heartbeat felt through bone and boot sole.

Sol looked at the brass rings. At the cold iron door. At the woman standing before it, lantern light catching the grey at her temples, the line of her mouth.

"You are afraid for him," Sol said. "For Renn. For what the line has taken."

Ulla's eyes met hers. The irises were pale in the lamplight, the pupils dilated. "I am afraid for his air. The third gallery is narrow. The air is bad. Renn's level hand: you saw his chalk. He was not well when he wrote it. He may not have measured the distance. He may not have timed the breath."

"She is afraid for his hope," Kael said quietly. "Not the line. Not the plate. The hope that he comes back whole."

Ulla did not deny it. Her fingers tightened on the wool at her waist. "He has been down there. He knows the stair that has no bottom. If anyone closes the line while he is down there, he will be the nearest living body to the source point. The stored seventeen will go to him."

"Unless another body stands closer," Sol said.

The words were a statement. A fact. The rule measured distance. The nearest living body paid. The nearest living body received.

Kael's stick shifted on the stone. His boots scraped as he adjusted his weight. "The stranger measured the east approach," he said. "Whoever he is, he knows the geometry."

The lantern light moved on the wall. Shadows of the three of them stretched and contracted as the flame stirred. The Kindling's pulse thrummed, steady as a held breath.

Sol thought of the word *later*. She thought of the narrow places, the dark beyond the door, the plate waiting at the foot of the second stair. She thought of Kael's hand at her back, warm through wool, guiding without steering. She thought of the wish spoken on the sill above the dark Reach, acknowledged, recorded, not undone.

She did not say *later*. Ulla was a pace ahead, listening.

The silence stretched. Kael lifted the second lantern, the flame steady behind glass. Corren stood braced against the tunnel wall. Ulla did not move, her face turned to the door.

Sol counted her own breaths. In. Out. In. Out. The rhythm steadied her. The pulse in the rock kept its slow cadence beneath the counting.

Then a sound.

Not footsteps. Not the stick. A tap. Metal on stone. Sharp. Deliberate.

*Tap. Tap. Tap. Tap. Tap. Tap. Tap. Tap. Tap. Tap. Tap.*

Eleven.

The sound came from behind the door.

Sol's palm went flat against the iron. Ulla's hand found her wrist, fingers cold, grip tight.

*Tap. Tap. Tap. Tap. Tap. Tap. Tap. Tap. Tap. Tap. Tap.*

Eleven again. The same rhythm. The same count.

The charge cycle. Eleven breaths. The east pulse added a twelfth, but this was eleven. The old cycle. The second line's count before the east break added its breath.

A hand behind the door, tapping out the cycle.

The tapping stopped.

Silence flooded the passage. The Kindling's pulse thrummed. Sol's breath caught in her throat.

"Renn," Ulla whispered.

"Or the man with the chain," Corren said, very low.

Nobody answered either of them. Kael stepped up at Sol's shoulder and said nothing, and Sol kept her palm on the cold iron and counted eleven again, waiting for a twelfth.
