# ROMANCE BEATS - BATCH 1 (CH.1 TO 5) - Kindling Line Book 3

Written 2026-10-04 to replace the one-line romance gestures in BEAT_MAP.md for ch.1 to 5. Each romance beat is a FULL SCENE of at least 600 words (a quarter of the chapter), placed first, before any plot. The plot beats in BEAT_MAP.md stay as written and follow each scene; the scene hands over to them. No new characters or places. No restaged first kiss, first "I love you" or first night (Book 2 spent them). Counts only where the BEAT_MAP entry allows. No em dashes. The collar beat belongs to ch.12 and ch.23 only.
How to read each entry: SCENE (where, mood) | WHAT HAPPENS (talk, touch, want, friction, humour) | WHAT CHANGES between them | HANDOVER to the plot beat.

## CH.1 | SOL | Day 0, night, the room over the tar-shop
SCENE. Late, quiet after the longest day. Bread from Ansa eaten standing. Both are tired past sense. Mood: tender, a little giddy, with something unsaid between them.
WHAT HAPPENS.
- Kael cannot stop moving. He straightens the page, the lamp, the stick. Sol watches from the cot with her boots off. Humour: she says he wrote it down so he could not change his mind halfway through saying it. He admits that is exactly why.
- He gives her the fourth page. She makes him sit while she reads, because he is standing on the bad leg. He sits like a man told to sit.
- She reads slowly on purpose. Friction: one line says he would spend his years first. She finds the crossed-out line under it and asks to hear it. He reads it: he does not know how to want her safe without standing in front of her. She does not forgive it and does not scold it. She says: "That is the whole problem, and you wrote it down."
- Touch: she takes his pen, writes one line under his words and signs I.V. Her hand leans the other way from his. He watches her write as if it were the first time anyone had written on his page.
- Want: they sit shoulder to shoulder on the sill with the lamp behind them. Neither moves for a long time. She wants him and wants the numbers said first, and says so plainly: she wants to say her count out loud tonight, for the first time since the Reckoning, and she wants him to hear it. He says: "Tell me now." She says: "Ask me again in an hour." He does, to the minute, which makes her laugh.
WHAT CHANGES. They agree the numbers will be said aloud from now on. It is a promise made lightly and it is the weight of the whole book.
HANDOVER. The hour passes. Sol feels the Kindling stir with the pulse from the east and says "Now." That is scene 1B (the small flare and the stand-back ask) as in BEAT_MAP.md. Ledger addition: one line early in the chapter that the tap is shut and the east pulse remains.

## CH.2 | KAEL | Day 1, morning, the stair and the table
SCENE. Grey morning. Ansa's stair is steep and the stick does not like it. Mood: wry, easy, a little nervous about the house they are going to.
WHAT HAPPENS.
- Sol walks on his numb left side down the stair and says nothing about it. He notices the third time and says so. She says: "Then keep up."
- At the table, she writes the order of the questions on the back of his hand in ink, the way he once let her write his words on hers. He lets her. Humour: the ink smudges on his cuff and Ansa remarks on it without being asked.
- Friction: he wants to ask Aldous first. She wants to see Malrik's face when the question lands. They trade fairly. Kael gives in, then notices himself giving in and says so before she can. She says: "That counts."
- Touch: she turns the stick in his hand so it sits on the right side, and tells him to stop hiding it from Malrik. He says a man in a house like Thorne's wants to look sound. She says Malrik will look at the stick and see what the house cost. He carries it openly.
- Want: a quiet moment at the door. She hands him his gloves and keeps hold of her end a second too long. Neither mentions it.
WHAT CHANGES. He stops hiding the stick, which is the first public thing he has stopped hiding.
HANDOVER. They go up to the Thorne house. Scene 2B (the political scene) follows as written.

## CH.3 | SOL | Day 1 evening, Ansa's room
SCENE. Evening. The room over the tar-shop smells of tar and soup. Mood: warm, domestic, with a rope of worry underneath (Renn).
WHAT HAPPENS.
- Ansa feeds them and teases. Humour: she teases Kael about the stick and the two names, and calls Sol "ward-girl" until Sol says "Eat," and Ansa says "I am eating." Sol watches Kael take the teasing without defending himself and finds it harder to watch than to bear.
- Friction: Kael tries to carry the dishes. Sol says sit. He sits, and the stick falls. Tam picks it up and hands it over with no remark, which is its own kindness.
- Touch: under the table her foot rests against his good leg. Neither looks down.
- After Ulla and Corren leave, Kael says plainly that he is afraid for Renn and that he does not know how to be afraid beside someone without taking the fear off her. She says: "Then do not take it. Hold your end." He says he will try. She says trying is all she has asked since the first day.
- Want: she leans her head against his shoulder and falls quiet. He does not shift his arm.
WHAT CHANGES. Sol notices she is not afraid of the line any more. She is afraid for someone else, which is new.
HANDOVER. Corren's scouts and Ulla come to the door with Renn's folded sheet as in BEAT_MAP.md (the sheet and the word "warm"). Ledger additions: Ansa says in one line that the awning came down the morning after the vote and wages were paid to the day; Wenna Varel sent bread and Varel is home.

## CH.4 | KAEL | Day 2, before and after the healer's room
SCENE. The passage outside Yelva's back room, then the room itself, then the passage again. Mood: heavy, honest, no jokes until the end.
WHAT HAPPENS.
- In the passage Kael says he wants to say something to Mara that he has not said to anyone. Sol asks whether he wants her inside. He says yes, then no. She says: "I will stay by the door. I will keep my hand on your sleeve. I will not listen to the words." Friction: he does not trust that and says so. She says: "Then trust the sleeve."
- Inside he says it. The words stay private on the page. Sol's hand stays on his sleeve and does not tighten.
- Humour (the only one): Yelva scolds both of them for mud on her floor and gives each a cloth. They wipe their boots like children.
- Touch and want, after, in the passage: Sol puts her forehead against his shoulder and says what she is afraid of: that he will make the pegs his job because it is the only way he knows to stay near danger and near her at the same time. He says that is exactly what he was thinking. She says: "Then say it now, not on the stair."
WHAT CHANGES. They name his habit aloud, a day earlier than he would have done alone.
HANDOVER. Joren's quiet line that the cold has reached her forearms. Plot as written.

## CH.5 | SOL | Day 3, the walk to the east approach
SCENE. Wet stones on the way to the boiling house. Mood: playful, then taut.
WHAT HAPPENS.
- Sol ties the line between them without being asked. Kael ties his own end badly. Humour: she undoes it, ties a bowline, makes him do it three times, and tells him a man who ties a knot like that should not be allowed near a cliff. He says he has been near several. She says "I know. That is what worries me."
- Friction: he tests the line with a pull and she slaps his hand off it. He says it is a test. She says he can test it on someone who is not wearing it.
- Touch: on the wet stone his stick slips and she takes his weight before he knows he is falling. The rope between them goes tight and they are very close. He can feel her pulse through the line.
- Want: she looks at his mouth and decides not here. He sees her decide. She says: "Later." He says: "Hold me to it." Small private word between them from now on.
WHAT CHANGES. "Later" becomes a word that means they both want to and are waiting for a safe place.
HANDOVER. Odo's two readings at the iron door (eleven, then twelve). Plot as written.

## NOTES FOR THE WRITER
- Each scene needs a different face of the couple: ch.1 tender, ch.2 wry, ch.3 domestic, ch.4 heavy, ch.5 playful and charged. If two sound alike when drafted, change the later one.
- Sol is always an equal in the room: she sets the order, she ties the knots, she names his habit. Kael lets her. That is the whole romance in the first act.
- Counts stay off the page except ch.1 (Sol says hers aloud once) and not at all in ch.3 and 5.
