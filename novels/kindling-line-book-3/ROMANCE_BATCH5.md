## CH.21 | SOL | Day 19, the 211 steps to the anchor chamber
SCENE. The long stair down. Cold, damp, narrow. Torch light on wet stone. Mood: quiet and intimate in an odd way, because they are both counting.
WHAT HAPPENS.
- Kael counts the steps under his breath, as Odo did. At forty Sol joins in without being asked. They count the rest together, side by side where the stair allows it and one behind the other where it does not.
- Humour: Kael loses the count at one hundred and seventeen and says a number that is not a number. Sol corrects him in a flat voice. He says he did it on purpose. She says he is a very bad liar.
- Friction: Kael offers to carry the lamp and the leaf case. Sol says she will carry both; they are hers. He says they are heavy. She says that is what makes them hers.
- Touch: the stair narrows and their shoulders press together for twenty steps. Neither leaves a gap. At one point her hand finds the small of his back for balance and stays there.
- Want: somewhere past one hundred and fifty she stops counting aloud and he keeps counting for both of them, quietly, so she can think. She notices and does not say thank you.
WHAT CHANGES. The shared count becomes their way of staying close in places where closeness is dangerous.
HANDOVER. The round chamber, the anchor plate not touched, the leaf and Renn's sheet and the diagram laid side by side; the dotted line ends at the warm mark; Sol decides to count her mother's carried year as hers. Plot as in BEAT_MAP_BATCH5.md. At the top she says the one sentence to Kael.

## CH.22 | KAEL | Day 20, the room over the tar-shop, evening
SCENE. Lamp, a pot of Ansa's soup on the floor, a gauge-book page, a pencil. Mood: domestic, warm, then suddenly honest.
WHAT HAPPENS.
- Sol sits on the floor with her back against Kael's shins while he writes, so her weight rests on the good leg's side. She eats Ansa's soup from the pot and gives him half the spoon-work. Humour: she says the spoon is hers and he can have the pot. He says that is an unfair division of labour.
- Kael does the arithmetic on the back of the gauge-book page, in his square hand, with her initials still in the margin from Book 2. He says the gap aloud: seventeen and a half. He notices the gauge reads seventeen and says nothing about it.
- Friction: he offers to leave the Reach for a winter if the gap is what is hurting her. Sol answers with the rule, plainly: when no one stands near, she pays all of it; when he stands near, he pays for her; his leaving is only her paying. She refuses. She says she wants him where she can count him. He stops writing.
- Touch: she tips her head back against his knee and looks up at him. He puts the pencil down and rests his hand on her hair.
- Want: he realises his offer was the old habit, deciding for her what she could bear. He says so before she can. She says: "Thank you for saying it first."
WHAT CHANGES. Kael catches the old habit himself, before she has to point at it.
HANDOVER. Sol: "Then we say it out loud, where they can hear." Kael does not ask who "they" are. Plot as in BEAT_MAP_BATCH5.md. The text does not write the sum of her count and the stored seventeen.

## CH.23 | SOL | Day 21, MIDPOINT, the gallery mouth and the room after
SCENE. Dusk. Before they go down to the Lower Reach, in the stair above the gallery mouth; and then, late, the room over the tar-shop. Mood: fraught and bright before; loud with relief after.
WHAT HAPPENS BEFORE.
- Sol fixes Kael's collar and he lets her. Then he fixes hers. Neither speaks. (The only echo of ch.12's collar beat; do not extend it.)
- Humour (nervous): Kael says he has rehearsed his line nine times and cannot remember it. Sol says to say it the way he would say the weather. He says he has never been good at the weather.
WHAT HAPPENS AFTER (private scene, written to the door and cut away at the same point ch.17 cut).
- Register: high heat, laughing, shaking. Sol lets him see she was afraid of being seen.
- Friction: Kael says it was easier to say the count to the Reach than to say her name to her. Sol says that is the most honest thing he has said all week and is also ridiculous.
- Touch: they cannot stop touching each other's faces. She traces the edge of his jaw and he catches her hand and holds it there.
- Want: this is the one night the Reach has seen them claim each other; they are fierce about it and then gentle. Cut away at the door.
WHAT CHANGES. They are no longer private about being together. It costs them nothing and changes nothing about who pays, and they are both a little stunned at how much lighter it feels.
HANDOVER. The public step as in BEAT_MAP_BATCH5.md: Sol names herself and her count, Kael names himself, Ansa says "Eat," Odo reads seventeen unchanged, Bram folds a slip and goes. The private scene comes after as above.

## CH.24 | KAEL | Day 22, the room over the tar-shop, morning
SCENE. Morning after. Tea, a window, nothing to explain. Mood: easy, teasing, a little shy.
WHAT HAPPENS.
- Sol, over tea: "You hedged in front of the whole Reach." Kael: "I do not promise what I cannot measure. About thirty-four." She laughs. They are easy with each other the morning after, and nothing is explained.
- Humour: she asks if "about" is a legal term. He says it is an actuarial one. She says he can keep it.
- Friction (very light): he starts to walk to the yard without the stick, forgets it entirely, and she lets him go without comment to see how far he gets. Tam jogs after him with it. She says: "That is how far."
- Touch: she lays her palm against the bare skin of his wrist and counts. She tells him his pulse is steady. He says it is because he is not looking at her. She says he is looking at her now. He says: "Ah."
- Want: a long look, then Tam appears at the door with the stick. They both pretend they were having a conversation about tea.
WHAT CHANGES. The walk without the stick is not triumph; it is frightening to trust it, and she is there when he admits that.
HANDOVER. Level walk to the yard, Odo's closing plan, pegs and cords and pullers, Tessa's line: "Whoever stands at the plate is nearest. That is not a choice, that is where the line is." Plot as in BEAT_MAP_BATCH5.md.

## CH.25 | SOL | Day 23, the tar-shop door before the Thorne house
SCENE. Morning at the door, then the stair up. Mood: relaxed, a shared joke, a little protective.
WHAT HAPPENS.
- Kael tells her where he will be (Odo's hut) before she asks, and she tells him where she is going before he asks. It is "tell me now, and tell me first, every time", inverted into ease. Humour: they say it at exactly the same time and then both stop and say "You first." Ansa says from inside that if she has to listen to this every morning she will charge rent.
- Friction: Kael offers her the lantern. She says it is day. He says take it anyway. She says she does not need it. He says that is not the reason. She takes it.
- Touch: he adjusts the cord on her collar where it has twisted, and she lets him. Their hands brush. She says: "Later." He says: "Hold me to it." It is the fourth time, and it is beginning to sound like a vow.
- Want: she walks away down the lane without looking back, which costs her. She looks back at the corner. He is still at the door.
WHAT CHANGES. The rule is no longer a duty; it is how they say goodbye.
HANDOVER. The Thorne house, the still-room, the small room beside it; the clasp on the folded cloth, a note in a right-leaning hand; Sol wants the woman heard. Plot as in BEAT_MAP_BATCH5.md (no search, no questioning, no accusation).
