## CH.41 | SOL | Day 38, the cellar mouth and the second stair, the descent
SCENE. A cold stone mouth, a lantern, a stair going down into warmth. Mood: quiet, close, slightly funny, because they are walking down to the thing that nearly killed them and neither of them wants to be the one to be solemn first.
WHAT HAPPENS.
- At the cellar mouth Odo and Tessa go down first, with the cords, arguing in low voices about whose reading of the sill will be taken. Sol waits with Kael for them to be out of earshot. She says she wants to go down the stair with him, not behind him and not ahead, and asks him, aloud, to walk beside her where the stair allows. He waits a count of three before he says yes, and they both notice how much the count has become a habit.
- Humour: on the stair the stick clicks on every step. She says it is the best sound in the Reach. He says it is the sound of a man being slow on purpose. She says it is the sound of a man who is coming, which is better. He says he would like to be heard from further off in future. She says she will hear him from the top of the world.
- Friction: at the turn she stops and asks him to wait there while she goes on to the foot. He says he can see the plate from the turn. She says he can watch from there, and that he is allowed one day of watching and tomorrow he will be at the door. He says that is a very generous allowance. She says she is a generous woman and he should not tell anyone. He does not move from the turn.
- Touch: she goes on alone, down the last steps, and comes back up the same steps and stands in front of him with her palm out. She says the iron is warm. She puts her hand on his cheek. It is warm, the same as the plate, and she says: "That is what it is like." He says it is like her. She says that is not a compliment to the iron, and he says it is not meant for the iron.
- Want: they stay a moment with her hand on his face and the lantern on the step, saying nothing, and then the techs call up the stair about the chalk. She says she will tell the chalk to wait. He says the chalk has waited forty years.
WHAT CHANGES. She has touched the plate and come back to him, and the warmth is the same warmth. The thing that frightened them is, for one breath, only a place where she has to sit.
HANDOVER. The marks in chalk, the gauge read twice, Sol sits on the single step with her palm flat on the iron, the line's eleven breaths and the twelfth. Plot as in BEAT_MAP_BATCH9.md. The scene above is the walk down; the cheek on the way back up. The touch of the plate itself is in the plot, after the scene.

## CH.42 | KAEL | Day 39, the lane behind the tar-shop, the hour
SCENE. A back step in the sun, a wall, a patch of lane nobody is using. Mood: idle, warm, wry, with a heavy day coming and a lightness set against it.
WHAT HAPPENS.
- Sol says today is the hour she asked for and he said he would keep in his coat. They sit on the back step with nothing in their hands. For a long time neither of them says anything. Then she says this is harder than the plate. He says it is the hardest thing he has ever done on purpose.
- Humour: they try to do nothing properly. They make a game of it, who can stay idle longest. Kael loses at once, because he starts counting the stones in the wall. Sol loses next because she starts untying a knot in her sleeve. Ansa passes with a pot, looks at them both and says they look unwell. They say they are resting. Ansa says it does not suit them and carries on up the lane.
- Friction: Kael's face goes still; she can see he is making a list. She puts her hand over his eyes and says he is doing it. He says he is not. She says she can feel the list through her palm. He says he is adding "stop making lists" to the list. She laughs into his shoulder.
- Touch: she lies back with her head in his lap and her feet on the step. He sits with one hand in her hair and the other flat on the stone. She asks what he would do with a whole season with no order and no plan. He says he would walk the Reach and not look at one line. She says she would sit on the gallery wall with Ansa and learn to splice.
- Want: they talk about "after", small and plain: where the bed would go, whether Ansa would let the room be theirs, a lamp. Neither says a number. Neither says "if". She says it is the first time she has said "after" without it sounding like a prayer. He says it is the first time he has said it at all.
WHAT CHANGES. "After" becomes something they can say. It is a lighter thing than either of them expected, and it is the thing the next two days are for.
HANDOVER. Joren with word that the pick and two men are on the east approach; the hour ends; Kael says "Joren and Corren" aloud and goes to the Thorne line. Plot as in BEAT_MAP_BATCH9.md. Nobody says "stand back".

## CH.43 | SOL | Day 40, the yard of the boiling house at the cellar mouth, morning
SCENE. Grey early light, the Thorne guard in line at the far gate, Mara on her stool at the cellar mouth with her stick across her knees. Mood: bright and shaky, fond, ceremonial in a way neither of them would call ceremonial, with the day all around it.
WHAT HAPPENS.
- Sol asks Kael, aloud and in small things, as they have practised: to pass her coat, to hold the lantern, to take her left hand on the stair. He waits the count of three each time and says yes each time. Mara watches from her stool and says that if they are going to do it in front of her they might do it faster, because her stool is hard.
- Humour: Odo, on the stair, says he takes everything twice. Sol says then she loves him twice. Odo says in his deep voice that once is plenty and he will take the second as read. Tessa says she wants it on record. Sol says the record is her coat.
- Friction: Kael starts to say he wishes he could do it for her, and stops himself, and says instead that he wishes he could be there. Sol says that is the true sentence. She asks him not to say the other one even in his head today. He says he will try. She says "I will take try."
- Touch: she kisses him once, on the mouth, in the yard in front of the stair, quickly, hard, with her hand flat on his chest as if to feel the answer through his ribs. He holds her face in both hands and says her name and no number. She says it is the best thing anyone has ever said to her and walks to the stair.
- Want: she says to Mara over her shoulder that she is going to be a long time and a short time. Mara says she has all day and all night, she is good at it. Kael follows her to the cellar mouth and stops at it, because the map says the foot is hers.
WHAT CHANGES. The kiss and the small asking are the last thing before the room, and she goes down knowing exactly what she has been given and what she is about to give back. The echo is not in this scene; it comes at the plate.
HANDOVER. The descent in order, the pegs set, Kael at the foot an arm's reach behind her, Sol seated at the plate; the moment she says "Kael, stand back" and he says yes; the burn; the pull; the close; Mara's "Now." Plot as in BEAT_MAP_BATCH9.md. The kiss in this scene is a farewell for an hour, not a restaging of the first.

## CH.44 | KAEL | Day 40 night, the room over the tar-shop
SCENE. One lamp, the door barred, rain somewhere far off. Mood: raw, relieved, laughing a little, shaking, tender; the night after a thing that could have gone the other way. Written to the door and cut away at the same point ch.17 and ch.23 cut.
WHAT HAPPENS.
- They come in quietly, shed their coats and sit on the bed without speaking. She says she is warm, a different warm, as if there were more of her. He says he can feel it from across the room. She says it is not her; it is the years. He says it is her.
- Humour: she says she feels like a woman who has eaten a very large meal. He says she looks like one. She says that is the first insult she has taken as a compliment in a week. He says it is possible both are true.
- Friction: he says he is afraid to touch her in case it hurts her, in case there is something in her that is not hers. She says it is hers now, and that if he does not touch her she will count it against him. He takes her hand to see. It is only warm. He laughs, once, helplessly.
- Touch: she pulls him down; they are fierce and then very gentle, with the years between them in a way neither can name. Written to the door and cut away. No first night is restaged.
- Want: after, she lies against him and says she would like to stay awake and she does not manage it. He holds her and watches her go.
WHAT CHANGES. The night is not about the years or the line; it is about two people who can say "after" and mean it. She sleeps first, and he is the one who counts.
HANDOVER. Sol asleep; Kael counting her breaths and saying her count aloud once, softly; Joren at the door; Aldous with Malrik's sentence; the loose ends in a handful of lines. Plot as in BEAT_MAP_BATCH9.md.

## CH.45 | SOL | A season later, the room over the tar-shop, morning
SCENE. Ordinary morning light, a pot of Ansa's tea, the lane noise coming up through the boards. Mood: easy, funny, plain and happy, with nothing at stake and a great deal at hand.
WHAT HAPPENS.
- They wake in the same bed with no plan in the room. Sol says her count aloud, thirty-three, and Kael says his. She says he still says "about." He says it is on principle. She says to say it without. He says it, and it comes out clean, and he looks startled at it.
- Humour: she says the sock is the best thing she has ever knitted and that she did not knit it. He says he knew she did not. She says she bought it. He says he knew that too. She says he knows everything and does nothing about it. He says he does the sock.
- Friction: Kael gets up without the stick and walks to the window and back, and does not mention it, and Sol does not mention it, and neither of them can stop watching the other not mention it. Sol says finally that it was a very good walk. He says it was twelve steps. She says she was not counting. He says she was.
- Touch: she sits on the bed and he stands in front of her; she puts her arms round his waist and her face against his chest and listens. He says what does she hear. She says it is slower. He says that is because she is not counting. She says she will go on.
- Want: at the end of the scene, quietly, she says "Later." He says "Hold me to it." They look at each other and they both know it is no longer a promise to survive; it is a plan. It is the fifth time and the first time it is only a joke.
WHAT CHANGES. The private words go from a vow made under threat to a small shared joke about an ordinary day. The count has stopped being a clock.
HANDOVER. Joren with the letter from the Ashworth gate; Sol reads it aloud; the lane, the blue lamp, Ansa's "Eat"; the cellar a last time, no rope, no cords, the plate cold. Plot as in BEAT_MAP_BATCH9.md. The last image is the iron.
