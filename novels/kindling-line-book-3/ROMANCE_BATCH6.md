## CH.26 | KAEL | Day 24, the room over the tar-shop, early morning
SCENE. Grey light, a cold floor, one lamp not yet put out. Mood: domestic, sleepy, tender, with a small edge underneath because both of them know the trial of the rig is today.
WHAT HAPPENS.
- Kael sits on the edge of the bed and looks at his numb left foot as if it belonged to the room. He reaches for the sock first, to do it himself. Sol takes it out of his hand, kneels on the boards and says "I know you can" before he has said it. He says he was only going to say that the floor is not that cold. She says the floor is exactly that cold.
- Humour: the sock is too big, or she puts it on the wrong way round and has to turn it, and he says the foot has no opinion. She says the foot is the only honest one of the two of them, since it never pretends to feel anything. He says that is a harsh review of his leg. She says it is a fair one.
- Friction: he watches her hands because he cannot feel them. He says it is strange to see it done and not know it is done. She says that is most of what being cared for is: you do not feel it, you watch for it. He goes quiet and then says he has spent his life on the other side of that sentence.
- Touch: she holds the foot a moment longer than the sock needs, then lays her cheek against his knee. His hand goes to her hair. Neither of them says what the day is.
- Want: he asks her, lightly, to stay five more minutes. She says she has counted and there are four. They take all four.
WHAT CHANGES. Kael lets himself be seen needing a small thing and does not repay it at once. It is the first time in the book he accepts care without keeping a ledger of it.
HANDOVER. The yard of the boiling house, Odo's two cords and the stand-in lamp, Joren at the gate. Sol's "I know you can" returns in the yard, once, when she tells him he may not pull, and it lands differently because he has heard it kindly first. Plot as in BEAT_MAP_BATCH6.md. Kael says "Joren and Corren" aloud as he leaves.

## CH.27 | SOL | Day 25, the room over the tar-shop, the hours before the knock
SCENE. Night, one lamp, a spare line coiled on the floor. Mood: taut and quiet, a held breath, with real tenderness showing through the dread.
WHAT HAPPENS.
- Sol told Kael first, as the rule requires, that the steward would come tonight. She sits cross-legged on the floor and ties a bowline on the spare line, pulls it out, ties it again. Kael sits opposite and holds the loose end, as he has done since the first one she ever tied for him. For a while they do not speak.
- Humour: after the twentieth bowline Kael says in a grave voice that it is a very good knot. Sol says it is the only one she has not made worse by thinking about it. He asks if that is a hint. She says it is a confession.
- Friction: Kael asks, carefully, whether she wants him in the room when the steward comes or in the passage. Sol says the room, but he is not to speak for her. He says he would not. She says he would, a little, with his face. He tries to arrange his face and fails, and she laughs despite the dread.
- Touch: the line goes slack when her hands stop. He moves from his end of the rope to her side and takes both her hands, still wrapped in the bight, and holds them until they are warm. She presses her forehead to his collarbone.
- Want: she says she would like one hour tomorrow in which nothing is waiting to happen to them. He says he will put it in the ledger. She says no, he will put it in his coat, where she can find it. A knock has not come yet; the lamp burns lower.
WHAT CHANGES. Sol lets Kael be the quiet one in the room while she is the one the visitor has come for. He does not take the lead and she does not have to ask him to hold back.
HANDOVER. The knock and the steward coming in; the scene as in BEAT_MAP_BATCH6.md, one short scene, no investigation. Kael holds out the primer and is not given it back. Sol takes the line off the floor before the steward sits so that nothing in her hands is a weapon or a prop.

## CH.28 | KAEL | Day 26, the room over the tar-shop, late night and then first light
SCENE. A lamp turned low, Sol asleep, the sound of rain on the tar-shop roof. Then morning. Mood: intimate and private, then exposed, because what he wrote cannot be taken back.
WHAT HAPPENS.
- Kael sits at the gauge-book and writes his fifth page in his square hand. Sol wakes halfway and says, without opening her eyes, that he is writing. He says go back to sleep. She says she can hear him being careful. He says that is not a sound. She says it is, and it sounds like a man afraid of a comma.
- Humour: she asks, half asleep, whether it is about her. He says it is about him, with her in it. She says that is the right way round and goes back to sleep. He laughs under his breath and has to stop writing until he is steady.
- In the morning she picks the page up from the floor by the lamp, where he left it face up, and reads it without being handed it. Friction: he says that was not for her yet. She says it was at her feet, in her room, in his square hand, and that a page left on the floor is a page left for her. He says that is not how privacy works. She says it is how hers works.
- Touch: she sits on the bed, pats the blanket beside her, and he sits. She folds the page along its old crease and puts it in his hand and closes his fingers on it. She says: "Keep it where you can reach it."
- Want: he asks, very quietly, whether she believes it. She says she does not need to believe it today, she needs it to exist. He says that he is afraid it is a promise his legs may not keep. She says then it is a good thing the promise is spoken first and the legs come after. The page is never quoted in full on the page.
WHAT CHANGES. The fifth page is the first thing he has written for both of them that he did not decide alone how she would receive. She read it before he could manage her reading of it, and it did not break anything.
HANDOVER. A knock at the door; Bram, grey ribbon, very straight spine, with the custodian's note, exactly as in BEAT_MAP_BATCH6.md. Kael puts the page inside his coat before he opens the door and does not hide that he has done it. Joren is at the stair; Kael says "Joren" aloud before he goes down.

## CH.29 | SOL | Day 27, the cliff road at dusk, going down to the healer's back room
SCENE. Wind, a long fall on one side, a cold pink sky. Mood: quiet, warm, a little silly, because the next room holds Mara and they are both postponing it by the length of the road.
WHAT HAPPENS.
- On the narrow stretch Sol steps round Kael and takes the drop side before he can. He sees it, stops, and laughs, a real laugh, the first one in front of her since the public step. She says it is not funny. He says it is the most unnecessary thing anyone has done for him on this road, and he has been on it with a surveyor.
- Humour: he points out, gravely, that the road is the same width for both of them. She says the drop does not know whose side it is, and neither does she, and she would rather it learned. He says that is bad cliff law. She says she is a bad lawyer, and the road is not his.
- Friction: he moves to take the drop side back, and she puts her free hand flat against his chest and says no. He says his leg can feel the wall, which is more than hers can feel the edge. She says that is exactly why. He lets his hand fall and lets her stay where she is.
- Touch: she takes his hand and tucks it through her arm, so he can walk on the wall side with weight on her without it looking like help. Their hips knock; he does not move away. She says the wind is trying to read their conversation. He says it can have the unimportant half.
- Want: for a little way they walk in step, not counting, not planning. She says she wants to have been on this road with him on a day when nobody was dying. He says that is a fair thing to want and he will keep it. They go quiet because the healer's lamp is in sight.
WHAT CHANGES. The drop side becomes hers, by agreement and not by argument. It is a small, silly, true thing: he lets her stand where the danger is without taking the danger from her.
HANDOVER. Yelva's back room, Mara's cold at the elbows, Tessa with the gauge at seventeen, the copper clasp, Mara's "That one." Sol keeps hold of his hand until the door, then lets go at the threshold without being asked. Plot as in BEAT_MAP_BATCH6.md.

## CH.30 | KAEL | Day 28, the room over the tar-shop, the night before, written first in the chapter and before the demand
SCENE. One lamp, the window shut against the lane, two cups of cold tea nobody finished. Mood: brief and warm, with the day ahead folded into it and not named.
WHAT HAPPENS.
- They lie on the bed in their clothes, on top of the blanket, as if they might need to be up at once. Sol says her count aloud, sixteen and a half, the way another woman might say good night to a man at her door. Kael says his, about thirty-four. She says he still hedges. He says he is rounding toward the truth. She says it is the first time anyone has called a hedge a direction.
- Humour: they argue, softly, about who says it first tomorrow night and decide it will be whoever is awake. Sol says that is the unfairest rule she has heard, because she is always the one awake. Kael says he is the one who says it in his sleep. She says he does, and it is not a number.
- Friction: Kael starts to say what he will do at the door tomorrow and she stops his mouth with her fingers. She says tonight is not for the plan; she has it, he has it, and what is left over is for them. He says he is afraid to waste the night on the plan. She says he is wasting it on being afraid of wasting it.
- Touch: her head on his shoulder, her hand over his heart, his hand flat between her shoulder blades. She counts his pulse against her palm in silence and tells him the number; it is steadier than yesterday. He says that is because she is counting. She says she will go on counting.
- Want: they do not make love; they lie still and are very close. He says he would like the night to be longer than the order. She says the order does not get a say in how long the night is.
WHAT CHANGES. The counts become a good-night. What had been a burden said aloud for others is now said to each other, and they sleep.
HANDOVER. The morning at the gallery mouth: Bram reads the formal demand; Kael's two sentences; the custodian and the pick leave without threat. Odo and Tessa check each cord twice. Kael's last line to the lane: "Tomorrow, then." Sol, quietly: "Say it first." The night scene does not use "Say it first"; that line belongs to the hook only. Plot as in BEAT_MAP_BATCH6.md, under half the chapter, no debate.
