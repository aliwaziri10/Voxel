# Brief 3 | Bryony | thaw | touch level 1

PLOT: Dawn in the Net Sheds. The Heron stands on her cradle in the big shed and Bryony surveys her with her hands: the oak keel soft at the heel, the garboards weeping, her father's plane marks on every frame. Her verdict is flat and professional: the keel must come out. Ottoline arrives with her ring of keys, shows her the loft above (a stove, a cot, a window on the lake) and the iron Net Sheds lantern with its green glass panel on the seaward wall, which she says burns every dusk. Ottoline says the Heron was made Fairlie keel, Marrick comb, so the seams will be waxed with Marrick wax and Ewan will bring it. She sets a jar of last autumn's honey on the bench and orders her to eat. Bryony takes one spoonful: it tastes of a man standing at a window not opening the door.
PRESENT: Bryony (flat pencil behind her ear, sawdust in her hair, scar across the back of her left hand, canvas tool roll). Ottoline (ring of keys at her belt, bent back, upright voice). NOBODY ELSE.
SETTING: the Net Sheds at dawn, the Heron on her cradle, the loft, the seaward wall.
MAGIC: honey rules 1 and 5: a spoonful tastes of the beekeeper's truest feeling the day the comb was cut. She does not know which day; she knows whose.
OBJECTS: the Heron (exact: the Larkmere ferry, a broad wooden ferry with a wheelhouse, built by Bryony's father; her oak keel is soft at the heel). The Net Sheds lantern (iron, one green glass panel). The lime jar. Bryony's tool roll.
LOCKED: "Fairlie keel, Marrick comb." Ottoline, here (echoed once by Bryony in chapter 40 only).
WITHHOLD: no Leaving Jar, no letter confession, no mention of who lights the lantern.
BODY: she remembers the exact weight of his wrist; the honey on her tongue; the held look in her own memory of the lane.
INTERIOR: she came for the Heron and tells herself the honey is only honey.
STAKES: the lime-bloom is coming and the Heron has to be in the water for it; Ottoline says it flat.
CALLBACK: ch1: his hand at her collar; ch2: nothing (Ewan's chapter, not hers).
ENDS ON: she sets the spoon down and the honey still tastes of waiting.
