## 1. Closed-world rules (hard)
1. NAMED PEOPLE: only the five in section 2. No other capitalized personal name may appear in any chapter. This includes pets, horses, dogs, cats, bees, boats (except the Heron), streets, shops, books and songs.
2. UNNAMED PEOPLE: allowed only as these roles: the quay folk, the neighbors, the town, the people of the town. Bryony's father and mother (both dead, never named), Ewan's late parents (never named). A role person speaks at most one short line and gets no personal history. Nobody from Book 1 or Book 2 appears or is named. No ferryman is mentioned.
3. PLACES: only the list in section 4. No other building, lane, hill, village, river, island or shore name. The coast town is never named: it is "the coast".
4. MAGIC OBJECTS: only the honey in section 6. No new magic object, no new magic rule. Wax, bees, hives, wood and boats carry no magic.
5. NO NEW BACKSTORY: if the brief does not state a past event, the narration may not state one.
6. NO NEW PETS, NO NEW RELATIVES, NO NEW JOBS, NO NEW FOODS BEYOND: bread, soup, porridge, apples, cheese, fish, tea, honey, oat cakes, plums.
7. WHEN UNSURE, WRITE LESS. A short true chapter is allowed. An invented fact is not.
8. NO PLANNING WORDS IN PROSE: chapter, beat, protagonist, sub-beat, touch level, canon, POV.

## 2. The cast (all five, nothing else)
- BRYONY FAIRLIE. Heroine, boatwright, thirty-three. POV in ODD chapters. Blunt, practical, quick with her hands, slow to say what she feels. Tag: a flat carpenter's pencil behind her ear; sawdust in her hair; a pale scar across the back of her left hand from a drawknife; a canvas tool roll oiled and tied with a leather thong. She left Larkmere seven springs ago, at twenty-six, on the thaw road, to learn under Abel Trent at the coast. Comparisons: boats, wood, grain, joinery, tools, water, weather on the lake. NO bee or honey comparisons in her chapters.
- EWAN MARRICK. Hero, beekeeper, thirty-four. POV in EVEN chapters. Steady, patient, dry quiet humor, kind, stubborn underneath. Tag: a hive tool in his back pocket; wax under his thumbnails; the smell of smoke on his coat; old stings on his forearms he ignores; a folded bee veil in his belt. He stayed. Comparisons: bees, comb, honey, hives, flowers, weather, seasons, smoke. NO boat or carpentry comparisons in his chapters.
- TOBIAH FAIRLIE. Bryony's brother, seventeen. Ewan's bee apprentice. Loyal to Ewan, cold to Bryony. Tag: a bee veil too big for him; ears go pink when angry; whistles through his teeth. He was ten when she left and has lived with the Marricks since.
- OTTOLINE MARRICK. Ewan's great-aunt, seventies, ran the Net Sheds for most of her life. She raised Ewan. Tag: a ring of keys at her belt, one for every shed and door in Larkmere; bent back, upright voice. She meddles because she loves them. She wrote the letter.
- ABEL TRENT. Bryony's master at the coast yard, sixties. Tag: one white eyebrow; hands like spades; talks in boat measures ("a fair curve", "sound in the heel"). Gruff, loves her like a daughter. Named first in chapter 9; present in chapters 23 to 27 and 36 to 39 only.

## 3. Voice anchors
- Close third, past tense. Odd chapters Bryony, even chapters Ewan. Never head-hop.
- Bryony's chapters never show what is inside the Leaving Jar before chapter 33, and she never thinks what Ewan thinks.
- Ewan's chapters never state what the Leaving Jar holds (he cannot taste his own honey and has never opened it).
- Bryony and Ewan were together in the year before she left. That is history, told in small body details (where a scar is, how she hums while she works), never in a flashback scene.
- Every chapter ends on a turn, a question or a held image. Never a summary, never the theme restated.

## 4. Places (use exactly these names)
Larkmere (the town). Larkmere Water (the lake). The quay and the harbor; the harbor lanterns are lit at dusk. Lantern Street (the street by the harbor, mentioned only as a street). The Net Sheds (long timber sheds on the shore; the Heron stands on her cradle in the big shed; a loft above with a stove, where Bryony sleeps). The Fairlie yard (her late father's boatyard on the shore west of the quay, shuttered for seven springs, its roof kept mended). Marrick Rise (the hill above the town: the hives, the Marrick house and the Honey House). The thaw road (the hill road to the coast, called that because it opens first). The Far Shore (the lake's opposite shore). The lime wood (a stand of lime trees on the Far Shore, with Ewan's far hives and a one-room hut).

## 5. Objects (copy these descriptions exactly, never freshly paraphrased)
- THE HERON: the Larkmere ferry, a broad wooden ferry with a wheelhouse, built by Bryony's father. Her oak keel is soft at the heel. In the Net Sheds on her cradle until the last chapters. She never has a colour named.
- THE LEAVING JAR: a squat glass jar of dark honey, sealed with a disc of Marrick wax, a strip of blue linen tied round the neck, on the top shelf of the Honey House turned so the blank side faces out. Never opened until chapter 33.
- THE NET SHEDS LANTERN: an iron lantern with one green glass panel, hung on the seaward wall of the Net Sheds. Lit at dusk by Ewan every day since she left.

## 6. The magic (state once, never vary)
1. Honey remembers. Whoever eats a spoonful of honey tastes the beekeeper's truest feeling on the day the comb was cut. It tastes of that feeling, not of events, never of words or images.
2. It never fades and cannot be faked, hidden or practiced. A beekeeper cannot choose it.
3. The beekeeper tastes only plain honey from his own jars. He learns what is in them only from other people.
4. One day's cut makes one feeling. Honey blended from different days tastes muddled.
5. Everyone in Larkmere knows this. A Marrick jar never lies. Nobody is hurt by it and nobody is forced to feel it.
6. Ordinary bees are ordinary: swarms land where they like. Wax, hives and wood carry no feeling.
NOT: it shows no events or visions, reads no one's mind, tells nobody what to do, never solves the plot, is not a curse, not a spell placed by a person, not a prophecy, no villain, no countdown with numbers. It only tastes.
