CHAPTER THIRTY-SIX

Wren screamed first, a sound torn out of her that Mara had never heard from her before, not during the pocket watch, not during any of the readings that had left her nose bleeding and her hands shaking, and Mara's whole body went cold with a fear sharper than anything she'd felt since this began.

"It's pulling toward her," Yusuf shouted over the rising roar of golden light, his own arm visibly straining against the well's rim, as though something invisible had hold of his hand and was trying to drag it somewhere he didn't want it to go. "It's not dividing evenly, it's finding whoever's weakest and pulling everything toward them!"

"Wren, let go!" Caleb's voice cracked with terror, but Wren's hand stayed locked against the stone, whether from her own stubborn refusal or something the well itself had done to hold her there, Mara couldn't tell, and the golden light around her thinned and darkened at the edges, concentrating, gathering, the exact failure Mara had feared and never managed to solve.

"I can't," Wren gasped, tears streaming down her face, her whole body shaking now with the effort of something Mara could only half understand. "It's not letting me, it's already inside, I can feel all of it, Mara, all of it, fifty years, it's trying to put fifty years into me at once—"

"No." Mara's mind raced, the shape of the failure suddenly, horribly clear, the exact structural collapse she'd warned about in Denise's kitchen two nights ago, the load finding its path of least resistance regardless of how many channels they'd dug to prevent it. "It's not evenly distributed because we never built anything to force it to be. We just hoped."

"Denise!" Priya's voice, sharp with command even through her own visible strain. "Fix this, now, whatever it takes!"

Denise's eyes were open now, wide with horror, her own hand pressed flat against the stone as she fought visibly against a current none of the rest of them could see directly. "I'm trying. I've never done this, I don't know how to redirect what's already redirecting itself—"

"Then give it something else to pull toward," Mara said, thinking faster than she'd ever thought in her life, the engineer in her screaming at the obvious, brutal solution even as every other part of her recoiled from it. "If it's finding the weakest point in the system, we make ourselves the strongest point instead. Everyone, hold tighter, not looser. Fight for your share instead of just standing here letting it choose for you."

"That's not how consent works," Dev said, his voice tight with fear even as his hand tightened obediently against the stone.

"It's not about consent anymore, it's about physics," Mara snapped, gripping the well's edge with both hands now, feeling the golden light surge up her arms in response, foreign grief slamming into her chest hard enough to steal her breath. "Wren already consented. She's just the weakest link in the chain right now, and if we don't actively pull our share away from her, this collapses exactly the way Denise warned us it could."

Caleb moved before anyone else could, releasing one hand from the well only long enough to grab Wren's shoulder, physically bracing her upright as her knees began to buckle under the weight pouring into her. "Wren, look at me. You are not carrying this alone, do you hear me? Pull back. Actively pull back, the way Mara said. Fight it."

Wren's eyes, wild and terrified, found his, and something in her seemed to steady, some old, familiar stubbornness surfacing even through the pain. "Fight it," she repeated, gritted through clenched teeth, and Mara watched her visibly brace, shoulders squaring despite everything, hand gripping the stone with sudden, fierce intention instead of helpless endurance.

The golden light shuddered, flickered, and for one terrible moment seemed to gather itself for something worse.

Then, slowly, agonizingly, it began to redistribute.

Mara felt it happening inside her own chest, a fresh wave of foreign grief settling hard and sudden into spaces that had been empty a moment before, and around the circle she heard the others gasp in unison, felt rather than saw six people simultaneously absorbing pieces of weight that had, seconds before, been rushing entirely toward one girl who couldn't bear it alone.

"That's it," Denise gasped, her own strain visibly easing as the light around her began, at last, to thin toward something manageable. "That's exactly it. You're forcing it to divide instead of hoping it would."

"Keep pulling," Mara ground out through her own gathering pain, foreign memory after foreign memory settling into her mind like stones dropped into still water, each one someone else's precious, irreplaceable loss now becoming, impossibly, partly her own to carry. "Everyone, keep pulling your own share. Don't let it choose the easy path again."

Around the well, six people held on, fighting now instead of simply offering, forcing a fifty-year weight to divide the way it had never once, in a hundred years, actually been divided before, while Wren, shaking but upright, Caleb's hand still steady on her shoulder, found her own strength returning piece by piece as the crushing flood finally, mercifully, began to ease.
