CHAPTER THIRTEEN

Mara couldn't stop seeing it. Every time she closed her eyes that night, the image came back uninvited: Wren's body gone rigid at the kitchen table, blood sliding slow toward her lip, her eyes open and unseeing while something older than any of them reached back through an old man's pocket watch and showed her exactly as much as it wanted her to see.

Caleb found her standing at the kitchen window long after midnight, staring out at nothing, the dish towel with Wren's blood on it still sitting folded on the counter because neither of them had been able to bring themselves to wash it yet.

"You should be asleep," he said, though he made no move toward bed himself.

"So should you." Mara didn't turn around. "I keep thinking about how close that was. Not to failure. To something worse than failure."

Caleb crossed the kitchen and stood behind her, close enough that she could feel the warmth of him without either of them needing to say anything about it yet. "She's twenty, Mara. Not a child, whatever we want to keep believing. She made a choice with her eyes open, and it wasn't ours to make for her."

"I know that." Mara finally turned to face him, and whatever composure she'd been holding through the last few hours cracked, just slightly, at the edges. "I know it wasn't our choice. That doesn't make it easier to have stood there and watched her bleed because of a decision none of us got to stop her from making."

"No," Caleb agreed. "It doesn't."

He reached for her hand, and she let him take it, the same way she had a few nights before when he'd been the one lying awake with something he couldn't outrun. There was a strange, quiet symmetry in it, she thought. Two people learning, slowly, how to be afraid together instead of separately.

"I keep thinking about what she saw," Mara said. "Hands. Not old, not young. Working something small and fast between her fingers." She shook her head. "It's not enough to prove anything. It's not even enough to rule anything out. Denise's hands would fit that description. So would a hundred other women in this valley."

"You think it was her."

"I think everything keeps pointing the same direction, and I'm tired of pretending that direction is a coincidence." Mara pressed the heel of her hand against her eyes, exhaustion finally catching up with the adrenaline that had carried her through the last several hours. "But Wren paid a real price tonight for a piece of proof that still isn't proof. And I don't know how to feel good about that trade, even a little, even knowing we're closer than we were yesterday."

Caleb was quiet for a moment, and when he spoke again, his voice had gone into the low, steady register she'd come to associate with him thinking something all the way through before committing to saying it out loud.

"Do you remember the night before the wedding," he said, "when you told me you were scared of exactly this. Not the danger itself. The part where caring about people means you don't get to protect them from every choice they make, even the reckless ones, even the ones that scare you."

"I remember."

"You told me that was the hardest part of loving anyone honestly. That you could build every structural safeguard in the world and someone you loved could still choose to walk out onto a bridge you'd rated for half their weight, because it was their bridge to walk, not yours to forbid." He almost smiled, though there was no real humor in it. "I didn't understand it then, not really. I think I understand it tonight."

Mara let out a breath that was almost a laugh, though it came out shakier than she meant it to. "I hate being quoted back to myself. Especially when I was right."

"You're usually right. It's one of the more exhausting things about you." He pulled her in then, both arms around her, and she let herself lean into it fully for the first time since Wren had collapsed at their table, the fear finally finding somewhere safe to land instead of circling endlessly with nowhere to go. "I love you enough that watching Wren almost break herself tonight terrified me on your behalf as much as hers. Because I know exactly how far you'd go if it were you sitting where she was sitting. I've watched you do it already, more than once, since the moment this whole thing started."

"That's not the same," Mara said, though without much conviction.

"It's exactly the same. You just do your version of it with paperwork and property maps instead of a Finder's gift, and it looks more responsible on the surface, but it's the same instinct underneath. Push until something gives, even if what gives is you." Caleb's voice softened further. "I'm not asking you to stop. I don't think I could ask that of you and still be honest about who I fell in love with. I'm asking you to let me stand where I'm standing right now more often. Not to stop you. Just so you're not carrying the weight of it alone every single time."

Mara closed her eyes against his shoulder, feeling something in her chest ease that had been wound too tight for days now, maybe weeks. "You already do that. More than you probably know."

"I want to do it on purpose, then. Not by accident, not by stumbling into the kitchen right after something's already gone wrong. On purpose, from the start of whatever comes next, whatever the stakeout ends up looking like, whatever happens the day we finally stand in front of her and say her name out loud."

"You think it's coming to that. Standing in front of her."

"I think it's already coming to that," Caleb said. "I think it has been since the wedding, since the letter, since the first time Wren found a name in a drought file that shouldn't still be walking around this valley looking forty years younger than it has any right to. We've just been circling the moment, gathering enough courage and enough proof to survive actually arriving at it."

Mara pulled back enough to look at him properly, the exhaustion in both their faces softened, for a moment, by something steadier underneath it. "When we do arrive at it," she said, "I want to know you'll still be standing where you're standing right now. Not somewhere further out, protecting yourself from whatever it costs. Here. With me."

"Always here." He said it simply, without embellishment, the way he said most things that mattered most to him. "That was the vow, Mara. I didn't say it thinking it would only apply to easy things."

She kissed him then, slow and unhurried despite the late hour and the blood-stained towel still sitting on the counter behind them, and for a little while the valley's danger receded to something distant and theoretical, replaced by the simpler, steadier fact of two people choosing, again, on a hard night, to stay chosen.

They checked on Wren before finally going to bed themselves, easing the door open quietly to find her asleep on the couch where Caleb had settled her hours before, color returned to her face, her breathing slow and even in a way that finally let some of Mara's fear unclench.

"She'll be angry with us in the morning," Mara whispered, "for hovering."

"She can be angry," Caleb said. "As long as she's breathing to do it."

They stood together in the doorway a moment longer, watching the rise and fall of Wren's breathing, and Mara found herself thinking, not for the first time since this investigation began, that the found family she'd built out of grief and stubbornness and one impossible summer last year had become, somewhere along the way, the thing she was most afraid of losing, more even than she'd once been afraid of losing herself.

"We're going to get her out of this," Mara said quietly. "All of us. Whole."

"I believe that," Caleb said, and though his voice carried the full weight of a promise neither of them could fully guarantee, Mara let herself believe it too, at least for the length of that quiet, dark hour, with the whole valley sleeping around them and Denise Voss's kitchen window, somewhere down the road, gone dark for the night like any other ordinary house.
