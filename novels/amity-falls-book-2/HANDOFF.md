# WHAT THE VALLEY STILL OWES — HANDOFF NOTES

**Series:** The Amity Falls Series, Book 2
**Working title:** "What the Valley Still Owes" — TENTATIVE, not yet confirmed by Zia. Alternatives to consider if this doesn't land: "The Names It Forgets", "Every Ending But the Last."
**Target length:** 100,000+ words, approximately 41 chapters (see beat_map.md's renumbering note, ch.14, ch.19, and ch.29 each absorbed an extra beat). Confirmed by Zia 2026-09-13; renumbering logged 2026-09-14.

## What this project is

Direct sequel to "Where the Frost Doesn't Reach" (Book 1, `novels/where-the-frost-doesnt-reach/`, published). Picks up from Book 1's final-page hook: at the wedding, Wren gives Mara a century-old letter from a woman named **Adelaide**, who made the *original* bargain — trading a town's safety for a person's memory — believing it would end with her own life. It didn't. The letter says the bargain has been "renewed again" since, by someone else, more than once. The same night, Wren cross-checks the county's 1980s drought-crisis file (the one Mara's grandfather investigated and never solved) and finds a name in it that shouldn't still belong to a living person, not if they aged the way ordinary people do.

**Book 2's engine:** someone in present-day Amity Falls has been renewing Adelaide's original bargain for generations, is still doing it, and is hiding in plain sight, aged unnaturally like Kell was — Mara, Caleb, and Wren have to find out who, stop the next renewal before it costs someone close to them, and finally close the loop Adelaide couldn't. **As of ch.29-33, the investigation phase is complete**: Denise has confessed fully, and the story has shifted into its climax phase — attempting, together with Denise's now-willing cooperation, to actually end the bargain via a "split the weight" redirection before her reserve runs out.

## Established canon this book must not contradict

- Kell "renewed" a version of the bargain himself and was exposed/collapsed publicly at the harvest festival (Book 1, ch.43) — Book 2's antagonist is a DIFFERENT, still-hidden person who either taught Kell how, or renewed the original bargain independently of him. Not the same person, not a resurrection of Kell.
- Wren is 20 now, formally enrolled in the valley's Finder apprenticeship, her "gift" (reading objects/psychometry) steadier but not fully controlled.
- Mara and Caleb are newly married. Caleb's missing two years are CONFIRMED (ch.29) to be Denise's own 8-years-ago renewal, taken deliberately using his existing grief as cover — not Kell's doing, not ambiguous. Ch.31: the memory wasn't destroyed, Denise still carries it intact inside her own mind, which is the mechanical basis for why "splitting the weight" (redistribution, not erasure) is even possible. Ch.33: Caleb has chosen to take part in the redirection attempt despite this, not from forgiveness but from wanting to see it through.
- Thomas (Mara's father figure/informant in Book 1) died in November, before the wedding — not available as a source in Book 2.
- Priya's brother Dev was convicted of a coerced violation and is serving a supervised community-service term, not exile — he's a valley resident again in Book 2, on those terms. Ch.24 onward: Dev formally joins the active investigation team, with Priya's reluctant blessing.
- Yusuf is now permanent council chair. Ch.25: Yusuf authorizes a full formal inquiry using emergency chair authority, without a full council vote, citing precedent from the night Kell collapsed.
- The bargain's mechanic (established in Book 1): trading the valley's safety/prosperity for erasing a specific person's memory of something, at a cost of the trader's own unnaturally extended life, which eventually manifests as sudden aging/collapse (Kell's fate). Ch.3 adds: Denise's version of the bargain is surgical - one small, specific memory taken per cycle, not a person's whole memory/life torn apart the way Kell attacked Caleb. This is WHY she's gone undetected for decades. **Ch.30-31 add real mechanical detail**: the bargain is a continuous pull that collects early, from the carrier herself, the longer a renewal is delayed — this is what aging looks like from the inside, and it's why the six-to-eight-year interval is a deadline, not a preference. Taken memories are transferred into the carrier, not destroyed; Denise still consciously carries every memory she's ever taken, including Caleb's two years. This reframes "ending the bargain" as a redistribution problem (moving what Denise already carries onto several willing people) rather than a destruction problem.
- Mara's grandmother's maiden line is Voss (established Book 1). Ch.21: Odette's information puts Denise's real birth year at 1938 (not the 1951 Denise has told people), making her closer to 90 than the 75 originally estimated.
- Ch.20: Adelaide's letter reread for mechanics — the bargain "compounds" and cannot simply be refused once underway; it must be "completed, or redirected onto something willing to carry the weight in a different shape." **Ambrose Whitlock** named as the man who taught Adelaide the bargain, possibly not its originator.
- Ch.21: Odette confirms Whitlock per old family stories (predates the town itself, taught one person per generation). Surfaces an uncorroborated rumor: a past keeper once "split the weight" across several willing people instead of passing it whole to a successor — first real hope of a non-death, non-collapse ending.
- Ch.22: Denise confronted directly by Mara over the birth-year discrepancy. Doesn't deny it outright, delivers a warning dressed as kindly advice, mask slips briefly. Turn of the book — Denise now knows she's under active investigation.
- Ch.23: Priya sets a new rule (nobody goes anywhere alone) and commits to briefing Yusuf. Wren raises fear of preemptive retaliation.
- Ch.24: Denise retaliates precisely — Priya loses exactly one hour of memory overnight, specifically the hour in which the "nobody goes alone" rule was set and the group's next move was decided. Proves Denise is capable of targeted, deliberate action against the investigators themselves, not just passive self-protection. Dev formally asks to join the active investigation and is accepted.
- Ch.25: Yusuf authorizes a full, on-the-record formal inquiry (letter, drought file, marriage record, stakeout log, Priya's missing hour, Whitlock/Odette findings, all compiled properly), using emergency chair authority rather than waiting for a council vote. Group leaves his office resolved to move toward direct confrontation soon.
- Ch.26: Wren's dangerous full read on the pocket watch finally shows Denise's actual face, decades younger, mid-ritual — the connection actively resists/fights back for the first time, a new and more dangerous kind of cost.
- Ch.27: Aftermath — Caleb's fear and anger at how close Wren came; the group realizes Denise's magic can react to being watched, not just leave passive residue.
- Ch.28: Full confrontation formally planned. All six going (Mara, Caleb, Wren, Priya, Yusuf, Dev). Goal explicitly set as attempting the "split the weight" redirection, not merely exposing her. Three-day timeline set.
- Ch.29: Direct confrontation at Denise's homestead. She confirms everything immediately, no denial, including that she personally took Caleb's two years eight years ago using his existing grief as cover. Invites them inside.
- Ch.30: Inside the kitchen, Denise explains the bargain's real mechanics (continuous pull, collects early from her when delayed) and reveals she's now critically overdue, having held off specifically because of the group's scrutiny — an unmanaged collapse is imminent and would be worse than Kell's.
- Ch.31: Mara pushes for mechanical understanding over folklore. Denise reveals taken memories are transferred into her, not destroyed, including Caleb's — the redirection plan is redistribution of what already exists inside Denise, not undoing an erasure. Group commits to attempting the "split the weight" method.
- Ch.32: Planning chapter. First rule: informed, freely-given consent only, no one talked into carrying a piece. Ritual mechanics sketched from Denise's own established practice (well, dusk, a physical token, the loss spoken aloud) but largely unproven; Mara flags a real risk the well could still concentrate the load onto one person instead of dividing it, unsolved by chapter's end. Denise's reserve confirmed at two days, forcing a hard deadline.
- Ch.33: Caleb's dedicated emotional reckoning at home with Mara, processing that Denise personally and specifically carries his stolen years. Chooses to take part in the redirection attempt, explicitly not from forgiveness but from wanting to see it through at Mara's side.

## Standing rules for this book (same as Book 1 unless noted)

- Target 2,000–2,500 words per chapter, 2,100 acceptable floor (some chapters have landed slightly under this when the scene reached a genuinely natural stopping point — confirmed fine by Zia; don't pad to hit the number if it would dilute pacing). ~41 chapters total as currently renumbered (see beat_map.md). Per Zia: the priority above word count is pace and page-turn tension - every chapter should leave the reader wanting to know what happens next, not feel stretched to hit a number. Never pad a scene just to close in on 2,500.
- **No em dashes anywhere in chapter prose.**
- Match established voice exactly (Mara: dry, controlled, engineering-metaphor; Caleb: steady, plainspoken, dry humor sideways; Wren: blunt, a little raw, faster-talking than the adults around her).
- **Avoid the word "particular" entirely if possible.** This is the single most recurrent AI-tell word in this book. Treat the find-and-replace pass as mandatory on every chapter without exception before pushing. Same applies to "the specific ___" and "the kind of ___" constructions, lower frequency risk but same family.
- No other AI-authorship tells (uniform 40-85 word clause-stacked chapter openings, rule-of-three lists, stock phrases) — apply this discipline as each chapter is written, not as a separate later pass.
- New chapters must not contradict Book 1 or any earlier Book 2 chapter — check `architecture.md` and adjacent chapters first.
- All filenames lowercase (`chapter_NN.md`).
- Zia is a non-technical, voice-dictation user (expect transcription garbles — "Waza boys"=Wazzaboyzz, "Vaza"/"Voxel"/"Watson"=Voxel). Give exact file paths and full GitHub URLs in copy blocks for anything Zia needs to see/paste himself.
- Push each finished chapter directly via the API, verify by reading it back, before moving to the next.
- CORRECTION 2026-09-14: repo owner is `aliwaziri10` (the connected account's own repo), NOT `Wazzaboyzz`. `Wazzaboyzz/Voxel` is a real but unrelated public repo that caused a false "this book doesn't exist" conclusion in one session. Always verify against `aliwaziri10/Voxel` first.
- Per Zia 2026-09-14: once a session is actively drafting, keep writing multiple chapters back to back without pausing to check in after each single chapter. Each chapter still gets individually pushed the moment it's finished ("one chapter, one push"), but batches of updating beat_map.md/HANDOFF.md can happen every few chapters.
