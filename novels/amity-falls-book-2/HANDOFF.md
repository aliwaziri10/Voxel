# WHAT THE VALLEY STILL OWES — HANDOFF NOTES

**Series:** The Amity Falls Series, Book 2
**Working title:** "What the Valley Still Owes" — TENTATIVE, not yet confirmed by Zia. Alternatives to consider if this doesn't land: "The Names It Forgets", "Every Ending But the Last."
**Target length:** 100,000+ words, now approximately 43 chapters (down from 45 — see beat_map.md's renumbering note, ch.14 and ch.19 each absorbed an extra beat). Confirmed by Zia 2026-09-13; renumbering logged 2026-09-14.

## What this project is

Direct sequel to "Where the Frost Doesn't Reach" (Book 1, `novels/where-the-frost-doesnt-reach/`, published). Picks up from Book 1's final-page hook: at the wedding, Wren gives Mara a century-old letter from a woman named **Adelaide**, who made the *original* bargain — trading a town's safety for a person's memory — believing it would end with her own life. It didn't. The letter says the bargain has been "renewed again" since, by someone else, more than once. The same night, Wren cross-checks the county's 1980s drought-crisis file (the one Mara's grandfather investigated and never solved) and finds a name in it that shouldn't still belong to a living person, not if they aged the way ordinary people do.

**Book 2's engine:** someone in present-day Amity Falls has been renewing Adelaide's original bargain for generations, is still doing it, and is hiding in plain sight, aged unnaturally like Kell was — Mara, Caleb, and Wren have to find out who, stop the next renewal before it costs someone close to them, and finally close the loop Adelaide couldn't.

## Established canon this book must not contradict

- Kell "renewed" a version of the bargain himself and was exposed/collapsed publicly at the harvest festival (Book 1, ch.43) — Book 2's antagonist is a DIFFERENT, still-hidden person who either taught Kell how, or renewed the original bargain independently of him. Not the same person, not a resurrection of Kell.
- Wren is 20 now, formally enrolled in the valley's Finder apprenticeship, her "gift" (reading objects/psychometry) steadier but not fully controlled.
- Mara and Caleb are newly married; Caleb has never recovered his missing two years of memory in full, only pieces. Not yet resolved, deliberately left open until the climax chapters per the beat map.
- Thomas (Mara's father figure/informant in Book 1) died in November, before the wedding — not available as a source in Book 2.
- Priya's brother Dev was convicted of a coerced violation and is serving a supervised community-service term, not exile — he's a valley resident again in Book 2, on those terms. Ch.24 onward: Dev formally joins the active investigation team, with Priya's reluctant blessing.
- Yusuf is now permanent council chair. Ch.25: Yusuf authorizes a full formal inquiry using emergency chair authority, without a full council vote, citing precedent from the night Kell collapsed.
- The bargain's mechanic (established in Book 1): trading the valley's safety/prosperity for erasing a specific person's memory of something, at a cost of the trader's own unnaturally extended life, which eventually manifests as sudden aging/collapse (Kell's fate). Ch.3 adds: Denise's version of the bargain is surgical - one small, specific memory taken per cycle, not a person's whole memory/life torn apart the way Kell attacked Caleb. This is WHY she's gone undetected for decades.
- Mara's grandmother's maiden line is Voss (established Book 1). Ch.21: Odette's information puts Denise's real birth year at 1938 (not the 1951 Denise has told people), making her closer to 90 than the 75 originally estimated.
- Ch.20: Adelaide's letter reread for mechanics — the bargain "compounds" and cannot simply be refused once underway; it must be "completed, or redirected onto something willing to carry the weight in a different shape." **Ambrose Whitlock** named as the man who taught Adelaide the bargain, possibly not its originator.
- Ch.21: Odette confirms Whitlock per old family stories (predates the town itself, taught one person per generation). Surfaces an uncorroborated rumor: a past keeper once "split the weight" across several willing people instead of passing it whole to a successor — first real hope of a non-death, non-collapse ending.
- Ch.22: Denise confronted directly by Mara over the birth-year discrepancy. Doesn't deny it outright, delivers a warning dressed as kindly advice, mask slips briefly. Turn of the book — Denise now knows she's under active investigation.
- Ch.23: Priya sets a new rule (nobody goes anywhere alone) and commits to briefing Yusuf. Wren raises fear of preemptive retaliation.
- Ch.24: Denise retaliates precisely — Priya loses exactly one hour of memory overnight, specifically the hour in which the "nobody goes alone" rule was set and the group's next move was decided. Proves Denise is capable of targeted, deliberate action against the investigators themselves, not just passive self-protection. Dev formally asks to join the active investigation and is accepted.
- Ch.25: Yusuf authorizes a full, on-the-record formal inquiry (letter, drought file, marriage record, stakeout log, Priya's missing hour, Whitlock/Odette findings, all compiled properly), using emergency chair authority rather than waiting for a council vote. Group leaves his office resolved to move toward direct confrontation soon.

## Standing rules for this book (same as Book 1 unless noted)

- Target 2,000–2,500 words per chapter, 2,100 acceptable floor (some chapters have landed slightly under this when the scene reached a genuinely natural stopping point — confirmed fine by Zia; don't pad to hit the number if it would dilute pacing). 43 chapters total as currently renumbered (see beat_map.md). Per Zia: the priority above word count is pace and page-turn tension - every chapter should leave the reader wanting to know what happens next, not feel stretched to hit a number. Never pad a scene just to close in on 2,500.
- **No em dashes anywhere in chapter prose.**
- Match established voice exactly (Mara: dry, controlled, engineering-metaphor; Caleb: steady, plainspoken, dry humor sideways; Wren: blunt, a little raw, faster-talking than the adults around her).
- **Avoid the word "particular" entirely if possible.** This is the single most recurrent AI-tell word in this book. Treat the find-and-replace pass as mandatory on every chapter without exception before pushing. Same applies to "the specific ___" and "the kind of ___" constructions, lower frequency risk but same family.
- No other AI-authorship tells (uniform 40-85 word clause-stacked chapter openings, rule-of-three lists, stock phrases) — apply this discipline as each chapter is written, not as a separate later pass.
- New chapters must not contradict Book 1 or any earlier Book 2 chapter — check `architecture.md` and adjacent chapters first.
- All filenames lowercase (`chapter_NN.md`).
- Zia is a non-technical, voice-dictation user (expect transcription garbles — "Waza boys"=Wazzaboyzz, "Vaza"/"Voxel"/"Watson"=Voxel). Give exact file paths and full GitHub URLs in copy blocks for anything Zia needs to see/paste himself.
- Push each finished chapter directly via the API, verify by reading it back, before moving to the next.
