# WHAT THE VALLEY STILL OWES — HANDOFF NOTES

**Series:** The Amity Falls Series, Book 2
**Working title:** "What the Valley Still Owes" — TENTATIVE, not yet confirmed by Zia. Alternatives to consider if this doesn't land: "The Names It Forgets", "Every Ending But the Last."
**Target length:** 100,000+ words, 45 chapters (matches Book 1's exact structure at 2,000-2,500 words/chapter). Confirmed by Zia 2026-09-13.

## What this project is

Direct sequel to "Where the Frost Doesn't Reach" (Book 1, `novels/where-the-frost-doesnt-reach/`, published). Picks up from Book 1's final-page hook: at the wedding, Wren gives Mara a century-old letter from a woman named **Adelaide**, who made the *original* bargain — trading a town's safety for a person's memory — believing it would end with her own life. It didn't. The letter says the bargain has been "renewed again" since, by someone else, more than once. The same night, Wren cross-checks the county's 1980s drought-crisis file (the one Mara's grandfather investigated and never solved) and finds a name in it that shouldn't still belong to a living person, not if they aged the way ordinary people do.

**Book 2's engine:** someone in present-day Amity Falls has been renewing Adelaide's original bargain for generations, is still doing it, and is hiding in plain sight, aged unnaturally like Kell was — Mara, Caleb, and Wren have to find out who, stop the next renewal before it costs someone close to them, and finally close the loop Adelaide couldn't.

## Established canon this book must not contradict

- Kell "renewed" a version of the bargain himself and was exposed/collapsed publicly at the harvest festival (Book 1, ch.43) — Book 2's antagonist is a DIFFERENT, still-hidden person who either taught Kell how, or renewed the original bargain independently of him. Not the same person, not a resurrection of Kell.
- Wren is 20 now, formally enrolled in the valley's Finder apprenticeship, her "gift" (reading objects/psychometry) steadier but not fully controlled.
- Mara and Caleb are newly married; Caleb has never recovered his missing two years of memory in full, only pieces. Ch.3-5 develop an open thread (his, unconfirmed): his missing years may have been someone else's "payment" via the same mechanism Denise uses, not Kell's own doing directly. Ch.5 is a dedicated emotional scene on this - not yet resolved, deliberately left open until ch.41/44 per the expanded beat map.
- Thomas (Mara's father figure/informant in Book 1) died in November, before the wedding — not available as a source in Book 2.
- Priya's brother Dev was convicted of a coerced violation and is serving a supervised community-service term, not exile — he's a valley resident again in Book 2, on those terms.
- Yusuf is now permanent council chair.
- The bargain's mechanic (established in Book 1): trading the valley's safety/prosperity for erasing a specific person's memory of something, at a cost of the trader's own unnaturally extended life, which eventually manifests as sudden aging/collapse (Kell's fate). Ch.3 adds: Denise's version of the bargain is surgical - one small, specific memory taken per cycle, not a person's whole memory/life torn apart the way Kell attacked Caleb. This is WHY she's gone undetected for decades.
- Mara's grandmother's maiden line is Voss (established Book 1). Book 2 ch.1 establishes Denise Voss as a name in the 1980s drought file, age recorded as 31, meaning present-day Denise (a beloved, ordinary-seeming valley fixture) would be 75+ despite appearing about 20 years younger.
- Ch.3 canon: Ruth Calloway, a widow, lost the specific memory of her late husband's voice in a single night with no other effects - the book's first on-page example of an active "renewal," proving the bargain isn't just historical.
- Ch.4 canon: Wren finds a full decades-spanning pattern in valley records (1974, 1981, 1989, 1996, 2003, 2009, 2016 - roughly every 6-8 years), every single incident on or beside Voss family land. Mara and Wren now have strong circumstantial evidence pointing at Denise, not yet told to Caleb (that's next).
- Ch.5 canon: Caleb told everything Wren found; he has a hard night processing what it might mean for his own missing years. No new plot facts, but locks in his emotional stance going into the rest of the book - wants answers, is choosing to face this rather than avoid it, and both he and Mara now explicitly acknowledge that catching Denise may not cleanly "end" anything, the way Adelaide's original bargain didn't end anything either.
- Ch.6 canon: First on-page scene with Denise. She feeds Mara and Wren pie, tells a warm story about Caleb as a boy with one half-beat hesitation right before a specific phrase ("before you ever looked twice at him"), as if catching herself. A thin circular scar is visible on her wrist when her sleeve slips. Wren almost reaches to Finder-read an old wooden spoon near Denise's hand; Denise moves the spoon out of reach a half second before Wren consciously decides to act, suggesting she's sensed something like this before. Nothing said or done by Denise is objectively strange; all of it plays as an old woman being warm and careful with her words.
- Ch.7 canon: Mara and Wren argue both sides of what they saw in ch.6, agreeing none of it is proof on its own (a hesitation, a scar, a moved spoon all have innocent explanations). They decide to treat it as an unconfirmed data point, not a conclusion. Caleb affirms he trusts Wren's read but pushes for outside verification. All three agree to bring Priya in, with Wren leading the telling herself.
- Ch.8 canon: Wren tells Priya everything (letter, drought file, decade pattern, ch.6 events) in Priya's substation office. Priya treats it professionally: nothing alone is enough for a case, but the four of them agree to start a dated, sourced incident log rather than an informal theory file. Priya identifies that the renewal pattern is "due" again soon based on the 7-8 year interval, and that a new incident caught in real time (not reconstructed from old records) is what would actually move this from suspicion to case. On the way out, Dev (serving his community-service term at the substation) mentions in passing that he's heard old-timer "folk stories" about "the pie lady never aging" — Wren and Mara both clock this as significant but don't raise it in front of Priya/Caleb yet. Sets up ch.9's Dev/Priya scene.
- Ch.9 canon: Priya confronts Dev privately in the supply closet. Dev reveals the source: Walt Pruitt, an old-timer he assists as part of his community-service hours, has joked more than once that Denise hasn't aged, and once let slip his own grandmother's warning never to ask Denise for a favor without expecting to "pay it back double." Dev agrees to take Priya to Walt that afternoon, informally, as siblings rather than deputy-and-source. The scene doubles as a trust-repair beat between Dev and Priya: he asks her to let this be the thing where he helped rather than another test of whether she still doubts him, and she tells him plainly, for the first time, that she no longer sees him as the person who committed his offense. Sets up a Walt Pruitt visit next.
- Ch.10 canon: Mara, Caleb, Wren, and Priya lay the full case in front of Yusuf. He reveals personal history not previously stated: Denise sat with his dying mother nightly for the last six weeks of her life so he wouldn't have to be alone with it. He refuses to commit to a formal inquiry on the spot, torn between what he owes her and what he owes the valley, but agrees to keep the papers and reread them privately that night. Ends unresolved, with Yusuf alone in his office, Denise's phone number visible taped inside a drawer.
- Ch.11 canon: Three days of silence from Yusuf. Wren, increasingly impatient, pushes to proceed without full council backing (informal surveillance, more digging with Odette, a possible stakeout) with only Priya's oversight. The group confronts Yusuf again at the grange hall; he reveals he privately visited Denise for coffee cake and found nothing suspicious, which shakes his confidence further rather than settling it. He grants partial, informal backing (quiet investigation with his and Priya's knowledge, short of a formal inquiry that would notify Denise) but withholds full council authorization. Ends on Denise alone at her table, aware in some unstated way that she is being watched, turning a wooden spoon in her hands.
- Ch.12 canon: Wren borrows Ruth Calloway's late husband's pocket watch and, against her own safety judgment, performs a full unsupervised Finder read at Mara's kitchen table. Gets a genuine flash: a woman's hands, not old but not young, working something small and fast between her fingers, no face (the read seems actively blocked from revealing it). Real physical cost shown for the first time: nosebleed, trembling, near-collapse. Caleb arrives partway through and helps her recover; both he and Mara are shaken and clear that this cannot become routine. First concrete supernatural corroboration of the pattern, though still not enough to name Denise conclusively.
- Ch.13 canon: Aftermath of ch.12's scare. Late-night kitchen scene between Mara and Caleb, processing the fear of watching Wren nearly break herself, and a marriage-deepening conversation about loving someone enough to let them make their own dangerous choices. No new plot facts; a checkpoint emotional beat before the investigation turns procedural again.
- Ch.14 canon: Mara builds a full structural map of all seven known incidents against county survey records. The pattern resolves to two specific structures on Voss land, not the acreage generally: the old well (4 of 7 incidents) and the root cellar (2 of 7), with the collapsed barn foundation as the one outlier. Wren and Caleb agree the location is concrete enough to plan around. Group agrees to bring the finding to Priya that night.
- Ch.15 canon: Priya is briefed on the well/cellar map. She raises the real legal and ethical stakes of surveilling Denise: public right-of-way along the county road is lawful to watch from, but going onto Voss land itself is not, and any misstep could taint the case or end her own career. She agrees to a cautious plan (rotating shifts, road-only, log everything, do not approach or confront if something happens) but defers the actual shift schedule and roles to the next session, promising to bring it "tomorrow." Ends on Priya driving off toward the property in the dark, tension unresolved.
- Ch.16 canon: Priya finalizes the stakeout plan: three daily shifts (dawn, midday, dusk), always two people per shift, one watching one logging, roles swapped mid-shift. Establishes three hard rules: watch only from public right-of-way, nobody goes anywhere alone during a shift, and any observed act must be logged in plain factual language with no confrontation or approach. Establishes the escalation trigger explicitly: only a second person present who appears to be in active distress during a ritual-looking act converts documentation into an emergency call. Priya takes the highest-exposure shifts herself. First dusk shift (Mara and Caleb) scheduled for Thursday.
- Ch.17 canon: First stakeout. Mara and Caleb watch Denise walk alone to the well at dusk, remove a small object from her apron pocket, and perform a fast, practiced hand motion with her eyes closed, lips moving inaudibly, matching Wren's ch.12 Finder-read flash almost exactly. No second person present, so the escalation trigger is not met; they log it factually and call Priya, who confirms this is the first real-time corroboration in the whole file, distinct from decades-old records or a psychic impression. Open question raised: whether the bargain requires the victim's physical presence at all, which unsettles Caleb given his own missing years were never tied to any specific place he can identify.
- Ch.18 canon: Wren, working alone at night over several days, finds a marriage record proving Denise Voss married Harold Pruitt the same year the drought committee disbanded, directly tying Denise's Voss maiden name to the Pruitt family, i.e. to Walt Pruitt, the source of Dev's ch.9 folk-story lead. Wren independently verifies the original certificate at the county clerk's office alone before telling anyone, needing to be certain rather than handing the others a maybe. Reveals the finding to Mara and Caleb that evening. No new supernatural event; this is the paper-trail confirmation that Denise's identity and the folk-story thread are the same woman, closing the loop between ch.4's pattern, ch.9's folk story, and the drought file itself.
- Ch.19 canon: Wren shares the ch.18 confirmation with Mara and Caleb in full; the reaction is grief and dread rather than triumph, all three explicitly naming how strange it is to hold both "trusted lifelong presence" and "the person doing this" as true about the same woman at once. Group debates confronting Denise immediately versus waiting; Caleb argues successfully for waiting, reasoning that a premature accusation she can deny burns their only element of surprise. They agree the next concrete step is rereading Adelaide's original letter specifically for the mechanics of how the bargain was struck (not just that it happened), looking for a way to prove someone is mid-cycle rather than only proving the pattern existed historically. Priya is briefed same night, approves the plan, and reveals a small personal detail: she once had to build a case against her own brother Dev, adding texture to her empathy for Wren's mixed grief/certainty in this chapter. Ends on Mara, Caleb, and Wren staying together overnight rather than separating, letter unopened until morning.

## Standing rules for this book (same as Book 1 unless noted)

- Target 2,000–2,500 words per chapter, **2,100 acceptable floor** (raised from 1,800 per Zia 2026-09-14). 45 chapters total (see target length above) - do not shorten the outline to finish faster; the word-count target depends on hitting all 45. Per Zia 2026-09-14: hit the natural end of the scene, not a word-count quota — a chapter that lands cleanly at 2,100-2,200 should not be padded just to reach 2,500 if padding would dilute the pacing or plot. The floor still matters (don't leave a chapter thin and undercooked), but length is a byproduct of telling the beat right, not the goal itself. Per Zia 2026-09-14 (restated): the priority above word count is pace and page-turn tension - every chapter should leave the reader wanting to know what happens next, not feel stretched to hit a number. Never pad a scene just to close in on 2,500.
- **No em dashes anywhere in chapter prose.**
- Match established voice exactly (Mara: dry, controlled, engineering-metaphor; Caleb: steady, plainspoken, dry humor sideways; Wren: blunt, a little raw, faster-talking than the adults around her).
- **Avoid the word "particular" entirely if possible.** This is the single most recurrent AI-tell word from Book 1 and needed same-session self-correction in ch.3 (2 instances), ch.4 (3 instances), ch.6 (2 instances), ch.7 (2 instances), ch.8 (2 instances), ch.9 (1 instance), ch.11 (4 instances), ch.12 (1 instance), ch.14 (3 instances), ch.15 (1 instance), ch.17 (1 instance), and ch.18 (1 instance) of this book. Ch.5, ch.10, ch.13, ch.16, and ch.19 had zero instances of "particular" (ch.16 had one "the kind of" instance, self-corrected; ch.19 had one accidental reintroduction during editing, self-corrected before push). The word keeps recurring in first drafts of most chapters and does NOT reliably decrease with practice — treat the find-and-replace pass as mandatory on every chapter without exception before pushing, never skip it on the assumption a clean recent streak means the habit is broken. Same applies to "the specific ___" and "the kind of ___" constructions, lower frequency risk but same family. Ch.18 also had one "the kind of" instance, self-corrected same session. Ch.19 also had one "the kind of" instance during drafting, self-corrected same session.
- No other AI-authorship tells (uniform 40-85 word clause-stacked chapter openings, rule-of-three lists, stock phrases) — apply this discipline as each chapter is written, not as a separate later pass.
- New chapters must not contradict Book 1 or any earlier Book 2 chapter — check `architecture.md` and adjacent chapters first.
- All filenames lowercase (`chapter_NN.md`).
- Zia is a non-technical, voice-dictation user (expect transcription garbles — "Waza boys"=Wazzaboyzz, "Vaza"/"Voxel"/"Watson"=Voxel). Give exact file paths and full GitHub URLs in copy blocks for anything Zia needs to see/paste himself.
