# WHAT THE VALLEY STILL OWES — HANDOFF NOTES

**Series:** The Amity Falls Series, Book 2
**Working title:** "What the Valley Still Owes" — TENTATIVE, not yet confirmed by Zia. Alternatives to consider if this doesn't land: "The Names It Forgets", "Every Ending But the Last."
**Target length:** 100,000+ words, now approximately 43 chapters (down from 45 — see beat_map.md's renumbering note, ch.14 and ch.19 each absorbed an extra beat). Confirmed by Zia 2026-09-13; renumbering logged 2026-09-14.

## What this project is

Direct sequel to "Where the Frost Doesn't Reach" (Book 1, `novels/where-the-frost-doesnt-reach/`, published). Picks up from Book 1's final-page hook: at the wedding, Wren gives Mara a century-old letter from a woman named **Adelaide**, who made the *original* bargain — trading a town's safety for a person's memory — believing it would end with her own life. It didn't. The letter says the bargain has been "renewed again" since, by someone else, more than once. The same night, Wren cross-checks the county's 1980s drought-crisis file (the one Mara's grandfather investigated and never solved) and finds a name in it that shouldn't still belong to a living person, not if they aged the way ordinary people do.

**Book 2's engine:** someone in present-day Amity Falls has been renewing Adelaide's original bargain for generations, is still doing it, and is hiding in plain sight, aged unnaturally like Kell was — Mara, Caleb, and Wren have to find out who, stop the next renewal before it costs someone close to them, and finally close the loop Adelaide couldn't.

## Established canon this book must not contradict

- Kell "renewed" a version of the bargain himself and was exposed/collapsed publicly at the harvest festival (Book 1, ch.43) — Book 2's antagonist is a DIFFERENT, still-hidden person who either taught Kell how, or renewed the original bargain independently of him. Not the same person, not a resurrection of Kell.
- Wren is 20 now, formally enrolled in the valley's Finder apprenticeship, her "gift" (reading objects/psychometry) steadier but not fully controlled.
- Mara and Caleb are newly married; Caleb has never recovered his missing two years of memory in full, only pieces. Ch.3-5 develop an open thread (his, unconfirmed): his missing years may have been someone else's "payment" via the same mechanism Denise uses, not Kell's own doing directly. Not yet resolved, deliberately left open until the climax chapters per the beat map.
- Thomas (Mara's father figure/informant in Book 1) died in November, before the wedding — not available as a source in Book 2.
- Priya's brother Dev was convicted of a coerced violation and is serving a supervised community-service term, not exile — he's a valley resident again in Book 2, on those terms.
- Yusuf is now permanent council chair.
- The bargain's mechanic (established in Book 1): trading the valley's safety/prosperity for erasing a specific person's memory of something, at a cost of the trader's own unnaturally extended life, which eventually manifests as sudden aging/collapse (Kell's fate). Ch.3 adds: Denise's version of the bargain is surgical - one small, specific memory taken per cycle, not a person's whole memory/life torn apart the way Kell attacked Caleb. This is WHY she's gone undetected for decades.
- Mara's grandmother's maiden line is Voss (established Book 1). Book 2 ch.1 establishes Denise Voss as a name in the 1980s drought file, age recorded as 31. Ch.21 revises this further: Odette's information puts Denise's real birth year at 1938 (not the 1951 Denise has told people), making her closer to 90 than the 75 originally estimated.
- Ch.3 canon: Ruth Calloway, a widow, lost the specific memory of her late husband's voice in a single night with no other effects - the book's first on-page example of an active "renewal," proving the bargain isn't just historical.

- Ch.6 canon: First on-page scene with Denise. Warm, feeds them pie, one half-beat hesitation before a Caleb-as-a-boy story, a thin circular scar on her wrist, moves a wooden spoon out of Wren's reach a half-second before Wren consciously decides to act.
- Ch.7 canon: Mara and Wren agree none of ch.6 is proof on its own; decide to bring in Priya, Wren leading.

- Ch.10 canon: Full case laid before Yusuf. His personal history: Denise sat with his dying mother nightly for six weeks. He stalls, takes the papers to reread privately.

- Ch.18 canon: Wren independently confirms Denise Voss's marriage to Harold Pruitt at the county clerk's office, tying her maiden name to the Pruitt family and Dev's ch.9 folk-story lead.
- Ch.19 canon: Wren tells Mara and Caleb the full confirmation; grief, not triumph. Caleb argues successfully against confronting Denise prematurely. Group decides to reread Adelaide's letter for mechanics next. Priya briefed, shares a personal parallel (building a case against her own brother). Group stays together overnight.
- Ch.20 canon: The three reread Adelaide's letter specifically for mechanics. Key new lines: the bargain "compounds" and cannot simply be refused once underway (Adelaide nearly died trying twice) — it must be "completed, or redirected onto something willing to carry the weight in a different shape." A previously-unknown name surfaces: **Ambrose Whitlock**, who taught Adelaide the bargain, himself possibly not its originator. Group decides the next step is consulting Odette, since Adelaide never learned how redirection actually works.
- Ch.21 canon: Odette confirms Whitlock per her grandmother's old stories (a man who came through the valley before it was properly a town, taught one person per generation how to make the bargain). Odette also surfaces a new, uncorroborated rumor: a past keeper once "split the weight" of the bargain across several willing people instead of passing it whole to one successor — the first real hope of an ending that isn't death, collapse, or another lonely keeper. Odette warns Mara to be careful, noting that people who've carried a burden longest often fight hardest to keep carrying it. Wren is told everything on their return and reacts with cautious hope rather than the earlier grief.
- Ch.22 canon: Denise invites Mara for coffee. Mara confronts her directly with the birth-year discrepancy; Denise doesn't deny it outright but delivers a warning dressed as kindly advice ("be careful how deep you go digging... some things get uncovered because somebody couldn't leave well enough alone, and ends up wishing very badly that they had"), her mask slipping briefly to reveal something old, exhausted, and dangerous underneath. Turn of the book — Denise now knows she's under active investigation. Group agrees that night to move faster and to let Denise believe her warning worked.
- Ch.23 canon: Priya pushes the group to genuinely hold both "guilty" and "innocent-but-frightened" readings of Denise's warning, using the Kell precedent as a caution against reckless certainty, but ultimately states she personally believes the guilty reading is more likely. New plot beat: Wren raises the fear that Denise might act preemptively against one of them rather than simply wait to be exposed. Priya sets a new rule (nobody goes anywhere alone from here on) and commits to briefing Yusuf directly the next day. Ends with Mara alone at the table that night, uneasy, unable to shake the sense that whatever comes next won't wait for them to be ready.

## Standing rules for this book (same as Book 1 unless noted)

- Target 2,000–2,500 words per chapter, 2,100 acceptable floor (some recent chapters have landed slightly under this, e.g. 1,912-2,072 words, when the scene reached a genuinely natural stopping point — Zia has confirmed this is fine; don't pad to hit the number if it would dilute pacing). 43 chapters total as currently renumbered (see beat_map.md). Per Zia: the priority above word count is pace and page-turn tension - every chapter should leave the reader wanting to know what happens next, not feel stretched to hit a number. Never pad a scene just to close in on 2,500.
- **No em dashes anywhere in chapter prose.**
- Match established voice exactly (Mara: dry, controlled, engineering-metaphor; Caleb: steady, plainspoken, dry humor sideways; Wren: blunt, a little raw, faster-talking than the adults around her).
- **Avoid the word "particular" entirely if possible.** This is the single most recurrent AI-tell word in this book, and has needed same-session self-correction in nearly every chapter from ch.3 onward (2-4 instances typical), including chapters as recent as ch.20, ch.21, and ch.22 (2 instances each). It does NOT reliably decrease with practice — treat the find-and-replace pass as mandatory on every chapter without exception before pushing. Same applies to "the specific ___" and "the kind of ___" constructions, lower frequency risk but same family — also self-corrected in recent chapters (ch.20 "the specifics", ch.22 "the specifics").
- No other AI-authorship tells (uniform 40-85 word clause-stacked chapter openings, rule-of-three lists, stock phrases) — apply this discipline as each chapter is written, not as a separate later pass.
- New chapters must not contradict Book 1 or any earlier Book 2 chapter — check `architecture.md` and adjacent chapters first.
- All filenames lowercase (`chapter_NN.md`).
- Zia is a non-technical, voice-dictation user (expect transcription garbles — "Waza boys"=Wazzaboyzz, "Vaza"/"Voxel"/"Watson"=Voxel). Give exact file paths and full GitHub URLs in copy blocks for anything Zia needs to see/paste himself.
- Push each finished chapter directly via the API, verify by reading it back, before moving to the next.
- CORRECTION 2026-09-14: repo owner is `aliwaziri10` (the connected account's own repo), NOT `Wazzaboyzz`. `Wazzaboyzz/Voxel` is a real but unrelated public repo that caused a false "this book doesn't exist" conclusion in one session. Always verify against `aliwaziri10/Voxel` first.
- Per Zia 2026-09-14: once a session is actively drafting, keep writing multiple chapters back to back (drafting, self-correcting, pushing) without pausing to check in after each single chapter. Zia has specified he wants each chapter individually pushed the moment it's finished ("one chapter, one push") but batches of updating beat_map.md/HANDOFF.md can happen every few chapters rather than after every single one (confirmed 2026-09-14, "write three chapters and then update").
- MULTIPLE SESSIONS MAY BE WRITING CONCURRENTLY. Before starting a new chapter, always re-fetch the actual chapters/ directory listing and re-read HANDOFF.md fresh — do not trust a chapter count from earlier in your own conversation, since another session may have pushed additional chapters in the meantime. This happened on 2026-09-14: one session drafted a chapter 9 locally while another session had already written and pushed real chapters 9 through 19; the stale draft was discarded before pushing.
